1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The Master's degree programme in human security is a multidisciplinary programme focusing on the interaction between political, social and environmental factors in global and local conflicts. The aim of the programme is to provide students with insight into relevant themes, academic perspectives and issues within human security, including training their skills in applying theory to the analysis of specific empirical cases.

1.2 Learning outcomes

The Master's degree programme in Human Security provides students with the following knowledge, skills and competences:

Knowledge:

  • insight into how political, global, environmental, agricultural, food-related and social issues can be analysed from the perspective of interdisciplinary human security
  • knowledge of comparative and interdisciplinary approaches to human security, including climate change and understanding and management of conflicts
  • theoretical and practical knowledge of qualitative and to some degree quantitative methods of study with regard to producing, analysing and discussing data in relation to relevant literature in the field
  • understand and reflect on concepts, theories and debates in relation to human security
  • identify scientific problems in relation to human security, and formulate an academically sound, ethically viable, and logistically feasible study within the field of human security.

Skills:

  • conduct critical assessment of texts involving theory and detailed empirical studies within environmental and agricultural analyses, anthropology and social science
  • conduct a considered and critical analytical assessment of human and environmental security
  • apply approaches from both the natural and social sciences in understanding human security with the ability to combine these in a relevant context
  • establish an academic argument or thesis at a scholarly level and critically consider the inherent methodological and theoretical requirements
  • apply mainly qualitative and to some degree quantitative methods in the completion of specific studies within human security
  • assess the viability of and make a choice between theories, methods and tools in relation to human security, both by means of individual study and group work, including peer feedback processes
  • analyse and solve human security related problems by means of relevant tools and skills, including the Logical Framework Approach, and skills in conflict analysis and management
  • written and oral communication of human security projects of high empirical and theoretical complexity.

Competences:

  • the ability to produce new knowledge on complex human security-related issues in a global context
  • competence to participate in interdisciplinary international teams and projects, including establishing projects based on specific guidelines
  • an interdisciplinary and solution-oriented approach that is open to new ideas, techniques, methods and viewpoints
  • initiate and conduct a study within human security in a cross-disciplinary context
  • take responsibility for working in a group in a cross-disciplinary context
  • plan and carry out a complex research project within human security
  • take responsibility for their own academic development and specialisation.

2. Degree programme structure

2.1 Degree programme rules
In addition to the general rules (section 3) at the end of the academic regulations, the following rules apply to the programme:

2.1.1 Prerequisites for exam participation
Prerequisites are demands with which students must comply before taking an exam. If an exam includes prerequisites, this will be stated in the exam description for the specific course under the heading "Prerequisites for the examination participation". These must be met prior to taking the exam and must only be met once. Students who fail to meet this requirement will not be able to take the exam, and a examination attempt must be used.
The rules and deadlines for proving compliance with the prerequisites are stated on the study portal (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Lecture participation is an exam form and involves: Regular, active and satisfactory participation in the teaching. ?Regular? means participating in at least 75% of the teaching activities offered. At the start of the semester, the teacher will announce in writing (on Brightspace) the teaching activities in which students are expected to participate, and the requirements regarding what will be construed as active and satisfactory participation.

2.1.3 Language of teaching and exams
The language of the teaching and exams is English.

Rules regarding the syllabus can be found on the study portal.

Tropical ecosystem management human security

10 ECTS

Tropical ecosystem management and human security is a course offered by the Institute for Biology at Aarhus University.

The course provides the participants with biological knowledge of tropical ecosystems, including case studies of ecosystem management and how these ecosystems are influenced by local people and the current and future climatic changes.

The course will e.g., focus on the student acquiring the following:
- knowledge on major tropical ecosystems and their ecological characteristics and how climate change will influence the livelihood of people in developing countries
- ability to identify and analyse problems in relation to natural resource management in the tropics
- ability to analyse natural resource management approaches for tropical ecosystems.

The specific purpose, content, overall academic objectives and form of examination will be stated in the course description in the course catalogue. Click on "See courses".

Core concepts contextualised 20 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:
Purpose:
The course introduces students to key concepts, theories and debates in relation to human security. Theories and themes include a focus on politics and state, war and violence, nature and environment, and health and livelihood strategies.
A series of cases (e.g. old/new wars; drought/water resources; energy, deforestation; natural catastrophes) outline relevant aspects of human and environmental security. Through the lens of human security, the course examines and discusses specific socio-political contexts and possible solutions to identified problems. The course includes a supervised process where students produce a human security report about a given case in groups of max 6 persons.
The course is an introductory course that provides students with key concepts and theories relevant to the Human Security programme as a whole.

Academic goals:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge of how the concept of security has been extended horizontally to include economic, environmental, health, personal, community, food and political threats
- demonstrate knowledge of how the concept of security has been extended vertically to include individuals.

Skills:
- present a case that includes threats to human security
- analyse the case by means of relevant literature from the course syllabus
- apply arguments to a discussion of security challenges related to one of the topics in the course syllabus.

Competences:
- discuss whether the horizontal and vertical extension of the security concept is fruitful or whether the security concept loses all its meaning and coherence.

Contents:

The commencement of studies exam is associated with this discipline. For further information about the form and content of the exam, please see http://kursuskatalog.au.dk/en

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

Classroom teaching combined with group discussions and peer-feedback assignments. The course is divided into thematic sections and key concepts and theories are examined before they are discussed in relation to relevant cases. The course includes a supervised process where students produce a human security report about a given case in groups of max 6 persons.

Exam language:

Exam options:

Comments:

Ordinary and re-exam:

Agro-ecology, Food Systems, and Food Security 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:
Purpose:
The course will enable the student to understand and address complex relations between agricultural systems, food systems, food security and human security. This entails the understanding of both natural and social science concepts and theoretical frameworks. The focus is on agroecology’s potential to contribute to the redesign of agriculture and to the transformation of food systems, as well as overall sustainability in relation to climate change, the environment, and livelihoods, without compromising global food security.

Content
The course combines natural and social science approaches to understanding the transformative potential of agroecology in the context of food systems and food security. This entails exploration of ecological, political-ecology, climate change, natural resources, and livelihood problems, at both agency and structural levels. These problems are explored in relation to agroecology’s principles and its transformational aspirations, as an adaptation and resilience-strengthening pathway representing an alternative to high external input dependent agriculture, and to current dominant food systems. In this context, attention is paid to the role of agricultural knowledge systems.

The course navigates between the empirical and theory by pairing concrete project and policy examples with theoretical and analytical frameworks and paradigms.   A written project in the shape of a research or development proposal constitutes the foundation for the exam.

Academic goals:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge of agroecological principles and methods, agricultural systems, ecological conditions, as well as relationships between the above
- demonstrate understanding of the social, institutional, governance and political conditions which influence agricultural production, food systems and food security
- demonstrate understanding of agricultural systems in relation to climate change mitigation and adaptation
- demonstrate knowledge of local and global food systems in relation to food security.

Skills:
- describe and analyse agroecology and food system relevant cases and associated cross-disciplinary problems, applying relevant analytical methods and theoretical knowledge
- describe and apply agroecological principles and approaches in relation to agricultural systems, and social and ecological contexts
- identify and discuss the social, institutional, governance and politically related conditions which influence access to natural resources and food security, as well as the shaping of agriculture and food systems
- describe and analyse food security in the light of climate change.

Competences:
- Ability to work effectively with cross-disciplinary problems that require novel approaches. In doing so, demonstrate analytical overview and provide evidence-based solutions to complex cases within the scope of the course.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:
Lecture, classroom instruction and group exercises, small student presentations, guided analyses and simulations in relevant software programs.

Research project design 10 ECTS Semester 2 Passed /failed
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
This course gives the students the knowledge to plan and implement an academic research project within the field of human security. The course will emphasise project design, including research question development, research ethics, and the selection of research methods that will enable the students to collect relevant empirical data. The course consists of two integrated processes: 1) Class teaching with exercises, leading to the development of a project description; 2) Supervision and peer-reviewed feedback, focusing on the substance/contents of individual projects.
With a focus on qualitative methods and an introduction to quantitative methods the course covers the development of a research question and project description, i.e. sampling, participant observation, interviews and other methods, research ethics and logistics, as well as analytical strategies. Students are expected to acquire knowledge of a range of research methods, including some that they may not use in their own research.
The course provides students with the necessary skills for planning and carrying out a human security research project during or alongside the project placement on the third semester.

Academic goals:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe various quantitative and qualitative methods for the collection of data relating to human security, e.g. questionnaires, observation, interviews
- describe the coherence of the different elements of the proposed project (e.g. research question, chosen methods, realistic time planning).

Skills:
- develop a project description and research question, including giving an empirical and theoretical definition of this problem
- describe and reflect on mainly qualitative and to some degree quantitative methods, which are appropriate for the identified research question.

Competences:
- independently formulate an academically sound, ethically viable, and logistically feasible human security study
- assess the methodological basis, including choice of method and validity, in academic studies in the field of human security.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:
The teaching consists of a combination of lectures, classroom teaching, and supervision. Throughout the course, students work in groups on developing their project description. Students will submit several smaller writing assignments during the course as partial preparation for the project description. Classroom teaching is punctuated by 2-3 meetings with each student’s respective supervisor, ideally placed at the beginning, middle and/or end of the course.

Exam language:

  • English

Exam options:

   Take-home assignment (Assign)

Form of co-examination: Internal co-examination

Assessment form: Passed /failed

Comments:

Ordinary exam and re-exam:
The exam consists of a portfolio containing a number of assignments. One of the assignments will be a thesis project proposal. The total length of the portfolio is: 12-15 standard pages. The number of assignments as well as their form and length will be announced on Brightspace by the teacher at the start of the semester. The portfolio may include products. Depending on their length, and subject to the teacher’s approval, these products can replace some of the standard pages in the portfolio.

It must be possible to carry out an individual assessment. So if some parts of the portfolio have been produced by a group, it must be stated clearly which parts each student is responsible for, and which parts the group as a whole is responsible for.

The complete portfolio must be submitted for assessment in WISEflow before the deadline set in the examination plan. Each student submits a portfolio.


Conflict dynamics and management 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The course provides students with fundamental theoretical and practical knowledge about conflict dynamics and management.
In the first part of the course, students learn about contemporary violent conflict dynamics and conflict analysis, and in the second part, they learn about peacebuilding and transitional justice. The course ends with a workshop on practical conflict management, including training in mediation techniques.
The course includes an exam synopsis process, where each student receives peer-feedback and feedback from one or more course teachers.

Academic goals:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge of contemporary violent conflict dynamics and/or knowledge of peacebuilding and transitional justice
- demonstrate knowledge of central ideas/concepts/theories in relation to contemporary violent conflict dynamics and/or peacebuilding and transitional justice
- reflect critically on ideas/concepts/theories presented in the syllabus in relation to a specific case.

Skills:
- present a case in a clear way
- analyse the case by means of relevant literature from the syllabus
- discuss the case by means of relevant literature from the syllabus.

Competences:
- independently formulate and carry out a study within the area of either "contemporary violent conflict dynamics" or "peace building and transitional justice".

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:
The instruction form varies between lectures, group assignments and a practical conflict resolution workshop. The course includes an exam synopsis process, where each student received peer-feedback and also feedback from one or more course instructors.

Exam language:

  • English

Exam options:

   Take-home assignment on topic of student's choice

Form of co-examination: Internal co-examination

Assessment form: 7-point grading scale

Comments:

Ordinary and re-exam:
﻿﻿The examination consists of an individual take-home assignment. The topic must be within the areas of either "contemporary violent conflict dynamics" or "peacebuilding and transitional justice". The assignment must include relevant texts from the syllabus.

Length for 1 student: 10-12 standard pages.

The take-home assignment must be handed in for assessment in WISEflow by the date specified in the exam plan.

International elective courses (30 ECTS):
The objective of the international elective courses is to expand the degree programme's academic scope at a university outside Denmark. The courses must be within the degree programme's core field of study, and must comply with requirements for progression as well as being connected to the knowledge, skills and competences acquired during the first year of the Master's degree programme. International elective courses must also include elements that are thematically or methodically relevant for the student's Master's thesis on semester 4 of the Master's degree programme. Students must be able to participate actively in an academic educational environment outside Denmark and take responsibility for their own academic development in an unfamiliar educational environment.

Courses are subject to the advance approval of the board of studies based on the description of objectives above.

Profile courses (20 ECTS):
Students choose between the profile courses offered. A given semester’s range of profile courses is submitted by the respective boards of studies for approval by the dean before being published in the Aarhus University course catalogue one year before the courses start. Detailed stipulations regarding the form, content and exam format of each individual option are stated in the individual course descriptions in the catalogue. Detailed guidelines for application, prioritisation and admission to profile courses can be found on the website https://studerende.au.dk/en/studies/subject-portals/arts/electivecourses,profilecoursesetc/profile-courses

Project placement 30 ECTS Passed /failed
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The aim of the project placement is for students to gain practical experience in conducting a specific human security study in partnership with an institution, organisation or corporation.
During the project placement, the student is expected to contribute towards specific and defined knowledge needs in the field of human security as agreed between the student and the organisation, and in dialogue with the supervisor. In addition, the student must conduct an independent study (if necessary, this study may vary from the one described in the exam paper of the course Research Project Design). Furthermore, the student must perform the tasks in the supervision webinar, which prepare the student for the writing of the Master’s thesis.
The project placement provides the student with skills in the application of relevant methods and techniques for the collection and analysis of data in relation to relevant human security issues and provides the student with an opportunity to generate a dataset that can be used in the thesis process.

Academic goals:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- gain relevant empirical knowledge of the topic, the field location and its relevant context
- gain practice-based knowledge of the strengths and weaknesses of various methodological approaches.

Skills:
- conduct a human security study in partnership with a given organisation
- identify and present the project, its theoretical basis and key analytical concepts
- apply, discuss and reflect upon methods and techniques for the collection and analysis of data in relation to the research question of the project
- present results of a scientific project to a relevant audience.

Competences:
- independently take responsibility for working in a project-oriented manner, including the ability to plan and conduct project work
- enter into the daily work of a workplace, work on tasks in a professional environment and enter into cross-disciplinary collaborations with other people with different education and qualification profiles, and to bring academic knowledge from one area into play with knowledge from other subject areas.

Forms of instruction:

  • Distance learning with seminars
  • Supervision

Comments on form of instruction:

Online and/or face-to-face supervision. The placement is focuses on specific research or development programmes and is planned and carried out in partnership with a host institution (public or private) identified in advance. The host institution must ensure that students receive necessary support during the placement. In addition to this, the student is in regular contact with the academic supervisor, who oversees that the project complies with the required academic goals described above. Before the project placement is initiated, a contract is set up between the student and the host institution, approved by the supervisor, and signed by student and host institution. During the project placement, online assignments in the supervision webinar help the student to prepare an outline of the thesis. A follow up thesis writing workshop will be held in January to prepare the student for the thesis writing process.

Exam language:

  • English

Exam options:

   Take-home assignment on topic of student's choice

Requirements for sitting the exam:

As a prerequisite for taking the exam, the student must complete a thesis synopsis (max. 5 pages) that outlines the content and structure of the planned thesis.

For information about the rules and deadlines for prerequisites, see section 2.1 of the academic regulations.

Form of co-examination: Internal co-examination

Assessment form: Passed /failed

Comments:

Ordinary and re-exam:

Length for 1 student: 10-15 standard pages.

Thesis preparation 10 ECTS Passed /failed
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The course aims to improve the students' ability to produce a thesis, which complies with the academic standards of the subject field in question.

During the course the students must produce:

  • A project plan for completing their thesis
  • A provisional thesis statement
  • A delimitation of the foundations of the thesis, as well as
  • A definition of the academic theory/theories and method(s) of the thesis

Students whose thesis involves the creation of a product must also provide details of the product they intend to produce, including an explanation of its function in relation to the issue dealt with in the thesis.

The course will require the students to work on a range of academic and meta-academic issues that will equip them to work independently on the content and form of their thesis

Academic goals:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge within the subject field of the thesis, including knowledge of important theories, concepts, methods, research traditions and data/sources which are relevant for the study carried out in the thesis.

Skills:
- delimit and justify the choice of works used in the thesis. For theses involving the creation of a product, students can also describe the product to be developed
- produce a relevant study design in relation to a well argued thesis statement, and create a substantial and realistic project plan for completing the thesis within the time available
- argue for the connection between theoretical, analytical and methodological approaches in a large-scale piece of academic work.

Competences:
- work with a focus on solutions and process in relation to a specific large-scale project.

Forms of instruction:

  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

The teaching is divided into two elements:

  1. The students will be introduced to a range of meta-academic competences that will equip them to produce a process plan and work independently on the content and form of their thesis, as well as reflecting on issues related to design, method, analysis, text production and managing the process of producing a thesis. In this element, the focus will be placed on the ability of the students to enter into constructive and learning-oriented dialogues both within and across their core subject area. This element is done by all students at the School of Culture and Society. The teaching can involve lectures, workshops and/or seminars.

  2. And one element is covered by the subject environment in question. Students work on selecting a delimited topic as the focus of their thesis. The study methods may involve discussions and exercises as well as reading selected texts, including relevant methods, written sub-assignments and/or oral contributions by the students, data collection and processing, classroom discussions and group work. The teaching can involve lectures, workshops, seminars and/or collective supervision

Exam language:

  • English

Exam options:

   Lecture participation

Form of co-examination: No co-examination

Assessment form: Passed /failed

Comments:

Ordinary exam:
Students pass the exam through active participation. Part of the participation consists of preparing 3-4 small written assignments totalling 8-12 pages, set by the teacher during the course.

Overall, the written assignments must contain reflections on the following elements:

  • A draft plan for the completion of the thesis, rendering it probable that the thesis can be completed within the prescribed period of time
  • Reflections on the nature of a good thesis statement and a relevant study design
  • Reflections on the possible foundations of the thesis
  • Reflections on and a definition of the academic theory/theories and method(s) of the thesis
  • Reflections on the position of the thesis in relation to existing studies
  • For theses involving the creation of a product, students may also include a definition of the product which is to be part of the thesis

Before the teaching starts, the teacher announces the deadlines for when the various elements must be submitted on Brightspace during the semester.

Re-exam:
The re-exam involves producing an individual portfolio.

The portfolio may consist of the following elements:

  • A draft plan for the completion of the thesis, rendering it probable that the thesis can be completed within the prescribed period of time
  • Reflections on the nature of a good thesis statement and a relevant study design (This may be supplemented by a criterion of relevance and/or: "..., which is explained in a research discussion demonstrating how the topic has been dealt with previously in the research literature, and the position of the planned thesis within the research field in question.")
  • A draft table of contents (with comments), showing clearly how the thesis will be structured
  • Reflections on the empirical foundation, literature, academic theory/theories and method(s) of the thesis For theses involving the creation of a product, thoughts on the intended product must also be included
  • Reflections on collaboration with a supervisor

The portfolio contains a final assignment of 4-5 pages in which the student reflects on his/her own learning process.

Total length of the portfolio: 10-15 standard pages.

The complete portfolio must be handed in for assessment in WISEflow by the date specified in the exam plan.

3.2 Regulations for commencement of studies exam

The compulsory commencement of studies exam will be held before the end of September on the first semester of the Master’s degree programme.

The exam generates 0 ECTS credits and is a pass/fail internal examination with no co-examiner. Two attempts to pass this exam are allowed. First-semester students will be registered for the exam automatically. The result will be posted in the student’s self-service system. Students who do not pass their first examination attempt will be registered automatically for their second attempt.

The result of the second attempt will be posted in the student’s self-service system no later than 28 September. Students who fail both their first and second attempt will be withdrawn from their degree programme with immediate effect and without further warning.


3.3 Credit and flexibility

Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document the results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements.

Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad.

When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.


3.4 Registration and withdrawal

Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.

Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.

Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students cannot withdraw from the Master’s thesis.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.
Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.

If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Deadlines are stated on the study portal. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

Students can choose between the specified forms of exam for their Master’s thesis.


3.5 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of Master's thesis and other major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to the student's command of the content knowledge. 

In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.6 Regulations for assignments

Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.8 Project-oriented course

Options for project-oriented courses are stated in the description of the individual study element. It is possible to take project placement with more than one project host if it is deemed to live up to all requirements for project placement.

3.9 Exemption

An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the board of studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the board of studies forwards the application to the appropriate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and must be submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal.

3.10 Appeals and complaints

Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order.

More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.


3.11 Examinations

Calculation of average grade point
It is possible to get the weighted average grade point calculated and formally verified. The weighted average grade point is an expression of the average grade point calculated in relation to the ECTS weighting of the grade points. The average grade point is calculated to one decimal point based on all graded exams. The average grade is not calculated in relation to the grades of fellow students, and that the calculation of the average mark is not offered until the programme's last exam has been passed.
