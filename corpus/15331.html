1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The objective of the Master's programme is to meet an increased internationally and nationally focus on children's and young people's acquisition of literacy, and thereby contribute to creating competent, knowledgeable and critical citizens in a world where literacy and media skills set the agenda for access to information, entertainment and culture.
The MA in Children’s Literature, Media and Culture offers the students an integrated, international and coherent programme of study of the history, literary and media theory for children’s literature and young adult literature as well as critical methodologies through which to analyse, evaluate and critique the corpus of the children’s and young adults' literature and media texts.

Students will participate in an international degree that focuses on the interaction between children, young adults and adults in the production and consumption of multimodal texts. The degree promotes this interaction by combining culture and creativity, and considers the challenges of integrating multiliteracy into diverse educational contexts and policies.

The students will be able to help solve problems, disseminate knowledge about and take initiative in the production, reception, dissemination and use of children's and youths' texts, cultural products and media in national and international contexts.
Students will obtain skills that enable them to navigate the field of children’s literature, media and culture based on knowledge from literature and media science as well as childhood history and pedagogical/didactic research in an international perspective.


1.2 Learning outcomes

The Master's degree programme in Children´s Literature, Media and Culture provides students with the following knowledge, skills and competences:

Knowledge:

  • Overall understanding of knowledge about the literature, media and culture of children and young people
  • In-depth knowledge of the characteristics of the digital and analogue media of children and young people, as well as analyses of such characteristics
  • Knowledge of the conditions governing the production and reception of the literature and media of children and young people in national and global markets
  • Knowledge of the conditions and characteristics of the lives of children and young people in terms of media, currently and historically
  • Knowledge of theories and methods for examining the literature, media and culture of children and young people.

Skills:

  • The ability to analyse and form, content and media for children and young people with a focus on local, national and global conditions
  • The ability to analyse literature and media of children and young people in cross-medial contexts
  • The ability to use methods and theories at an academic level
  • The ability to analyse, manage and present complex material in the field
  • The ability to work methodically, independently and critically, and to approach an issue from various points of view
  • The ability to communicate knowledge about the media of children and young people in national and international contexts and across academic areas.

Competences:

  • The ability to design, launch and carry out research-related, investigative projects and development work
  • The ability to cooperate on children’s literature, media and culture in international teams in concentrated project processes
  • The ability to use a special area of expertise in the humanities to interact with other areas in actual national and international work situation
  • The ability to independently take responsibility for their own learning and academic development.

The course covers various aspects of literary and psychological features of this type of literature. The course provides students with an overall orientation competence in relation to Early Childhood Literature as this type of literature is crucial to children's development. Cross Boundaries: Children's Literature and other Media 5 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details

Description of qualifications:

Purpose: 
The course aims to enable students to (A) understand the relations between children’s literature and other media such as children’s theatre, film, comics, and adult literature; (B) examine the ways in which children’s literature is adapted and transformed to other media; (C) distinguish between different media and their specific characteristics; (D) understand the various stages and aspects of adaptation processes, (E) identify the relation between the verbal and the visual in picture books; (F) examine the negotiation of child and adult audiences in crossover texts; (G) use these insights to analyse and interpret an exemplary case; and (H) communicate their findings in classroom discussions as well as in an academic paper.

Since its inception in the second half of the 18th century, children’s literature has always been multi-medial. Text and images have played an important role. Besides books, there were children’s magazines and theatre plays for children. In the 19th century, children’s books were adapted for theatre, and during the 20th century, they were adapted for film, television and games as well. Today, children divide their time over a great number of different media, among which are books.

This course focuses on the relation between children’s literature and other media. Attention will be paid to the process of adaptation and the various transformations that take place when a written text is adapted to another medium. We will also study the interaction between the verbal and the visual in children’s books, notably, the interplay between text and images in picture books.

Furthermore, border traffic and adaptation are key concepts in discussions on the relationship between children’s and adult literature. During the past few decades, the relation between both forms of literature has been negotiated continuously. Therefore, we closely examine the resemblances and differences between the two forms of literature.

The course ties in with the other courses taught in the programme in the first semester. It adopts a practical approach to the trends and evolutions covered by “CL: From the Printing Press to Virtual Reality” and provides theoretical insights and case studies that complement the content of “Literature and Picturebooks for the Early Years”. In addition, it lays the groundwork for the second-semester courses “CL Texts & Media” and “New Media for C&YAs”.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- Explain why children’s literature has been essentially multi-medial since its inception in the second half of the 18th century;
- Describe the specific characteristics of different media as well as their common features, and the relations between children’s literature and other media such as children’s theatre, film, comics, and adult literature;
- Explain the various stages and aspects of adaptation processes;
- Explain how in picture books text and image interact to construct a narrative and describe various viewpoints on the relations between the verbal and the visual in picture books.

Skills:
- Distinguish between different media and their specific characteristics;
- Examine the ways in which A) children’s literature is adapted and transformed to other media, and B) adult literature is adapted and transformed to children’s literature, viz. comparing adaptations to the adapted text and identifying the adaptation strategies used.

Competences:
- Relate the knowledge provided in the course – on the text-image relationship in picture books, the relationship between children’s and adult literature, and the border traffic between media – to insights gained in the related courses in the programme;
- Independently collect sources and conduct research on a topic related to the course contents, and communicate their findings in classroom discussions as well as in an academic paper.

Children's literature and childhood. The Interaction between concepts of childhood, and children's literature and media 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose: 
The purpose of the course is to enable students (a) to account for, in general terms, the historical and current perceptions of childhood and their social context, (b) to analyse examples of texts and media of children and young people based on this knowledge and (c) to participate in and initiate discussions and investigations of the relationship between childhood perceptions and the texts and media of children and young people in transnational and cross-disciplinary global contexts.
The course focuses on the concepts of childhood and youth from a historical and current perspective, and on how these are linked to the production and reception of texts and media for children and young people. The course provides a general introduction to childhood history with a focus on modern times and to theoretical approaches that can form the basis for analysing the perceptions of childhood and youth in connection with texts and media for children and young people. The students learn to delimit an issue within the course and to discuss childhood/youth perceptions as reflected in and around specific texts and media across national borders.
The course elaborates on elements of the history and theory of childhood from the module 1 courses by developing the students’ knowledge, skills and competences in this specific subject area. It also creates the preconditions for the students to include issues regarding the perceptions of childhood and youth in a well-founded manner in the module 3 courses and in the Master’s thesis.

Academic objectives: 
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- Demonstrate an understanding of changing concepts of childhood in relation to children’s literature and media
- Demonstrate an understanding of intended and actual use of children’s texts and media based on knowledge of changing concepts of childhood.

Skills:
- Apply knowledge in order to critically analyze the interaction between concepts of childhood and examples of children’s literature and media
- Present, discuss and communicate aspects of the relation between children’s texts and media and concepts of childhood.

Competences:
- Independently participate in and initiate discussions and examinations of the relationship between concepts of childhood and children’s texts and media
- Apply knowledge concerning the relationship between concepts of childhood and children’s texts and media in interdisciplinary and international contexts.

Life writing and fan fiction 5 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose: 
The purpose of the course is that students (A) become familiar with the different genres of (verbal and visual) life writing, the main motives for life writing, and important theoretical concepts in the field of life writing studies, (B) get insight in how to apply these theoretical concepts in an analysis of life writing texts, (C) can analyse and interpret such texts independently, (D) are  able to communicate the results of their analysis and interpretation, and (E) are able to discuss and evaluate the ethics of life writing.
Life writing is usually associated with genres such as diary, autobiography, and biography, but it also appears in other forms, such as short narratives about one’s life and (partly) fictionalised accounts of one’s experiences. This course provides an introduction to these different forms of life writing in the field of children’s and young adult literature, including online forms such as blogs and fan fiction. These life writing genres are related to central concepts in the field of life writing studies such as authenticity, identity formation, self-fashioning, and the ethics and politics in and of life writing. The concepts are applied to life writing texts for children and young adults.
The course builds on the primary aim of the CLMC degree programme in that it provides a theoretical grounding in life writing and fan fiction (as life writing) as well as opportunities for students to acquire and develop skills in analysing life writing texts, and evaluate how they can function for young readers.

Academic objectives: 
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- Describe the characteristics of different forms of (verbal and visual) offline and online life writing;
- Demonstrate knowledge of the most important theoretical concepts in the study of life writing;
- Reflect on how motives for telling about one’s own life or someone else’s life, determine the form and content of life writing.

Skills:
- Apply theoretical concepts in an analysis of life writing texts;
- Discuss and evaluate the ethics of life writing;
- Communicate their knowledge of life writing to professional audiences.

Competences:
- Independently make an analysis of life writing texts;
- Identify the importance of life writing in their own lives and that of young readers

New media for children 5 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
This course will survey the emerging media forms and formats which affect the lives of many young people.  “New Media” in this course is broadly defined to encompass a wide array of artifacts and technologies which flavor modern childhood, including but not limited to:  movies, games, videos, websites, virtual reality, apps, toys and transmedia assemblages. The goal is to engage critically with these media forms, examining how children incorporate them (or don’t) in their daily lives, and how adults shape and reshape notions of child culture and play.  In the process, we will grapple with diverse conceptions of both media and children, and confront issues that sometimes lie below the surface of media creation and use, such as power and control, privacy, safety, gender bias, cultural stereotypes, authority and violence.

Knowledge:
- Identify the developmental attributes of youth as they relate to media design, selection, and use

Skills:
- Apply knowledge of young people and media to contemporary social concerns, including fair use, safety, privacy, violence, and demographic inequity
- Contribute to debates on the effects of media on youth culture and behavior
- Apply knowledge of the historical, cultural, and technological context of youth media to contemporary youth services
- Analyze and evaluate diverse technologies, media, programs, or services designed for and/or adopted by youth

Competences:
- Conceptually design or redesign youth media
- Identify and discuss future trends in new media for youth

Detailed assignment descriptions will be made available on Canvas. These descriptions are useful as a basic guide, but the full description is the final word on assignment parameters, including length and due date.

Preparatory Notes – students will engage with the assigned weekly reading and provide 150-200 words of reflection on a question or issue that the reading raised.  Notes should be uploaded to Canvas by 8:00 p.m. the evening before class. Students are expected to reflect on 6 of 9 opportunities for a B+/A- grade. No prep notes are expected on days an assignment is due.

Values Analysis – students will use a human values-oriented framework modeled in class to analyze a piece of children’s media, a media service, or a children’s technology. The values analysis deliverable will consist of a short (<2000 words) report using a template provided in advance.

Trend Spotting – students present on a provocative, engaging or emerging trend in children’s media (~10 minutes). The presentation may showcase an artifact, phenomenon, event, or theme related to kids and media, and involve interaction with the class. Visuals are strongly encouraged. Some portion of the presentation should be set aside for audience engagement, questions, and discussion.

Youth Media Final Project – students will develop and execute a final project focusing on youth media. Students have three choices for the format of a final project:

  • select a technology or piece of interactive media and critically analyze it using a theoretical framework (4-6,000 words);
  • analyze youth behavior with a technology or media artifact using empirical data (4-6,000 words);
  • design a piece of youth media or new technology (prototype plus 2,000 word paper).

Each of these formats and processes will be modeled using in-class activities. Projects will be presented briefly in a lightning talk format during the last class session (~5 minutes). The overall project is worth 60% of the final grade; however, the proposal (10%) and bibliography (15%) are key deliverables included in the final grade.

Purpose: 
The objective of the course is to produce a substantial piece of academic work which meets the academic standards of the programme. By working on their Master’s thesis, students develop in-depth knowledge of a topic they have chosen themselves which must be within the subject area, as well as knowledge of the relevant academic literature. Students thereby develop the ability to apply the theories and methods of the programme to address a well-defined thesis statement.

Students also develop the ability to independently structure their own work process, including the ability to plan, organise and work on a substantial academic assignment, identify the need for the independent acquisition of new knowledge, and present their work in a clear and comprehensible manner.

3.2 Credit and flexibility

Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document the results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements.

Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad.

When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.


3.3 Registration and withdrawal

Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.

Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.

Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students cannot withdraw from the Master’s thesis.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.
Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.

If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Deadlines are stated on the study portal. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

Students can choose between the specified forms of exam for their Master’s thesis.


3.4 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of Master's thesis and other major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to the student's command of the content knowledge. 

In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.5 Regulations for assignments

Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.8 Exemption

An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the Board of Studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the Board of Studies forwards the application to the appropriate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented.More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal.

3.9 Appeals and complaints

Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order. More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.

3.10 Examinations

Calculation of average grade point
It is possible to get the weighted average grade point calculated and formally verified. The weighted average grade point is an expression of the average grade point calculated in relation to the ECTS weighting of the grade points. The average grade point is calculated to one decimal point based on all graded exams. The average grade is not calculated in relation to the grades of fellow students, and that the calculation of the average mark is not offered until the programme's last exam has been passed.
