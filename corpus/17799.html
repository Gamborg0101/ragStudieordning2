1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The purpose of the Master's Degree Programme in Intercultural Studies (German) is to develop students' knowledge, skills and competences in interculturality in today's transnational world.

The programme works with different theoretical approaches to interculturality to analyse the interconnections of culture, language, society and history and to enhance better coexistence and collaboration among people with diverse and multiple cultural backgrounds. Particular attention is given to the interrelationships of local, national, regional and transnational identities and to asymmetrical power-relations.

Students are introduced to concepts and methods from various disciplines within the humanities and the social sciences with a view to develop their abilities to analyse, critically reflect upon and facilitate interculturality. Students are likewise initiated to apply their knowledge, skills and competences in collaboration with external stakeholders.

The courses are divided in two primary subject areas:

1) Cultural Representations, Society and Communication examines the construction, mediation and translation of cultural products and representations (e.g. literature, visual and performative arts, and audio-visual and text media) from English language contexts. This subject area develops students' 1) insights into the historical and societal framework of the language area and into its interrelatedness with the surrounding world; 2) understanding of otherness and of its particular expressions in the language contexts and 3) language skills and competences in German.

2) Interculturality and Organisations analyses processes of intercultural interactions and explores ways to facilitate interculturality within professional contexts that include the private, public, and third sector. This subject area develops 1) students' theoretical knowledge of interculturality in organisational contexts; 2) their abilities in applying relevant theoretical concepts and methods on empirical case-studies; and 3) their capacities in informing innovative practices.

The Master's Degree Programme prepares students for employment in the private and public sector, in international organisations and in NGOs. Graduates can, for example, hold professional positions such as intercultural mediators and facilitators, communicators, project managers and leaders, consultants, teachers and researchers.


1.2 Learning outcomes

Students acquire the following knowledge, skills and competences during the Master’s Degree Programme in Intercultural Studies (German):

Knowledge:

  • Knowledge and understanding of interculturality within historical and societal contexts and with a particular consideration for the role of language and the interrelationship of multiple identities
  • Insights to examine, understand, and critically discuss relevant academic literature on interculturality in professional and organisational contexts
  • In-depth knowledge of cultural representations and intercultural interactions within the German language contexts, including discussing interrelatedness with the transnational world
  • Substantial knowledge of socio-cultural structures and historical processes that enable and constrain interculturality.

Skills:

  • Ability to identify and analyse cultural representations and processes of intercultural interactions, and establish and explain possible solutions to intercultural issues on the basis of these
  • Ability to assess and apply relevant theories and methods based on theories within the humanities and social sciences
  • Ability to understand English academic texts as well as communicate clearly and easily in English both orally and in writing via various media
  • Ability to understand German academic and literary texts as well as communicate clearly and easily in German both orally and in writing via various media
  • Ability to communicate and discuss research-based knowledge with both colleagues and non-specialists in intercultural settings.

Competences:

  • Ability to function as intercultural players and identify, facilitate and evaluate creative solutions to complex cultural, organisational and societal challenges
  • Ability to cooperate in international and intercultural teams using English and German as working-language
  • Work in a project oriented and interdisciplinary way
  • Ability to independently identify, describe and carry out projects in intercultural contexts
  • Ability to independently take responsibility for own academic development and specialisation, including identifying the potential for development through cultural differences
  • Ability to reflect critically on what is practice and to apply academic expertise to intercultural contexts.

2. Degree programme structure

2.1 Degree programme rules
In addition to the general rules (section 3) at the end of the academic regulations, the following rules apply to the programme:

2.1.1 Prerequisites for exam participation
Prerequisites are demands with which students must comply before taking an exam. If an exam includes prerequisites, this will be stated in the exam description for the specific course under the heading "Prerequisites for the examination participation". These must be met prior to taking the exam and must only be met once. Students who fail to meet this requirement will not be able to take the exam, and a examination attempt must be used.
The rules and deadlines for proving compliance with the prerequisites are stated on the study portal (studerende.au.dk/arts).

2.1.2 Language of teaching and exams
Courses can be taught in English or the foreign language concerned. The language of the teaching in each semester is stated in the course catalogue. The language of the exam is the same as the language or languages of the teaching, apart from courses in which language proficiency (including translation between the foreign language and English) is one of the objectives of the exam. The language of the exam for such courses will be stated in the course description in the course catalogue, and no exceptions will be made. 

If language proficiency is not one of the objectives of the exam, students may by arrangement with their teacher, take exams in English or the foreign language, regardless of whether the teaching has been conducted in the foreign language or English. 

2.1.3 Syllabus
Rules regarding the syllabus can be found on the study portal.

Interculturality in Organisational Contexts 1 10 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students 1) to understand central issues and questions related to interculturality in organisational and professional settings in a transnational world; 2) to acquire insight into key theories and concepts and to reflect critically on these; 3) to analyse empirically intercultural issues in complex settings that are characterised by diverse and multiple cultures, by conflicts of interests and power asymmetries; and 4) to work in a project- and problem-oriented way in groups of peers with diverse and multiple cultures.

The course provides a general introduction to various theoretical approaches to culture (i.e. interpretive and critical) and discusses how each approach raises different questions and contributes with diverse insights into intercultural interaction and collaboration in organisational and professional settings. The course works in a problem-oriented way to identify and analyse empirical examples of intercultural collaboration in situated contexts, considering the interconnections of culture, language, society and history.

The course provides students with a theoretical basis for working on interculturality in organisational and professional settings as well as with skills to analyse intercultural issues empirically. Hence, the course forms the basis for the second semester course - Interculturality in Organisational Contexts 2 - that further develops students’ theoretical insights and analytical skills as well as their capacities in informing innovative practices.

Academic objectives:

Knowledge:
- present accurately and examine central issues applying theoretical insights on interculturality in organisational and professional settings in a transnational world
- discuss and reflect critically upon the theoretical approaches and concepts in the course literature.

Skills:
- identify and analyse intercultural issues in complex settings characterised by diverse and multiple cultures, by conflicts of interests and power asymmetries
- work with and discuss various approaches to culture to analyse intercultural issues empirically
- communicate in academic English using relevant concepts and terminology. 

Competences:
- work with interculturality in a problem-oriented way
- collaborate in a group of peers with diverse and multiple cultures using English as a shared language.

History, Society and Change in German Language Contexts 10 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students (a) to understand selected theoretical historical and social science concepts, (b) to discuss key structures and processes of change in the history and society of German-speaking countries, (c) to apply theoretical and contextual knowledge to independently and methodically analyse current and/or historical phenomena, media and discourses on the basis of relevant research literature and (d) to communicate their knowledge orally in academic German.

The course focuses on history and society from an identity-theoretical perspective. In this context, the role of history in society is discussed. The course introduces students to relevant theoretical and methodical approaches. Based on these, the course treats selected aspects of history and society in German-speaking countries. Historical as well as contemporary issues, topics and debates may be incorporated in the course with a focus on, for example, regional, national or transnational identity constructions. By working with specific analytical material, for example different types of texts and audiovisual media, students strengthen their analytical competencies. During the course of the semester, students independently find and analyse material and secondary literature, which enables them to study a specific sub-topic in depth. Further, the course trains students’ oral language proficiency, for example in group discussions, presentations, audio recordings etc.

The course builds on the basic academic and linguistic competencies acquired by students in their Bachelor’s degree programme, and it prepares them for the course Media, identity and oral communication. The course is relevant to students who wish to work in intercultural contexts, in teaching or in research.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- explain and assess selected historical or social scientific concepts and reflect on the role of history in society
- discuss key structures and processes of change in the history and society of German-speaking countries.

Skills:
- independently collect and process analytical material and research-based knowledge
- apply their theoretical and contextual knowledge to conduct analyses of specific phenomena and material in an independent and methodical manner
- communicate their knowledge orally in suitably varied and academic German.

Competences:
- independently plan and structure their own learning processes
- structure complex information with a view to communicating them to a specific target group.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

Teaching takes the form of classroom instruction. This may include student presentations, lectures, exercises, group work, discussions etc.

Tendencies in contemporary German Literature and Culture 10 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is 1) to develop and strengthen students’ practical and theoretical insight into modern German literature and culture from approximately 1870 until today, 2) to expand and consolidate students’ insight into different theories of literary interpretation and 3) to enable students to practice their ability to read and understand complex linguistic statements through reading literature.

The course focuses on both contemporary and historical topics, which, among other things, means that the literary tradition is used to shed light on new, contemporary literature. In this way, students learn to understand and account for the meaning and content of the most complex texts and cultural products in both recent and past German literary, cultural and intellectual history. The course covers texts as well as other types of products such as films, podcasts, YouTube videos and other digital media. The texts and products relate to questions regarding identity, the relationship between the I and the world, between the self and the other.

With its focus on literature, art and cultural expressions, the course complements the other courses on the degree programme and is relevant to students who wish to work with the communication of cultural expressions from German-language contexts in various work contexts that require analytical insight into German literature and culture.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- account for and analyse modern German literature and culture (from approximately 1870 until today)
- interpret, discuss and put complex and linguistically advanced literary and cultural statements into perspective and place them in a literary and cultural-historical context.

Skills:
- assess and interpret German literature and apply relevant analytical methods
- communicate, structure and present selected issues within modern German literary and cultural history in a clear and coherent manner.

Competences:
- independently plan and identify their own learning needs and gain and apply new knowledge of German literary and cultural history in a manner that is appropriate to a specific situation and context
- identify a topic within modern German literary and cultural history in an independent and focused manner and communicate the in-depth knowledge acquired to others in a relevant and clear way.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

The course alternates between working in smaller groups and plenary sessions. To the extent it is useful, the course will also include various internet-based sources (video material, interviews, podcasts etc.). Instruction is based on close, text-based analyses in the perspective of the main currents in literary history. The course takes a bottom-up approach. That means students are expected to acquire the subject matter on their own initiative, under the expert supervision of the course instructor. Great emphasis is placed on dialogue.

Language of instruction and exams:
The rules governing language of teaching and exams are stated in section 2.1 of the academic regulations.

Exam language:

  • German

Exam options:

   Take-home assignment on topic of student's choice

Form of co-examination: External co-examination

Assessment form: 7-point grading scale

Comments:

Ordinary examination and re-examination:
The exam consists of a take-home assignment in German on a topic of the student’s choice. The topic and method used in the assignment must be relevant in relation to the content of the course, and is subject to the approval of the instructor.

The assignment may be written individually or in groups of up to 5 students. Group assignments must be written in such a way that the contribution of each student, except for the introduction, problem statement and conclusion, can form the basis of individual assessment. The assignment should clearly state which student is responsible for which section.

Length for 1 student: 10-12 standard pages.
Length for 2 students: 18-20 standard pages.
Length for 3 students: 26-28 standard pages.
Length for 4 students: 34-36 standard pages.
Length for 5 students: 42-44 standard pages.

The assignment must be submitted for assessment in WISEflow by the submission deadline stated in the exam timetable.

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is 1) to further students’ comprehension of challenges and opportunities related to interculturality in organisational and professional settings in a transnational world; 2) to strengthen students’ insights into relevant theories and concepts and their capacity to identify and to analyse intercultural issues working in a project and problem-oriented way; 3) to introduce students to working with practitioners on addressing intercultural challenges and opportunities in a relevant external organisation; and 4) to introduce students to developing innovative practices and solutions to intercultural issues in situated contexts.

The course builds on and further develops the content that was introduced by the course Interculturality in Organisational Contexts 1. In addition, the course introduces and trains techniques to work innovatively with intercultural practices in organisational and professional settings in a transnational world. The course also presents and develops skills for communicating to practitioners and discusses ethical questions that might arise.

The course provides students with an overall orientation towards collaborating with external stakeholders on identifying and analysing intercultural challenges and opportunities as well as on informing innovative practices for facilitating intercultural interaction and collaboration. This prepares students for doing a project placement or for carrying out a project on a self-selected intercultural issue in the framework of the course Intercultural Projects in a Transnational World in their third semester. The course also prepare students for writing their Master's Thesis in the fourth semester.

Competences:
- work with interculturality in a problem-oriented way
- collaborate in a group of peers with diverse and multiple cultures using English as a shared language

Interculturality and Communication 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to explore the linguistic and cultural background for the establishment and realisation of intercultural communication. The course will enable students to (1) delimit a topic within language or linguistics, (2) describe and analyse the selected topic using the theories and methods from the subject area, (3) structure their study, (4) select, apply and assess relevant secondary literature and (5) communicate the selected topic in writing in correct German, adjusted to the specific situation and using the linguistic conventions that apply to the selected subject area.

The course has two parts. The first part focuses on providing students with a general overview of intercultural communication and competency, while the second part covers a single topic within intercultural communication in depth. The course covers the theoretical-linguistic basis for different cultural encounters relating to German, for example multiethnolectal language in cities, literary and specialist translation, language transfer, gender linguistics and phraseology. Students are included in the teaching in interdisciplinary collaboration with other students.

The course complements students’ understanding of the linguistic dimension of interculturality and enables students to translate the acquired knowledge of interculturality into adequate linguistic practice in different communicative and cultural contexts. The intercultural communicative competencies prepare students for later parts of the study programme and for their professional practice in intercultural work contexts.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe selected theoretical and/or practical aspects of the German language
- apply significant and relevant analysis and working methods within a given linguistic subject area, with a particular focus on cultural encounters
- identify and reflect on intercultural, communicative relations on an academic basis, including the connection between language and the formation of identity.

Skills:
- delimit a topic within the selected subject area
- identify, characterise, analyse and communicate the selected topic in writing in correct German and in an appropriate manner, taking the relevant target group into account
- structure their study and apply relevant secondary literature
- apply relevant working and analysis methods within a given topic at the intersection of language and culture.

Competences:
- independently formulate a relevant project and find empirical material and associated theoretical material
- independently plan and structure their own learning processes.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

Instruction takes place in classes with varying methods of work, including instructor presentations, group work, student presentations, group discussions and exercises. The course covers selected aspects of the overall topic, which will form the basis for the student's project-based work.

Media, Identity and Oral Communication 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students (1) to further develop their knowledge and skills in the fields of culture, society and oral communication by working with identity constructions in a variety of media in German, (2) to conduct considered and well-structured analyses in a clearly defined field within the subject area of the course by applying relevant theories and methods and incorporating relevant material on the culture, society and history of German-speaking countries, (3) to select, apply and assess relevant secondary literature, including secondary literature in German and (4) to discuss and communicate knowledge in correct and suitably varied German adjusted to the specific situation, adhering to the linguistic conventions that apply to the selected subject area and taking a given target group into account.

The course covers the correlation between media and identity in society and culture. To this end, it focuses on a specific subject area of current relevance to the German-speaking countries and examines the presentation of this subject area in current and/or historical media discourses in German in a national and transnational context. Examples of subject areas include migration, Germany’s relation to Europe, nationalism, colonialism, the division of Germany, climate and environmental discourses etc. The course provides an introduction to relevant theoretical concepts, including the correlation between media and identity, and to relevant qualitative methods of analysis. On the basis hereof, students work analytically with concrete examples. Different types of media are included, for example social media, political debates, literature, films, podcasts etc. During the semester, students also work independently on their own analyses.

Simultaneously, the course focuses on students’ oral communication skills. Through oral presentations, discussions or the presentation of their own media products, students are trained in oral communication, including argumentation, presentation and communication.

The course builds on the students’ existing competencies within cultural, social and media science as well as their practical language skills. The course is relevant to students who wish to work in intercultural contexts, in teaching or in research.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- explain and discuss sound and in-depth theoretical and practical knowledge within selected aspects of communicative, societal and media-related areas related to Germany or other German-speaking countries on the basis of relevant academic literature, including knowledge of the spoken language at different levels and in different communication contexts
- reflect on relevant analysis and working methods within areas of communication, society and media and reflect on relevant communication strategies in German-speaking contexts.

Skills:
- apply their theoretical and contextual knowledge to independently and methodologically analyse relevant material in German and communicate their results in correct oral German, taking the relevant target group into account
- structure their knowledge and analysis results by applying appropriate German language tools
- express themselves orally in German at a suitably varied, academic and grammatically correct level.

Competences:
- participate in processes of communication in an intercultural context
- identify their own learning needs and independently plan their own learning processes accordingly
- argue consistently and soundly in German for academic choices, omissions and steps taken in a given situation.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Teaching takes the form of classroom instruction. This may include student presentations, lectures, exercises, group work, discussions, the presentation of students’ own media products etc.

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is 1) to further the students’ insight and understanding of theories and concepts that are relevant for analysing interculturality on a broader societal and historical level; 2) to strengthen the students’ understanding of and competences in working project- and problem-oriented; and 3) to develop the students’ capacity to discuss and critically reflect on the implications of the theoretical and methodological choices that they make when analysing intercultural issues in a transnational world.

The course covers various phenomena on societal and historical levels while exploring challenges and opportunities of interculturality in a transnational world. Focus will be on strengthening the students’ skills in doing intercultural projects in a transnational world (i.e. constructing a research question, defining a research design, collecting empirical material, analytical strategies as well as on evaluating the quality of academic projects) as well as on furthering their critical reflection on methodological issues. Throughout the course, the students will work on a self-defined project that emphasizes their academic profile while allowing them to develop insights into a field of their own choice.

The course provides students with a toolbox for doing intercultural projects in a transnational world. The course also serves as a preparation for writing their Master’s thesis in the fourth semester.

Competences:
- work in a project-oriented way with an intercultural issue either individually or in collaboration with a group of students

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course Project placement is 1) to give the students insight into and experiences with working in an organisation in the private, public, or third sector; 2) to allow the students to apply theoretical insights on interculturality in practice and in collaboration with their host organisation; and 3) to enable the students to complete an independently defined project on an empirical and practice-orientated issue that underline their academic profile.

In the Project placement at a public or private organisation, the students are given the opportunity to shape their Master’s degree programme in a specific academic direction, including incorporating other subject areas and thus adding to their individual academic profile.

During the Project placement the students discuss with their supervisor the focus of the project assignment, the collection of relevant empirical material and the theoretical approach to be adopted. The project analysis includes a consideration of the academic competences developed by the students during the Project placement, as well as a discussion of how the students’ academic competences have helped to solve specific tasks and challenges. 

Parallel with the Project placement, the students will do preliminary work for the Master’s thesis, this includes handing-in assignments that outline their idea for the subject area and problem statement of their thesis, relevant literature and analysis material and a plan for the Master’s thesis writing process.

The Project placement strengthens the students’ ability to work with intercultural issues in an external organisation, drawing on their understanding of interculturality that they acquired through the courses on the degree programme. Skills:

- collect and apply relevant information and data in relation to identifying and analysing an intercultural issue.

Competences:

Master's thesis

30 ECTS
Master's thesis 30 ECTS Semester 4 7-point grading scale
Exam details

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the Master’s thesis is to produce a substantial piece of academic work that meets the academic standards of the degree programme - Intercultural Studies.

From within the degree programme’s two subject areas, the students choose a topic that allows for addressing interculturality and intercultural interactions within sites that are related to German language contexts.

The Master’s thesis work provides the students with an in-depth knowledge of the subject field that they have chosen as well as of the relevant academic literature. The students thereby develop the ability to apply the theories and methods of the degree programme to analyse a well-defined research question. The students also develop the capacity to independently structure their own work process, including the ability to plan, organise and work on a substantial academic assignment, identify the need for the independent acquisition of new knowledge, and present their work in a clear and comprehensible manner.

If the Master’s thesis takes the form of a product thesis, the student will additionally develop the ability to create a connection between the analytical/theoretical/empirical point of departure and the product. If the Master’s thesis takes the form of a portfolio thesis, the student will develop the ability to produce academic texts which deal with one overall academic issue, as well as a synthesising presentation which presents relevant theoretical and methodical issues and perspectives in relation to the overall assignment. If the Master’s thesis takes the form of a group thesis, students will also develop their collaborative skills.

The Master’s thesis is the final component of the Master's degree programme, which develops and strengthens the student’s ability to work independently on an academic basis and to provide an overview of more complex types of material and work processes, and to provide an opportunity to develop a special profile with regard to knowledge, analytical and methodological skills. This is crucial for the candidate’s further career.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to: 

Knowledge:
- independently examine and immerse themselves in a topic related to German language contexts
- explain and discuss the relevance of the selected subject field and research question in relation to current research and/or practice within the field
- critically reflect on and assess the bearings of the chosen theories, methodology and any empirical basis on the thesis’ analysis and any product.

Skills:
- prepare a delimited research question with a view to using it for academic research
- use academic theories and methods critically and consciously, including choosing and applying relevant primary and secondary literature
- communicate in writing in a coherent, well-argued and comprehensible manner at a high level of academic precision in terms of grammar, spelling and language; using a suitably varied vocabulary, including relevant concepts and terminology; and using academic conventions in terms of layout, notes, references and citations
- present academic knowledge accurately and appropriately.

Competences:
- conduct independent academic research in an ethically responsible manner
- independently and possibly in collaboration with a group of students, plan and carry out the Master's thesis process within the given framework.

3.2 Regulations for commencement of studies exam

The compulsory commencement of studies exam will be held before the end of September on the first semester of the Master’s degree programme.
The exam generates 0 ECTS credits and is a pass/fail internal examination with no co-examiner. Two attempts to pass this exam are allowed. First-semester students will be registered for the exam automatically. The result will be posted in the student’s self-service system (mit.au.dk). Students who do not pass their first examination attempt will be registered automatically for their second attempt.
The result of the second attempt will be posted in the student’s self-service system (mit.au.dk) no later than 28 September. Students who fail both their first and second attempt will be withdrawn from their degree programme with immediate effect and without further warning.


3.3 Credit and flexibility

Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document the results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements.

Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad.

When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.


3.4 Registration and withdrawal

Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.

Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.

Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students cannot withdraw from the Master’s thesis.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.
Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.

If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Deadlines are stated on the study portal. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

Students can choose between the specified forms of exam for their Master’s thesis.


3.5 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of Master's thesis and other major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to the student's command of the content knowledge. 

In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.6 Regulations for assignments

Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.7 Using computers for examinations

Most written exams at AU are taken in a digital exams system. More information about digital exams may be found on the study portal. See also Aarhus University’s rules and regulations on on-site examinations Collection of internal rules for students at Aarhus University (au.dk)

3.8 Project-oriented course

Options for project-oriented courses are stated in the description of the individual study element. It is possible to take project placement with more than one project host if it is deemed to live up to all requirements for project placement.


3.9 Exemption

An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the board of studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the board of studies forwards the application to the appropriate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and must be submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal.

3.10 Appeals and complaints

Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order.

More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.


3.11 Examinations

Calculation of average grade point
It is possible to get the weighted average grade point calculated and formally verified. The weighted average grade point is an expression of the average grade point calculated in relation to the ECTS weighting of the grade points. The average grade point is calculated to one decimal point based on all graded exams. The average grade is not calculated in relation to the grades of fellow students, and that the calculation of the average mark is not offered until the programme's last exam has been passed.
