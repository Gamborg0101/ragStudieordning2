1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The Bachelor’s Supplementary Subject in Addressing Global Challenges studies the rising global challenges to climate, environment, societies and states and possible sustainable solutions at global and local levels. Based on the premise that numerous global issues are interrelated and generate ever more dramatic, global challenges, the supplementary subject focuses on specific challenges on the UN’s list of global issues and on several issues among the seventeen Sustainable Development Goals. It will train students in developing a critical understanding of global processes, transformations and structural inequalities. The students will analyse how global challenges find expression at national, regional and global levels, and how they affect regions of the world in different ways. The supplementary subject focuses on practices of global governance through international organisations such as the UN as they respond to the global challenges. Through case studies of the most urgent challenges related to environment, climate change, migration, and different forms of inequalities, the supplementary subject will provide students with competences in comparing, assessing and analysing solutions at different levels. A ‘global lab’ will be established as a frame for student engagement with specific case studies and local solutions. The Global Lab refers to a study environment where students – supervised by educators – work in groups during and in-between classes on exploring local solutions to local challenges in collaboration with external partners.

The goal of the supplementary is to equip students with a global perspective and a strong sense of global responsibility, and to enable them to develop interdisciplinary competences to explore sustainable solutions and engage in concrete solutions at local levels, all of which complements students’ central bachelor’s programs and strengthens their ability to undertake interdisciplinary work in collaboration with external partners.


1.2 Learning outcomes

The Bachelor’s Supplementary Subject in Addressing Global Challegnes provides students with the following knowledge, skills and competences:

Knowledge:

  • Knowledge of global challenges and sustainable development goals
  • Critical understanding of global processes and their transformations, and of the structural inequalities between the Global North and the Global South
  • Knowledge of practices of global governance at different geographic scales and institutional levels
  • Knowledge of urgent, global challenges related to climate change, environment, migration, and of different forms of inequalities
  • Reflection on global challenges in specific contexts.

Skills:

  • Ability to analyse different interrelated factors that produce global challenges in different contexts
  • Ability to collect, manage, and analyse data on global issues and responses relating to global challenges at different levels
  • Ability to assess theoretical and practical responses to global challenges and to compare different solutions to these challenges
  • Ability to communicate to different stakeholders about solutions to global challenges within the field of study.

Competences:

  • Competence to handle complex challenges and to assess, analyse and engage with possible solutions within different contexts
  • Competence to enter into cross-disciplinary collaboration with the purpose of engaging in new ways of addressing global challenges
  • Competence to identify their own learning needs based on their disciplinary background and organise their own learning in different contexts and for different recipients.

Introduction to Global Challenges 15 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of this course is to enable students to understand and analyse the specific patterning, causes, developments, and implications of global challenges. Students will use this knowledge to develop the necessary skills to analyse the complexity of global challenges today, understand their entanglement with the overarching challenge of global inequalities, and gain basic skills in engaging, assessing and suggesting possible solutions.

Students acquire knowledge about theories of the global and of global interconnections, as well as about global inequalities, fault lines, and conflicts that determine global transformations. Global challenges are assessed in terms of their scale, their consequences, and the power relations they involve. The course studies how global challenges are formulated and approached by core institutions and actors – most notably in the form of the UN’s ‘Sustainable Development Goals’ and list of ‘Global Issues’ – and it analyses and evaluates possible ways to solve these. The course examines concrete cases that link critical analysis to workable solutions.

The course serves as an introduction to the degree programme. It conceptualises the notion of global challenges through theories of the global, of global transformations, and of global inequalities. It provides students with tools to analyse global challenges without losing sight of the political-economic structuring of our unequal world, and it engages students in working with concrete, local cases and with local partners such as the municipality or the university in the programme’s ‘global lab.’

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
-describe and account for the main theories of the global, global transformations, and global inequalities
-reflect on global challenges in specific contexts.

Skills:
-apply theories to understand the patterning, causes, developments, and implications of global challenges
-analyse and compare cases that illustrate different types of global challenges and their interconnection with the overarching challenge of inequality
-assess, analyse, contextualise, and compare global challenges and sustainable solutions.

Competences:
-Identify and plan sustainable and workable solutions in collaboration with external partners.

Global Governance 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students to understand and analyse the concept and practice of global governance as well as its role in relation to global challenges. The students will be able to understand and evaluate the complex mechanisms of international organisations and their relations to states and non-state actors when addressing the rising number of global challenges.

This course deals with concepts, theories and practices of global governance. Its central focus is on the understanding and analysis of discourses and policies created by international organizations such as the United Nations, the World Bank, or the International Monetary Foundation (IMF) in interaction with different state and non-state actors. Students learn how international legal regimes take form, operate and influence actors at different institutional levels. Through case studies they will analyse practices of global governance specifically oriented towards solving societal, political, economic and environmental challenges that require global collective action. Specifically, they will look at how power relations and inequalities affect the practice of global governance.

This course presents students with knowledge of global mechanisms (institutions, discourses, policies) created to address and manage the rising societal, political, economic and environmental challenges of a global nature, with a particular focus on the Sustainable Development Goals formulated by the UN. The course thus draws directly on Introduction to Global Challenges, and it provides a common framework for global responses and solutions that will inform the study of specific global challenges in the other two modules of this semester.

Academic objectives: 
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
-account for the main concepts and theories of global governance in the 20th and 21st century
-account for the main institutional mechanisms, the power hierarchies and the main actors of global governance as well as the practices and effects of global governance in different regional and local contexts
-recognise and reflect critically upon different justifications and rationales for sustainable development provided by global governance.

Skills:
-collect, manage and analyse data on actors, institutions and discourses of global governance in international organizations
-compare and analyse different solutions to specific global challenges through case studies.

Competences:
- independently present a case study based on theories of global governance
-apply knowledge of global governance in an intercultural and interregional context towards sustainable goals and solutions.

Migration and Mobilities 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of this course is to enable students to understand, identify and analyse human mobilities within and across borders as one of the key political and social issues of our time. This will enable them to assess, address and engage in solutions to the causes and effects of migration and mobility, if they present challenges to local societies or communities. Students will learn to reflect critically on human migration and mobilities in a diachronic perspective, and to apply different migration theories to explain migration dynamics in specific cases.

The course provides a general overview of why migration has become a global challenge in today’s world. It does so by introducing students to what constitutes migration, why it occurs, and how it is sustained. It provides knowledge of the political, social, and cultural impact of migration and critically discusses international and national responses to migration and mobility. The course includes an introduction to central theoretical approaches to understanding the causes and effects of migration and mobilities in different parts of the world. Subsequently, it will be a focal point of the course to analyse and evaluate local and global solutions aimed at mitigating the potentially negative effects of migration.

This course builds on the knowledge and skills students have acquired in “Introduction to Global Challenges” by focusing in depth on one particular global challenge, and it thus forms an integral part of the Bachelor's supplementary subject.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe the main theories of causes and effects of international migration and mobility
- account for and explain the main patterns of migration and mobility in a diachronic perspective
- reflect on migration in the specific context of sustainability.

Skills:
- choose appropriate methods to analyse aspects related to global or local challenges of migration and mobilities
- identify and evaluate global or local responses and challenges related to migration and mobilities.

Competences:
- take into account the significance of specific contexts in the assessment of migration and mobility as global challenges
- explore, analyse, and engage in sustainable solutions to migration and mobilities in collaboration with others.

Environment and Climate Change 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students to understand global environmental challenges and climate change adopting an interdisciplinary analytical framework. They will learn to analyse the complex interplay of historical, societal and political dimensions that characterises these challenges. The course trains students in communicating the analysis of this interplay orally and in writing for different audiences such as expert communities, decision-makers and the broader public. They also learn critically to assess proposed solutions aiming at socio-ecological sustainability.

The course covers various aspects of global environmental challenges and climate change at global, local, regional, and trans-regional scales. The course may adopt approaches from fields such as history, anthropology, sociology and political science. Students will approach environmental challenges and climate change from different angles such as socio-ecological sustainability, environmental justice, risk and vulnerability, trans-regional commodity and waste flows, and environmental governance.

The course builds on the course "Introduction to Global Challenges" and has important synergies with the courses on "Global Governance" and "Migration and Mobilities".

3.2 Curriculum for The Supplementary Subject

3.2.1 Credit and flexibility
Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document his or her results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements. Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.

3.2.2 Registration and withdrawal
Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.

Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.

Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.
Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.

If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Deadlines are stated on the study portal. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

3.2.3 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to his/her command of the content knowledge.
In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.2.4 Definition of standard page
Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.2.5 Using computers for examinations
Most written exams at AU are taken in a digital exams system. More information about digital exams may be found on the study portal. See also Aarhus University’s rules and regulations on the use of computers at exams http://www.au.dk/fileadmin/www.au.dk/Regelsamlingen/2016/REGLER_OM_BRUG_AF_COMPUTER_VED_PROEVER_2016.pdf

3.2.6 Exemption
An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the Board of Studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the Board of Studies forwards the application to the appro-priate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal portal.

3.2.7 Appeals and complaints
Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing and must account for the student’s reasons for lodging the complaint. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order. More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.

3.2.8 Exchange
A degree programme may include study abroad. More information is available on the study portal.
