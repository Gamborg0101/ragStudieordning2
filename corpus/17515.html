1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The aim of the Bachelor’s Supplementary Subject in Social Minds is to develop the student’s knowledge, skills and competences in understanding the social nature of human beings. Social Minds lies at the intersection of various disciplines addressing human sociality. Four courses will cover A) how human thought and emotion is shaped by other people, B) how different cognitive processes enable people to perform tasks together, C) how social media influence social interactions and D) how culture and human cognition interact. The student learns about multiple levels of the social realm, from analyses of small-group interactions to analyses of larger communities, and gains practical insight into various conceptual, qualitative and quantitative methods. The student can also practically train social and collaboration skills by working together with others in interdisciplinary groups.   

With an interdisciplinary approach, a focus on considering real-world challenges through the viewpoint of social influences and a combination of theory and practical experience through group projects, the Bachelor’s Supplementary Subject in Social Minds aims at preparing students for professions within e.g. communication, human-technology interaction, NGOs, social media, design, consumer choice, intercultural mediation, and education.

1.2 Learning outcomes

Students acquire the following knowledge, skills and competences during the Bachelor´s Supplementary Subject in Social Minds:

Knowledge:

  • Knowledge of basic theories in philosophy of mind, cognition and social interaction, social media and communication, and religion, cognition and culture
  • Knowledge of the basic functioning of various methodological approaches, both conceptual and empirical, related to social minds
  • The ability to understand the theoretical background of empirical evidence and the methods used to acquire it related to social minds
  • The ability to understand and reflect on areas of social influence and their changes, both from a historical and a contemporary perspective.

Skills:

  • The ability to develop new project ideas by collaboratively designing basic research projects on the topic of social minds, also considering their practical implementation
  • The ability to analyse data relating to social influences on human thought and behaviour
  • The ability to compare different theoretical, qualitative or quantitative methods and choose an appropriate one for a particular task
  • The ability to identify relevant questions pertinent to the study of social mind, linking scientific theories and research findings to contemporary topics and public discourse
  • The ability to critically assess and communicate theories and empirical evidence on social mind topics to relevant audiences.

Competences:

  • The ability to independently select relevant literature in the different study areas
  • The ability to collaborate with other people on projects across disciplines and take on responsibility for unique roles within a group project
  • The ability to identify individual or discipline-specific skills within an interdisciplinary work team.

Description of qualifications:

Purpose: 
The purpose of the course is to introduce students to classical and contemporary approaches to problems related to sociality and social relations. More specifically, the course will enable students to understand and reflect on A) the social nature of human living from its ontological (e.g. what is a group?), epistemological (e.g. how do groups develop knowledge?) and ethical aspects; B) the sociality of action, the possibility of sharing intentional states, and theories of joint action; C) the sociality and morality of emotional states such as happiness, love, and anger.

The course is concerned with basic problems, concepts and positions related to an understanding of the human being as a social being. It deals with areas and topics such as social ontology (e.g. what makes multiple individuals a group?), social cognition (e.g. how do I know what you feel?), collective or shared intentionality (e.g. what does it mean to do things together?) or social emotions (e.g. what is pride or shame?). It will address questions about how individuals understand themselves and each other in a social context.

The course provides a theoretical background for the three following courses, introducing ‘big questions’ that will be dealt with in more detail in the 2nd semester of the program.

Academic objectives:

In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate understanding of and explain basic theories in philosophy of mind and social cognition especially relating to the ontological, epistemological and ethical dimensions of sociality
- compare theories of collective intentionality and emotions.

Skills:
- identify and analyse challenges related to the social nature of human existence
- apply philosophically based conceptualizations and theories to a particular problem related to social minds
- critically assess and communicate about different theoretical accounts on course-relevant issues.

Competences:
- identify individual or discipline-specific skills and their potential within an interdisciplinary work team.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

The course will be taught in a mixture of group-based workshops, individual class work, lectures and discussions. It will start with a short introductory course, which opens up the field of issues, its major questions and topics that will be linked to the courses in the 2nd semester.

Cognition in Social Interaction 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose: 
The purpose of the course is to enable students to A) understand human cognition and its relevance for shaping social interaction; B) assess different methods for investigating cognitive processes and their challenges when applying them to a social context; and C) in an interdisciplinary work group, design an empirical study or project in the area of social interaction.

This course focuses on how social context influences human cognition, e.g. shared memory, interpersonal action coordination, social perception, learning, and decision-making. A central part of this course is about measuring cognition in social interaction, i.e. how we can make the cognitive processes in social context visible and observable.

The course provides the tools to understand social processes from a cognitive perspective. It enables students to convert theoretical research questions into testable hypotheses of cognition. In working towards a group-based project, this course gives students the opportunity to take up a topic or question from the introduction week of the 1st semester.

Academic objectives: 
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe, explain and relate theories on social cognition, with a focus on how basic cognitive processes provide the basis for social interaction
- explain and compare of the methods used for analysing cognition and behaviour in social context.

Skills:
- evaluate research in the area of social interaction, especially based on their methodological approach and interpretation
- design and prepare a project (e.g. empirical research, theoretical analysis or public dissemination) in the area of social interaction
- identify relevant research methodologies to address specific questions in the area of cognition and social interaction.

Competences:
- independently select relevant literature in the area of cognition and social interaction
- organize work in an interdisciplinary group by assessing own and other people’s skills.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

The course consists of lectures, class room instruction, discussions and group work. Students will also practically work together in groups to design and, if possible, implement a project that can directly relate back to the questions raised during the introductory workshop in the 1st semester.

Language of instruction:
The rules governing language of exam and teaching are stated in section 2.1 of the academic regulations.

Ordinary examination and re-examination:

The scope and nature of the product must be relevant in relation to the content of the course and is subject to the approval of the teacher. It must be possible to submit the product digitally in a documented form which can be accessed by the supervisor and co-examiner.

Cultural Interactions 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose: 
The purpose of the course is to enable students to A) understand how culturally transmitted ideas influence individual behaviour and motivate collective responses to major societal challenges; B) analyse worldviews and their implications for norms, values, and behaviours; and C) communicate insights and findings to different relevant target audiences.

The course covers various theories and models of culture-cognition interaction within particular domains of human life, e.g. religion, science, play, work, environment, gender, and health. The course introduces theories of how cultural practices and ideas work to align norms, values and behaviours among members of a given society. The course also introduces to qualitative and quantitative methods that are relevant for studying the link between culture and cognition.

The course expands on the scope of the other elements of the degree programme by connecting the cognitive processes of the social mind to a cultural level, where complex cognitions and behaviours become integrated in individual worldviews. 

Academic objectives: 
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe, explain and relate theories and models of culture-cognition interaction
- explain and compare qualitative and quantitative methods that are relevant for studying the link between culture and cognition.

Skills:
- identify areas of cultural influence on human interaction
- analyse data on culture-cognition interactions
- communicate theoretical and empirical insights on culture-cognition interaction to different relevant target audiences
- identify and evaluate relevant research methodologies to address specific questions in the areas of religion, cognition and culture.

Competences:
- independently select relevant literature in the area of religion, cognition and culture.

3.2 Curriculum for The Supplementary Subject

3.2.1 Credit and flexibility
Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document his or her results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements. Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.

3.2.2 Registration and withdrawal
Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.
Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.
Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.
Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.

If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Deadlines are stated on the study portal. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

3.2.3 Spelling and fluency
In the assessment of major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to his/her command of the content knowledge.
In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.2.4 Definition of standard page
Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.2.5 Using computers for examinations
Most written exams at AU are taken in a digital exams system. More information about digital exams may be found on the study portal. See also Aarhus University’s rules and regulations on the use of computers at exams http://www.au.dk/fileadmin/www.au.dk/Regelsamlingen/2016/REGLER_OM_BRUG_AF_COMPUTER_VED_PROEVER_2016.pdf

3.2.6 Exemption
An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the Board of Studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the Board of Studies forwards the application to the appro-priate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal portal.

3.2.7 Appeals and complaints
Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing and must account for the student’s reasons for lodging the complaint. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order. More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.

3.2.8 Exchange
A degree programme may include study abroad. More information is available on the study portal.
