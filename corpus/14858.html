1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The aim of the Master’s degree programme is to produce graduates who possess research-based knowledge and critical, analytical skills in dealing with heritage as a field of study and form of practice in a broad sense. We aim to equip graduates with practical skills and competences with regard to institutional frameworks and production and communication based in a thorough understanding of the pressing social, economical and environmental sustainability concerns of the contemporary heritage sector. This includes working with heritage at the level of international organisations, the state, local, and private companies, as well as in non-governmental organisations. We aim to provide graduates with the skills and competences needed to implement sustainable heritage projects locally, nationally and globally through utilizing appropriate project management tools. They will be provided with the necessary intellectual grounding and formal qualifications for further studies such as in a PhD degree programme.

1.2 Learning outcomes

The Master's programme in Sustainable Heritage Management provides students with the following knowledge, skills and competences:

Knowledge:

  • In-depth knowledge about the central theories and concepts that distinguish heritage studies as an interdisciplinary research field in an international context
  • Knowledge about the identification, documentation, data handling, registration, archiving and preservation of heritage
  • Reflective knowledge about the forms of production and techniques through which heritage can be presented, and their relations with users and stakeholders
  • Understanding of the relationship between heritage and production in various genres, media, materialities and institutional paradigms
  • Knowledge and understanding of digital technology and its potential in relation to heritage projects
  • Knowledge about heritage as a resource for society, in particular the creation of communities, sustainable development and economic progress
  • Knowledge about theories and practice of administration and the project management of heritage
  • Knowledge about the cultural/political, legal and administrative contexts in which heritage features locally, nationally and globally.

Skills:

  • Using the theories, methods and tools of the field to identify heritage, as well as the theoretics of planning and carrying out data collection, registration and archiving
  • Identifying, analysing and assessing central issues of heritage as a resource for society locally, nationally and internationally
  • Carrying out thorough analyses of specific heritage phenomena with a view to assessing their significance and potentials
  • Identifying concepts and utilizing case-studies to facilitate, designing and implementing new, motivational and sustainable forms of heritage in cooperation with specific national and international players
  • Presenting and discussing (in speech and writing) relevant issues of a practical and academic nature with due regard for the target group in question.

Competences:

  • Giving advice on issues of heritage based on academically sound knowledge
  • Developing and designing innovative and motivational forms of presenting and communicating heritage
  • Helping to develop institutional and political frameworks for socially, economically and environmentally sustainable heritage
  • Contributing to the organisational, financial and administrative development of heritage institutions and projects
  • Launching, organising and managing processes related to the sustainable administration, preservation and presentation of heritage
  • Entering into heritage projects in cooperation with public and civil institutions and networks as well as with private companies and organisations
  • Advising various stakeholders about the development and implementation of sustainable heritage initiatives on vaious levels.

Sources and methods in heritage studies 10 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
Heritage Studies is an interdisciplinary field. In this course, various sources and methodological approaches to heritage as a field of study are introduced. The goal is to equip students to identify heritage phenomena and assess their historical and/or academic anchoring, as well as to present them with a toolkit to engage with heritage through various disciplinary gazes. This course works systematically with various methodological approaches to heritage as a field of study and practice and the source categories in which heritage may be based. It is designed to give students a basic orientation for further research in Heritage Studies.
The different sources introduced through this course include text, material culture, people, and landscapes. Methods introduced through this course may include, for example, archival analysis, oral history interviews, participant observation, visual research methods, archaeological survey and analysis, affective research methods, discourse analysis, working with landscapes and the built environment, artistic research methods.
The course is characterised by an interdisciplinary approach to the field and provides an introduction to a wide variety of methodologies salient to heritage studies as practised within the various subject areas, as well as addressing their integration in heritage work.

Academic goals:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- understand the different sources and methodological approaches that characterize the field of Heritage Studies
- reflect on the strengths and weaknesses of these different sources and methodological approaches
- have a basic orientation towards heritage-focused research
- understand the ethics involved in using different sources and methodological approaches.

Skills:
- discuss and use different sources and methods to carry out heritage-focused research
- understand questions of sustainability and resilience with regard to the use of different sources and methods in carrying out heritage-focused research
- identify research topics, and to articulate sources, methods and research questions to carry out heritage-focused research.

Competences:
- the ability to work independently to carry out research.

Criteria for assessing the fulfilment of academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the performance, special emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student fulfils the specified academic objectives, including:
- can identify heritage and its historical and/or academic anchoring
- has an understanding of sustainability insofar as it applies to heritage as a field of study and practice
- can demonstrate knowledge of, and can use, various sources and methods to generation data and knowledge about heritage
- can compare various sources and methods and can assess their strengths, weaknesses and sustainability in the analysis of heritage phenomenon
- has a basic understanding of what sources are, and of the relationship between sources and methods in heritage-focused research
- has a basic understanding of research ethics and is able to apply this knowledge in a practical context.

Theory and concepts in heritage studies 10 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The objective of this course is to ensure students have a shared conceptual platform relating to the key issues being discussed and debated in contemporary heritage studies. This course introduces them to competing theoretical frameworks, relevant concepts and a variety of standpoints in heritage to facilitate them to decode the literature and ultimately so they can articulate their own research within a theoretically robust framework. The course articulates a broad conception of heritage ranging from heritage as an agent in, and source of historical processes, to heritage as a discursive field in the contemporary world. The theories are presented through case studies which strengthen the ability of the students to analyze the various facets on which heritage theories are based.
Based on sound understandings of the historical development and semantic field of Heritage Studies, the basic conceptions of what heritage is and how different theories conceive it are a starting point. More specific theories in the field are introduced such as how we understand tangible and intangible cultural heritage, natural heritage, authenticity, value, memory, community, identity and sustainability. The most significant subject traditions in the area and various academic perspectives on heritage will be considered. The role of heritage in society and its political embeddedness are discussed based on its relationship with (for instance) wider political process is such as nation-building, processes of decolonisation, local identity creation, community heritage, marginalized heritages, and counter-cultures. During the course, the students will also enhance their ability to give and receive feedback through group work.
This course provides the conceptual tools for the degree programme. Its completion will ensure the students are able to move forward being confident in decoding the academic texts that form the basis of Heritage Studies and proactively utilize relevant theoretical concepts in their own research and praxis.

Academic goals:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge about the central theories and concepts that distinguish heritage as an interdisciplinary research field in a global context
- demonstrate reflexive knowledge about the history and various understandings of the concept of heritage from its inception and as it has developed and diverged up until today
- have an overview of the central approaches to the understanding of heritage in theory and in praxis.

Skills:
- dissect academic writing and practical interpretation in terms of how it implicitly or explicitly utilizes heritage as a concept
- understand how theories about social, economic and environmental sustainability impact on the field of heritage
- use theories, concepts and standpoints within the wider field of Heritage Studies to dissect and interpret their own research.

Competences:
- present cogent reasons for the value and understanding of heritage within contemporary society
- work independently.

Criteria for assessing the fulfilment of academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the performance, special emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student fulfils the specified academic objectives, including:
- demonstrate insight into the historical development and current status of concepts and theories active within the field of heritage
- demonstrate understanding of significant disciplinary traditions and various philosophical perspectives on heritage as a subject of study and mode of practice
- can utilize a variety of relevant theories relating to the interconnection of social / cultural, economic and environmental sustainability as relevant to the field of heritage.

Forms of instruction:

Understanding heritage management frameworks 10 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The objective of this course is to introduce the students to the institutional frameworks through which heritage is organised, used and administered around the world. Drawing on an understanding of appropriate theory and principles of heritage management, students will be able to grasp some of the complexities of how heritage is used and administered within local, national and international contexts.
The course introduces the students to the legal and political basis for the administration of various forms of heritage, including international conventions and national legislation. Discussions focus on the scalar interplay of nation states alongside international institutions (e.g. UNESCO, the EU and WAC) and a range of regional and local community and cultural institutions in Denmark and elsewhere. The course also explores how frameworks in theory are translated into pragmatic practice in sometimes unexpected ways, for instance by examining how heritage has been impacted and influenced by legal and institutional frameworks that were not designed explicitly to engage with it. Thus, students will recognise that while elements of heritage are often valued on a personal, communal, or perhaps a 'universal' level, they tend to be managed within the context of socio-political and financial imperatives of the region, the nation or even the industrial conglomerate (such as a mining company, for instance).
Through the reflexive analysis of how heritage management and institutional frameworks operate, this module both builds upon the intellectual development prompted by the Theory and Concepts in Heritage Studies module, while making cogent and practical connections with the Sources and Methods in Heritage Studies module. Particular importance is given to the principles for the sustainable administration of heritage, which will be discussed based on questions of the politics of heritage and the roles of the various stakeholders in the field of heritage

Academic goals:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate reflexive knowledge about the history and various understandings of the institutionalisation of heritage
- demonstrate knowledge of legal, institutional and administrative frameworks for heritage as a field of practice, nationally and internationally.

Skills:
- discuss sources and conditions for knowledge of heritage institutions and framings
- convey ideas and theories about sustainability in the field of heritage management
- use theories and analytical skills within the various methodological approaches to heritage as an organisational field.

Competences:
- give reasons for and argue for the value and understanding of heritage
- articulate an understanding of how heritage is organized and managed through formal and informal institutions at a variety of scales
- connect specific case study material to broader intellectual framings of heritage scholarship and organizational principles.

Criteria for assessing the fulfilment of academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the performance, special emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student fulfils the specified academic objectives, including:
- demonstrate insight into the political and legal frameworks of heritage administration in regional, national and international contexts
- demonstrate knowledge and understanding of state and international organisations in the field of heritage and their roles in instutionalising and managing it
- can account for central principles, discourses within, and implications of, administrative practice
- can compare and reflect on the principles of a sustainable administration of heritage in various contexts.

Comments on form of instruction:

Production and communication of heritage 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
This course explores the production and communication of heritage in contemporary society. The course will prepare students for working out their own heritage intervention for a defined target audience, for example through cooperating with external partners and heritage entrepreneurs. Amongst other topics, it explores the relevance and importance of digital and media-dependent forms in producing and experiencing heritage, and ideas around the co-creation and co-production of heritage.
The course includes established and emergent theories and case studies in the production and communication of heritage, for example digital heritage, artistic productions and post-human and post-truth heritage. The course will offer an understanding of the economic, political and cultural relevance and consequences of heritage production and communication. It will also allow students to map out and analyze different user groups, for example social movements, local memory communities, artistic groups or solo artists, small entrepreneurs, and cross-cultural publics such as tourists. The course will offer an understanding of how multiple publics co-create, perform and experience heritage, by, for example, referring to theories and methods of participation, radical democracy, Human Computer Interactions, and theories of materiality. The course will touch upon elements of economic, environmental, socio-political and cultural sustainability.
The course provides students with both practical and conceptual skills relating to the production and communication of heritage, working with multiple media and multiple publics. It will equip students to design and to reflect on strategies of social inclusion and cooperation in the presentation and production of heritage. It will also allow students to reflect on questions of sustainability and resilience in the production and communication of heritage.

Academic goals:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge of heritage practices and the influence of different user groups in the production and communication of heritage
- demonstrate an understanding of new forms of heritage, and of heritagisation processes in different social contexts
- demonstrate familiarity with the role of different media and participatory processes in the production and communication of heritage.

Skills:
- produce their own data on various user groups and critically assess their roles in heritage production and experience
- see the potentialities of different media, materiality and participation strategies in heritage practices
- include and apply principles of social, economic, culture and environmental sustainability in heritage practice.

Competences:
- plan a heritage intervention in public space aiming at a target audience, working in groups or individually
- take responsibility for their choices with regard to the intervention, the target audience and the sustainability agenda of the course.

Criteria for assessing the fulfilment of academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the performance, special emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student fulfils the specified academic objectives, including:
- understand and can apply theoretical and methodological premises for the design, production and staging of heritage in the media and other social contexts
- demonstrate knowledge of communication and presentation theories
- demonstrate the ability to reflect on various user groups and to reflect on user inclusion processes in production and communication projects
- can use multi-modal, multi-sensory and aesthetic tools in heritage communication both for their peers and for various groups of non-specialists.

Forms of instruction:

  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:
The instruction alternates between lectures, tutorials, exercises, student presentations, case work and discussion. During the semester the students submit a number of small assignments in their study groups.

Heritage project management 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
Ultimately this course is an introduction to the world of project management tools, practices and skills. The students will be given an insight into the tools used in project management that are necessary to ensure professionally formulated proposal and they will be facilitated in utilizing them in response to a particular heritage issue or problem.
The course covers a number of aspects of project management that are relevant to all projects eg creating a budget, assessing health and safety concerns, creating communication strategies, and analysing the needs of the stakeholders involved. It also expects the students to think about which aspects of the project management toolkit are especially relevant and specific to their project design. They will be mentored in how to select the most relevant tools from the project management toolkit while also explicitly engaging with aspects of sustainability to ensure the proposal is viable and resilient.
The course provides students with the opportunity to work with real world heritage problems, issues and scenarios and to work through project management tools to create a professional-standard proposal that addresses and alleviates their problem. This course is based on real world problems affecting the heritage field and allows the students to visualise how heritage problem-solving can work in practice.

Academic goals:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge of a variety of tools and methods as relevant to heritage from the field of project management
- demonstrate knowledge of heritage management issues and how they relate to funding and administrative structures in the real world.

Skills:
- problem-solve a heritage-related issue, problem or scenario in order to propose a viable, potential solution
- apply various project management tools in the creation of a viable and coherent heritage project management proposal

Competences:
- develop a coherent and detailed project management proposal as relevant to the particular heritage problem, issue or scenario selected by the student
- articulate a proposal to apply for funding for a potential heritage project in the context of state and international institutions as well as companies and civil organisations
- work in cross-disciplinary fashion and on a project-focused basis.

Criteria for assessing the fulfilment of academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the performance, special emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student fulfils the specified academic objectives, including:
- can identify, select and use relevant project management tools in creating a heritage project proposal
- can choose, scale and manage a heritage project proposal
- can reflect on and consider the balance of sustainability issues in heritage projects
- can formulate a detailed proposal for implementing a potential heritage project
- can create a viable heritage project proposal.

Forms of instruction:

  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:
The instruction involves a combination of lectures, case-based practical exercises and group work outside of the classroom. At an early stage of the semester, the students will form into groups and will work primarily outside the classroom in order to use the appropriate aspects of the lecture-taught elements to create proposals for "real" heritage projects. Ultimately the students will draw up a general strategy for the management of a proposed heritage project and create a viable project design.

Assessment form: 7-point grading scale

Heritage management in practice 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
This course provides students with the chance to focus on and carry out in-depth study of selected themes and sub-areas within heritage practice. The course, therefore, targets specific areas of application within the broad field of heritage and gives the students the opportunity to direct their degree programme towards areas in which they are particularly interested.
The specific content of the course may vary from one year to the next, and the range of options can contain both primarily theoretical courses and specialised introductions to specific academic methods.
In all cases the specific content of the course is placed in close connection to and dialogue with the programme’s central body of theory, practice, analysis and methodology within sustainable heritage practice.

3.2 Regulations for commencement of studies exam

The compulsory commencement of studies exam will be held before the end of September on the first semester of the Master’s degree programme.

The exam generates 0 ECTS credits and is a pass/fail internal examination with no co-examiner. Two attempts to pass this exam are allowed. First-semester students will be registered for the exam automatically. The result will be posted in the student’s self-service system. Students who do not pass their first examination attempt will be registered automatically for their second attempt.

The result of the second attempt will be posted in the student’s self-service system no later than 28 September. Students who fail both their first and second attempt will be withdrawn from their degree programme with immediate effect and without further warning.


3.3 Credit and flexibility

Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document the results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements.

Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad.

When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.


3.4 Registration and withdrawal

Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.

Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.

Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students cannot withdraw from the Master’s thesis.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.

Students can choose between the specified forms of exam for their Master’s thesis.


3.5 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of Master's thesis and other major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to the student's command of the content knowledge. 

In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.6 Regulations for assignments

Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.7 Using computers for examinations

Most written exams at AU are taken in a digital exams system. More information about digital exams may be found on the study portal. See also Aarhus University’s rules and regulations on on-site examinations Collection of internal rules for students at Aarhus University (au.dk)

3.8 Project-oriented course

Options for project-oriented courses are stated in the description of the individual study element. It is possible to take project placement with more than one project host if it is deemed to live up to all requirements for project placement.

3.9 Exemption

An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the board of studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the board of studies forwards the application to the appropriate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and must be submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal.

3.10 Appeals and complaints

Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order.

More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.


3.11 Examinations

Calculation of average grade point
It is possible to get the weighted average grade point calculated and formally verified. The weighted average grade point is an expression of the average grade point calculated in relation to the ECTS weighting of the grade points. The average grade point is calculated to one decimal point based on all graded exams. The average grade is not calculated in relation to the grades of fellow students, and that the calculation of the average mark is not offered until the programme's last exam has been passed.
