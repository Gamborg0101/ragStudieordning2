1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The Master's Degree Programme in European Studies is a multidisciplinary programme focusing on political, social, and cultural developments in contemporary Europe, on processes of European integration and disintegration, and on European interactions with the rest of the world. The aim of the programme is to provide students with insight into themes of key European significance and with relevant academic tools to analyse these. The international profile of the programme allows students to develop strong intercultural competences.
The Master's Degree Programme in European Studies prepares students for work in institutions, organisations, NGO’s, and companies with a European or international profile, EU institutions, embassies, media, etc. in the public or private sector.


1.2 Learning outcomes

Students acquire the following knowledge, skills and competences during the Master's Degree Programme in European Studies:

Knowledge:

  • A comprehensive knowledge of theories of European integration and of political institutions and policies of the European Union
  • Knowledge of central theories, concepts, and methodologies applied in cultural and political analysis
  • Advanced knowledge of conceptions of European identities and identity politics
  • Knowledge of qualitative methods and their application
  • An advanced understanding of the cultural and political contexts of European integration
  • The ability to reflect analytically and critically on Europe’s position in the world
  • The ability to identify key contemporary issues related to the understanding and managing of European borders and border policies.

Skills:

  • The ability to analyse and critically reflect on the dynamics of the European Union and on external and internal challenges to integration
  • The ability to apply qualitative methods to collect, organise, and analyse relevant data
  • The ability to assess, choose, and combine theories of culture and of identity with theories of European integration
  • The ability to independently analyse cultural and political issues in Europe by using relevant theories from European integration studies, political science, anthropology, history and cultural studies
  • The ability to analyse manifestations of political mobilisations of cultural, ethnic, and other identities in Europe, and to communicate about these issues in a precise and adequate way to both specialists and non-specialists.

Competences:

  • The ability to manage interdisciplinarity by combining, comparing and employing relevant knowledge from different disciplines
  • The ability to use intercultural competences to collaborate productively and responsibly in an international and intercultural environment
  • The ability to operate in an international environment with English as the language of communication
  • The ability to organise and manage projects in an international setting
  • The ability to independently take responsibility for their own academic and professional development and specialisation.

1.3 Admission requirements and prerequisites

Direct admission
Certain Bachelor's Degree Programmes from Aarhus University and various other Danish Universities have been determined to meet the admission requirements to this Master's Degree Programme in European Studies: 

  • A Bachelor’s Degree Programme within the Humanities
  • A Bachelor’s Degree Programme within Social Sciences

International and other qualifications
If you have an international educational background, you must meet both the general admission requirement and the specific admission requirements: 

General admission requirements
You must have a Bachelor's Degree Programme or equivalent that is comparable to a Danish Bachelor's Degree Programme in both level and duration (180 ECTS). 

For more information about how your Bachelor's Degree Programme is assessed, please see the national database. 

Specific admission requirements
If you meet the general admission requirement, the university continue on to assess whether or not you meet the specific admission requirements.

You can be admitted to the Master's Degree Programme if the university assesses that your education has a level, scope, and content that corresponds to the academic requirements specified below.

Subject area ECTS
Cultural, Historical or Social subjects/areas  100

We cannot assess in advance whether your specific degree will meet the above requirements. Therefore, we recommend that you apply for the degree programme if you believe that you meet the academic requirements. In this regard, it is a good idea to compare your degree programme with the degree programmes that provide direct admission.

Language requirements
English at B level at upper-secondary school or the equivalent is a requirement for admission: read about English language requirements.


1.4 Students with a legal right of admission

No Bachelor's Degree Programme(s) from Aarhus University entitles applicants to an offer of admission to the Master's Degree Programme in European Studies.

The Master's Degree Programme in European Studies provides access to the PhD degree programme in accordance with current regulations.

European Identities 10 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students to (A) understand and discuss theories and concepts of collective identity and collective identification; (B) use these theories and concepts to analyse various forms of collective identities in Europe, and to reflect on the way such identities are constructed, contested and negotiated within Europe and in relation to European integrating processes; and (C) construct and communicate academic analyses within areas where questions of identity are at stake.

The course covers issues of identity relevant to Europe and European integration processes. The course provides an introduction to theories and conceptualisations of identity and identity construction from anthropology, cultural studies, cultural sociology and history. It deals with issues of identity in various contexts, e.g. EU institutions, European integration processes, political mobilisation of social, cultural and ideological communities and conflicts in and in relation to Europe.

The course provides a foundational theoretical and conceptual understanding that students may use in later courses at European Studies, e.g. in the course on European Borders and the course of Europe in the World. The course allows students to develop knowledge about social and cultural aspects of integration processes in Europe and is thus complementary to the course in EU institutions.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- evaluate relevant theories and concepts of collective identity from anthropology, cultural studies, cultural sociology and history
- examine different ways to conceptualise and analyse collective identity and identity politics.

Skills:
- analyse particular issues of identity relevant in a European context
- apply relevant theories about collective identities and identification in the analysis of socially, culturally and/or politically contested issues
- present and discuss issues of collective identity in a European context
- select and include relevant literature/teaching materials from the syllabus.

Competences:
- critically reflect on the way collective identities are constructed, contested and negotiated in European contexts.

Political Institutions of the EU 10 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of this course is to provide the students with comprehensive knowledge about the political institutions of the European Union. The course will enable students to reflect critically upon these institutions and provide students with an understanding of the policies they conduct.

Furthermore, the course provides students with a solid understanding of the history, the functioning and selected policies of the political institutions. Through the reading of academic literature and through participation in course activities, students will develop a thorough knowledge of the European Commission, the Council, the European Parliament and the Court of Justice of the European Union as well as skills in analysing the historical foundations and policies of these institutions.

The course introduces the students to classical and current theories of European integration. It investigates the historical foundations and the present functioning of the political institutions.  Cases will be selected to allow the students to apply theories in the analysis of the political institutions and policies.

The course is foundational and it provides students with knowledge and skills about political institutions and policies of the European Union in relation to the three courses at the second semester (Europe in the World, The Borders of Europe and EU Flashpoints). It is complementary to the course in European Identities.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- account for theories of European integration
- describe and discuss political institutions of the European Union
- reflect on relevant concepts and theories in relation to the political institutions of the European Union.

Skills:
- analyse historical foundations, political institutions, and selected policies of the European Union
- evaluate and discuss the impact of European Union policies on a national and supranational level
- communicate topics and analyses of the political institutions of the European Union orally
- select and include relevant literature/teaching materials from the syllabus.

Competences:
- contribute to academic problem solving regarding the political institutions and policies of the European Union
- combine different theoretical fields to address questions concerning political institutions of the European Union.

Exam details See courses

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable the student to (A) understand and work practically with qualitative research methods, (B) use qualitative methods to collect, manage and analyse qualitative data and (C) choose the relevant qualitative methods in particular contexts.

The course provides an introduction to qualitative research design and to relevant qualitative methods for collecting, managing and analysing qualitative data. Through the study of introductory literature to qualitative methodology and through careful reading of case studies using qualitative methods, the course will provide students with a thorough understanding of the different possibilities for analysis that qualitative methods provide. The course also provides the students with practical skills in selecting qualitative methods and with an understanding of ethical and legal issues in qualitative research. During the course students are introduced to software which can help facilitate data collection and data analysis.

The course is the tool box for the degree programme, providing students with methodological skills to be applied in the other courses of the degree programme.

Europe in the World 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students to understand and analyse entanglements between Europe and regions in other parts of the world based on historical and current knowledge, as well as on theories of external relations and reciprocal representations.

The course covers issues of external relations and reciprocal representations between Europe and other regions in the world. The course investigates different kinds of external relations between institutions and other actors in Europe on the one hand, and institutions and other actors in other regions of the world on the other. In the course, students will analyse European relations to a variety of actors outside Europe and study the cultural representations at play in these relations. This entails studying European representations of other regions as well as representations of Europe as they are articulated in other parts of the world based on data from a variety of European and non-European media.

The course gives the students basic insights into how Europe acts in the world vis-à-vis actors from other regions, and how reciprocal representations are forming these interactions. It trains the students in using a variety of media sources. The course will draw from the students’ skills in qualitative methods, their knowledge about EU’s political institutions and their familiarity with theories of identity.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe and explain Europe’s entanglements with other parts of the world
- reflect on Europe’s external relations and the cultural representations of Europe in European and non-European media based on theories of Europe’s external relations and cultural representations. 

Skills:
- apply the relevant theories to discuss perceptions of the EU’s external relations
- collect, analyse and critically reflect on cultural representations of Europe and of other parts of the world
- use qualitative methods to manage and analyse data
- select and include relevant literature/teaching materials from the syllabus.

Competences:
- independently use a variety of media sources, including digital databases, to collect relevant information
- work interculturally in different international and interregional contexts.

The Borders of Europe and the EU 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students to understand and explain how polity borders in and around Europe are produced, legitimised, maintained, or challenged in discourse and social/political/legal practice. Students will also learn how European bordering affects local, regional, national, or transnational communities and actors. This will enable students to apply interdisciplinary methods as well as theories and concepts from border studies to the analysis of phenomena related to European bordering.

The course provides a historically contextualised introduction to central EU policies regulating internal and external EU borders and cross-border migration. Selected case studies from across Europe demonstrate the complex impacts of bordering while simultaneously introducing to methods and approaches used in the field of border studies.

The study of European borders and bordering enables students to apply the knowledge, theoretical insights, and methods acquired in the first semester courses on the institutional structure and functioning of the EU, on European identities, and on qualitative research methods. The take-home assignment gives the students valuable academic writing practice for their MA thesis and for later employment.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- account for central theories and concepts pertaining to the development and functioning of polity borders
- present relevant aspects of EU and Member State rules and policies regulating borders and cross-border migration
- explain the impact of bordering on individuals and/or various types of communities.

Skills:
- apply relevant theoretical, methodological, and empirical insights in the analysis of border related issues in and around Europe
- design and present a study of an issue related to European bordering
- select and include relevant literature/teaching materials from the syllabus

Competences:
- select and combine relevant methods to investigate and analyse complex, multi-scalar phenomena
- address often controversial issues involving a large range of stakeholders.

EU Flashpoints 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students to analyse burning issues or challenges that the EU is currently facing. This will make students able to understand the potential conflicts, to evaluate the policies launched, and to assess the extent to which the situation is constituted as a crisis by political actors, the media, or actors in civil society.

The course can deal with the topics such as the 'refugee crisis', 'the Euro crisis', international conflicts, terrorism, climate change and states leaving the EU and will also treat how crises situations are co-created by the media and discursively constructed in public communication.

The course draws on the knowledge and skills gained by students in the first semester, allowing them to apply qualitative research methods and to extend their understanding of European institutions and notions of European identity.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- account for relevant conceptualisations of the issues investigated
- discuss the interplay of material and discursive elements in the constitution of an international crisis.

Skills:
- analyse the discursive, geopolitical and institutional impact of large-scale challenges in or to the EU
- assess EU policy responses and responses from other actors
- present ideas and findings to an academic audience
- select and include relevant literature/teaching materials from the syllabus.

Competences:
- seek and compile data of international issues from political, media or cultural sources
- collaborate across disciplines to collect and manage crisis data and reporting.

The objective of the international electiv e courses is to expand the degree programme’s academic scope at a university outside Denmark. Students must be able to participate actively in an academic educational environment outside Denmark and take responsibility for their own academic development in an unfamiliar educational environment.

Courses are subject to the advance approval of the board of studies based on the description of objectives above.

Students choose between the profile courses offered. Project Placement 30 ECTS 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the project placement is: A) to provide students with insight in the tasks at places of work relevant for graduates in European Studies and in the chosen topic; B) to acquire skills in identifying, combining, and applying key concepts, theories and methodologies of the core disciplines of the programme in a practical context; C) to learn how to contribute actively to the solving of tasks in the host institution. 

During the project placement of 3-4 months in duration at a company/organisation, the student must conduct an independent study that is academically relevant for European studies while also linked to the host institution. Next to this, the student is expected to contribute actively to the solving of relevant tasks defined in collaboration with host institution. Finally, in dialogue with the academic supervisor of the project placement, the student must initiate the process of defining an MA thesis topic and research question, including considerations of the theories and methods to be applied in answering the question. The student must also develop a plan for how to complete the thesis in time.

Combined, the contribution to the host institution, the writing of the study, and the preparatory reflections on the master’s thesis will require approximately 820 hours of work.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- increase their awareness and practical experience of how their academic qualifications can contribute to the solving of tasks in a relevant institutional environment
- account for the chosen topic and approach and its relationship to European studies.  

Skills:
- identify and apply academic knowledge, theories, and methods in a work-related setting and in the addressing of the chosen topic
- evaluate how their academic contribution relates to the activities and aims of their host institution
- apply relevant theories, concepts and methods in the concrete analysis.

Competences:
- independently and professionally plan their work process
- work in a result-oriented way while drawing on all relevant aspects of their academic training.

3.2 Regulations for commencement of studies exam

The compulsory commencement of studies exam will be held before the end of September on the first semester of the Master’s degree programme.
The exam generates 0 ECTS credits and is a pass/fail internal examination with no co-examiner. Two attempts to pass this exam are allowed. First-semester students will be registered for the exam automatically. The result will be posted in the student’s self-service system (mit.au.dk). Students who do not pass their first examination attempt will be registered automatically for their second attempt.
The result of the second attempt will be posted in the student’s self-service system (mit.au.dk) no later than 28 September. Students who fail both their first and second attempt will be withdrawn from their degree programme with immediate effect and without further warning.

3.3 Credit and flexibility

Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document the results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements.

Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad.

When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.


3.4 Registration and withdrawal

Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.

Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.

Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students cannot withdraw from the Master’s thesis.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.
Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.

If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled (before the deadline for registering for exams, as stated on the study portal) to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

Students can choose between the specified forms of exam for their Master’s thesis.


3.5 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of Master's thesis and other major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to the student's command of the content knowledge. 

In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.6 Regulations for assignments

Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.7 Using computers for examinations

Most written exams at AU are taken in a digital exams system. More information about digital exams may be found on the study portal. See also Aarhus University’s rules and regulations on on-site examinations Collection of internal rules for students at Aarhus University (au.dk)

3.8 Project-oriented course

Options for project-oriented courses are stated in the description of the individual study element. It is possible to take project placement with more than one project host if it is deemed to live up to all requirements for project placement.

3.9 Exemption

An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the board of studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the board of studies forwards the application to the appropriate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and must be submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal.

3.10 Appeals and complaints

Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order.

More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.


3.11 Examinations

Calculation of average grade point
It is possible to get the weighted average grade point calculated and formally verified. The weighted average grade point is an expression of the average grade point calculated in relation to the ECTS weighting of the grade points. The average grade point is calculated to one decimal point based on all graded exams. The average grade is not calculated in relation to the grades of fellow students, and that the calculation of the average mark is not offered until the programme's last exam has been passed.
