1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The purpose of the Bachelor’s Supplementary Subject in Sociology is to introduce students to humanistic as a discipline within the social sciences. This includes an introduction to the central theories, methods and subject areas of sociology. Students will acquire academic and theoretical knowledge and specific methodological and analytical skills enabling them to identify, examine and analyse current issues of relevance to society based on the academic field of sociology and their own core subject.

The Bachelor’s Supplementary Subject in Sociology includes courses which introduce students to both classical and modern sociological theory and analysis ("Introduction to Sociological Theory and Analysis of the Contemporary", 15 ECTS), provide students with knowledge of relevant political and economic aspects from the perspective of sociology ("Political and Economic Sociology", 10 ECTS) and provide insight into sociological methods as well as practical experience of specific studies on current issues ("Sociological Methods and Analysis in Practice", 20 ECTS).

Once they have completed the supplementary subject, students will be qualified to work in various fields in both the public and private sector, for instance analysis and consultancy, administration and organisation, politics and development, research and evaluation as well as education and teaching.

1.2 Learning outcomes

Students acquire the following knowledge, skills and competences during the Bachelor’s Supplementary Subject in Sociology:

Knowledge:

  • Knowledge of the history and development of sociology including knowledge of both classical and modern sociological theories and their current relevance
  • Insight into different sociological methods – both qualitative and quantitative – and how they can be used in practice
  • Knowledge of key sociological themes and issues, particularly within the field of political and economic sociology
  • The ability to independently identify and explain key sociological concepts and issues
  • Knowledge of the practical uses of sociology and key areas of employment for sociologists.

Skills:

  • The ability to apply several different sociological methods and forms of analysis to specific and current issues
  • The ability to critically reflect on, use and assess sociological theories, methods and forms of analysis
  • The ability to examine, analyse and discuss key sociological themes and issues with a focus on social complexity and social processes
  • The ability to independently and systematically collect, process and assess empirical data using relevant methods in order to carry out a sociological analysis of a current issue
  • The ability to combine theory and empirical data when dealing with a sociological topic
  • The ability to communicate sociological work both orally and in writing and to convert knowledge into understanding and practice, for instance by providing sociological insights to deal with significant issues.

Competences:

  • The ability to assess the strengths and weaknesses of sociological studies and their methodological, analytical and theoretical foundation
  • The ability to identify and present an issue, organise a study, collect data, analyse and present results, and present any relevant recommendations based on sociological considerations
  • The ability to work methodologically, analytically, theoretically and critically as well as the ability to match the approach used with the nature of the field and issue in question
  • The ability to work independently with defined sociological issues
  • The ability to engage in cross-disciplinary collaboration, including the ability to combine their own core competences with the competences of other people
  • The ability to plan and structure their own learning and experience in different learning processes and contexts.

Introduction to Sociological Theory and Analysis of the Contemporary 15 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to introduce students to classical and modern sociology. Students learn to account for the main theoretical and empirical contributions to the development of sociological theory. The course also aims to develop the students' understanding of how classical and modern sociological theory can be used in current sociological analyses, thereby preparing the students to analyse current phenomena using sociological theory.

The course introduces the main areas of sociological theory from classical to modern and contemporary sociology. Key sociologists such as Karl Marx, Max Weber, Émile Durkheim, Michel Foucault, Pierre Bourdieu and Judith Butler are introduced. The course also focuses on complementing the theory with current research literature in order to show how sociological theories are used in recent sociological research. In other words, the course also aims to increase the students' methodological awareness.

The course provides a basic introduction to sociological theory, thereby informing the students of the development of sociology and its ongoing debate with its own traditions. This enables the students to gain a clear overview of the field of sociology, enabling them to identify important positions and carry out preliminary independent analyses using sociological theory. In this way, the course gives the students a solid grounding within sociology which enables them to work with specific sub-areas of sociology in the course entitled "Political and Economic Sociology". The course also constitutes a theoretical foundation for further study of in-depth, empirical sociological methods and project work in the course entitled "Sociological Methods and Analysis in Practice".

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the students’ performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the students are able to:

Knowledge:
- describe classical and modern sociological theories, including their development
- explain the most central contributions to sociology including their differences, similarities and connections.

Skills:
- analyse current issues using sociological theories
- reflect on the applicability, strengths and weaknesses of different methods of analysis
- compare and discuss sociological theories.

Competences:
- work independently with sociological issues and theories
- produce delimited written studies
- reflect on their own core subject from a sociological perspective.

Political and Economic Sociology 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to provide students with a fundamental knowledge of important sociological theories and approaches in order to analyse current political and economic conditions. Political and economic sociology deals with the relationship between individual and structure as well as social conditions and their impact on subject processes both individually and collectively. The course introduces students to a number of current issues of a political and economic nature, as well as presenting sociological approaches to the analysis of these issues. The course enables students to carry out independent analyses of current sociological issues with a particular focus on political and economic aspects.

Students are introduced to important societal conditions and issues. This may include social conflicts, political movements, finance, crisis, debt, work, consumption, integration, management and organisations. The courses focuses a great deal on working with different forms of empirical data as well as recent research literature.

The course teaches the students how to analyse and reflect on society at a local, national, regional and global level as well as considering the role of individuals or groups in society. The course builds on "Introduction to Sociological Theory and Analysis of the Contemporary", with a particular focus on the political and economic aspects of social and contemporary analysis. The course supplements "Sociological Methods and Analysis in Practice" by introducing methodologically strong social analyses which serve as illustrations and examples of good analytical practice.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the students' performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the students are able to:

Knowledge:
- describe sociological theories about economic and political conditions
- account for important political and economic topics and their sociological analysis.

Skills:
- analyse current economic and political social conditions using sociological theories
- present analyses and results orally in a clear manner.

Competences:
- independently analyse and discuss economic and/or political sociological issues
- reflect orally on their own analytical progression and practice.

Forms of instruction:

  • Classroom instruction

Sociological Methods and Analysis in Practice 20 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to give students an understanding of a wide range of sociological methods as well as practical experience of the independent collection of data and the sociological analysis of specific issues. Students carry out a study which is developed in collaboration with external partners to the extent possible.

The course introduces students to fundamental sociological methodology focusing on both quantitative and qualitative methods. Students are introduced to various sociological methods and key methodological considerations enabling them to prepare, carry out, understand and assess sociological studies. Based on this foundation, students work individually or in groups with a specific, delimited issue which they examine using sociological methods in practice. The empirical data of the study forms the basis of a sociological analysis which sheds light on and provides insight into the issue in question. It should be possible to use this analysis as the basis of decisions and recommendations. It is possible to involve external partners from the public and/or private sector (organisations, companies, associations etc.) for whom the issue is relevant, and to whom the analyses and results can be presented. At the beginning of the semester, the teacher sets out the specific criteria for the project work.

The course provides students with an understanding of and skills in the appropriate and constructive use of sociological methods and analyses when dealing with specific issues. The students practise important competences such as defining problem statements, selecting methods, collecting data, analysing, presenting results and addressing ethical dilemmas and demands for usefulness and relevance.

Thanks to its focus on independent data collection and the sociological analysis of this data, the course adds to the other theoretical, analytical and thematic course elements of the supplementary subject. This gives students the opportunity to include classical and modern sociological theory from the course entitled "Introduction to Sociological Theory and Analysis of the Contemporary", and to draw on forms of analysis and thematic knowledge from the course entitled "Political and Economic Sociology". The course also encourages students to work together in cross-disciplinary groups in which they can use their own core competences.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the students' performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the students are able to:

Knowledge:
- explain and assess the relevance of different methods in relation to specific issues
- identify and assess methodological, practical and ethical aspects relating to the practical use of sociological methods and analyses.

Skills:
- apply different sociological methods to specific and current issues
- develop and carry out a sociological analysis which provides insight into a given issue, and which may form the basis for decisions in an organisation or result in relevant and well-founded recommendations
- reflect on and discuss key sociological issues of an ethical, methodological and analytical nature.

Competences:
- develop, organise and carry out a delimited sociological study of an issue which is relevant for external parties
- reflect on and assess the actual and potential role of sociology when studying and dealing with current issues.

Forms of instruction:

  • Classroom instruction
  • Supervision

Comments on form of instruction:

The teaching is distributed evenly across the semester.

Language of teaching:
Rules regarding the language of exams and teaching are set out in Section 2.1 of the academic regulations.

Exam language:

  • English

Exam options:

   Take-home assignment on topic of student's choice

Form of co-examination: External co-examination

Assessment form: 7-point grading scale

Comments:

Ordinary exam and re-examination:
The exam is a take-home assignment on a topic of the student's choice. The assignment may be written individually or in groups of up to three students. Group assignments must be written in such a way that the contribution of each student, except for the introduction, problem statement and conclusion, can form the basis of individual assessment. The assignment should clearly state which student is responsible for which section.

Length for one student: Max. 15 standard pages
Length for two students: Max. 25 standard pages

The topic and method used in the assignment must be relevant in relation to the content of the course.

The take-home assignment on a topic of the student's choice must be handed in for assessment in WISEflow by the date specified in the exam plan.

3.2 Curriculum for The Supplementary Subject

3.2.1 Credit and flexibility
Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document his or her results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements. Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.

3.2.2 Registration and withdrawal
Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.

Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.

Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.
Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.

If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Deadlines are stated on the study portal. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

3.2.3 Spelling and fluency
In the assessment of major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to his/her command of the content knowledge.

In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.2.4 Definition of standard page
Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.2.5 Using computers for examinations
Most written exams at AU are taken in a digital exams system. More information about digital exams may be found on the study portal. See also Aarhus University’s rules and regulations on the use of computers at exams http://www.au.dk/fileadmin/www.au.dk/Regelsamlingen/2016/REGLER_OM_BRUG_AF_COMPUTER_VED_PROEVER_2016.pdf

3.2.6 Exemption
An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the Board of Studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the Board of Studies forwards the application to the appro-priate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal portal.

3.2.7 Appeals and complaints
Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing and must account for the student’s reasons for lodging the complaint. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order. More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.

3.2.8 Exchange
A degree programme may include study abroad. More information is available on the study portal.
