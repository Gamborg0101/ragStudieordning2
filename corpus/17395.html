1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The Master Degree Programme in Curating is a research-based master degree programme. It is designed for practitioners and professionals in the fields of curating, cultural programming, education and communication, who wish to develop their interdisciplinary understanding of curating in contemporary society and their own position within the field.

The degree programme draws on the extensive professional experience among the enrolled students and focuses on the close relationship between practice and theory, doing and thinking, and experience and reflection specific to the curator’s expanded operations across disciplines and knowledge fields.

Throughout the degree programme, students are introduced to theories, concepts and methodologies that enable them to analyse and evaluate curatorial practices and projects and to self-reflectively develop their own curatorial practice. This will enable them to use academic tools and activate research-based knowledge in relation to their own curatorial work. The degree programme is furthermore organised in a way that allows students to pursue individual learning and research interests alongside the shared curriculum and course work.

The degree programme draws on the expertise of scholars across a number of departments at Aarhus University and collaborates with art and cultural institutions as well as visiting curators and scholars within the interdisciplinary field of curating. During the course of the degree programme, students will have the opportunity to engage with peers, both scholars and professionals, within the expanded field of curating.

The degree programme enables students to operate within and across a range of institutions, platforms and organisations that organise, present and discuss exhibitions and other curated activities, for example museums, art centres, galleries, biennials, festivals, online platforms, cultural programming at large, publishing and art education as well as organisations, private enterprises and self-organised initiatives within the cultural field.

The Master Degree Programme in Curating is a two-year, part-time, low-residency, international and interdisciplinary degree programme, and is part of the continuing and further education scheme at Aarhus University.


1.2 Learning outcomes

The Master Degree Programme in Curating provides students with the following knowledge, skills and competences:

Knowledge:

  • Knowledge and understanding of practice-based perspectives on curating
  • In-depth knowledge of formative discussions within the interdisciplinary field of curating
  • Understanding of methodologies, concepts and theories that enable critical and self-reflective analysis within the expanded field of curating
  • The capacity to identify and reflect on academic processes of knowledge production in relation to the contexts in which they unfold.

Skills:

  • The ability to articulate, situate and reflect on their own curatorial practice or experience in relation to the field of curating
  • The ability to employ academic tools and research-based knowledge in relation to curatorial work
  • The ability to analyse and evaluate curatorial practices and projects in their institutional and cultural contexts
  • The ability to communicate issues related to curating responding to the context, receivers and mediums in question.

Competences:

  • The competence to self-reflectively analyse, contribute to and manage complex curatorial processes in various work situations
  • The competence to activate and develop their own educational and professional background in relation to the field of study
  • The competence to initiate, organise and actualise interdisciplinary processes and collaborations by combining practical and academic knowledge
  • The competence to identify and reflect on asymmetries in collaborative undertakings in order to make informed decisions
  • The competence to independently identify relevant learning needs in order to further advance their own academic and curatorial development.

1.3 Admission requirements and prerequisites

Admission to the degree programme requires applicants to have completed at least one of the following degree programmes:

  • A relevant Bachelor’s or Master’s degree programme within the arts and humanities or social sciences, e.g. cultural studies, art history, aesthetics, philosophy, media studies, architecture, design, fine arts, performing arts, anthropology or sociology
  • A relevant professional or vocational degree programme, e.g. in fine arts, teaching, journalism or communication or
  • A relevant diploma degree programme, e.g. in communication.

It is also a requirement that the applicants have at least a B level in English.

The university may admit applicants who do not fulfil the conditions in section 1.3, but who, on the basis of a specific academic assessment, are deemed to have comparable educational qualifications.


1.4 Admission requirements (regarding business experience)

Admission to the degree programme requires applicants to have at least two years of relevant work experience after completing the degree programme on which admission is based.

Relevant occupations include but are not limited to:

  • Staff at cultural institutions and organisations, e.g. museums and galleries, archives and libraries (across exhibition, education and administration aspects)
  • Arts and cultural workers e.g., curators and programmers, also freelance and self-organised
  • Managers and coordinators (e.g., in connection with festivals and events)
  • Communication specialists in the cultural field
  • Educators (e.g., high school, higher education)
  • Artists
  • Designers and architects.

No exemption will be granted on the basis of work experience.

Prior to admission, the university reserves the right to carry out a compulsory supervisory interview with applicants.


1.5 Time frame for the completion of the degree programme

The degree programme must be completed within six years of the enrolment. The Master Thesis concludes the degree programme. Students must complete each single module no later than two years after having participated in the teaching in the single module. The period is calculated at the end of the semester, including the re-examination period.

Module 1

15 ECTS
Curatorial Practice 15 ECTS Semester 1 Passed /failed
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students to understand and critically engage with curatorial practices, including the interdisciplinary roles, the multidisciplinary contexts and the outcomes of the work of the contemporary curator. Students will furthermore be able to articulate, situate and reflect on their own positions in relation to the expanded field of curating.

The course provides an introduction to the role of the curator and situates the work of the curator within relevant institutions and platforms. It furthermore contextualises curating historically, socially and culturally.

Drawing on the students’ disciplinary and interdisciplinary experiences and backgrounds, the course engages with the various methods, formats and capacities involved in curating as well as critical analysis and evaluation of these activities. The course also introduces tools and exercises that enable students to articulate their own position and conduct minor academic analyses.

The course establishes a shared foundation for the degree programme. It enables students to engage critically with curatorial practices and projects and to make their own experiences the object of critical reflexion. These skills will support the students’ work throughout the degree programme and allow them to understand their own work in relation to a number of different institutions and contexts.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- present and analyse curatorial practices and engage critically with them
- articulate and reflect on the use of practice-based curatorial methodologies and qualitative methods
- relate academic considerations to historical, cultural and social contexts of curating.

Skills:
- analyse and evaluate curatorial practices and projects in their institutional and cultural contexts
- articulate, situate and reflect on their own professional experience in relation to the interdisciplinary field of curating
- employ relevant tools and perspectives in the analysis of curatorial projects and practices.

Competences
- identify their own professional position and competences in relation to the field of curating and determine their own learning needs and interests
- engage in and contribute to different types of interdisciplinary learning processes
- give and receive constructive and reflective feedback and critique.

Module 2

15 ECTS
Curatorial Theory 15 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to make students familiar with theoretical positions and methodological perspectives crucial to the understanding of curating as an interdisciplinary field of study. The course enables students to analyse and discuss key theoretical issues and concepts related to curating and to construct and carry out an independent academic inquiry.

The course introduces the students to significant theoretical formations related to the discourse on curating. It furthermore introduces the students to interdisciplinary perspectives and methodologies that substantiate curating as a multi-faceted knowledge field. The interface between theoretical perspectives and curatorial practices and projects is of particular significance to the course. During the course, students acquire the knowledge and skills necessary to conduct an academic inquiry and are supported in their efforts to do so.

The course draws on the knowledge acquired during the Curatorial Practice course and builds a theoretical framework. It provides students with theoretical knowledge about curating and the skills necessary to put this knowledge to work. The course is relevant for students who wish to understand and be able to navigate contemporary, interdisciplinary discourses on curating and to conduct independent investigations within this field.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe and present theories, concepts and methodologies relevant to the interdisciplinary field of curating.

Skills:
- select and evaluate relevant theories and methodologies in relation to an academic analysis
- analyse and discuss key issues and concepts in relation to an object of inquiry
- identify and combine topical information in writing aimed at a community of peers.

Competences:
- identify own academic learning needs based on educational and professional background
- organise a research and learning process in relation to an investigation into a particular topic or question.

Module 3

15 ECTS
Curatorial Knowledge Production and Dissemination 15 ECTS Semester 3 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The course provides students with the ability to understand and position themselves as knowledge producers within the field of curating. Students learn to produce curatorial knowledge by combining practical and theoretical knowledge, for example by working with concepts, and will furthermore be able to publicly present and discuss their findings.

The course introduces perspectives on curating as the production of and engagement with knowledge across practical and theoretical perspectives. It draws on current discussions within an interdisciplinary field of curating and engages with the discursive tendency and complex questioning taking place in this field.

The course furthermore introduces students to formats of academic knowledge production, including the symposium. During the course, students will partake in the organisation of a public, international symposium, which will serve as the framework of their oral presentations.

Drawing on the acquired knowledge and skills from the previous courses, this course enables students to produce and discuss curatorial knowledge. Students gain an in-depth understanding of their own role as knowledge producers and will furthermore be able to communicate their own research positions and inquiries, and test these perspectives in the context of an international community of peers.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- account for current perspectives and discussions within the context of curatorial knowledge production
- critically analyse and reflect on modes and conditions of curatorial knowledge production.

Skills:
- construct an academic inquiry by combining relevant theoretical perspectives, methodological considerations and practical experience
- critically and constructively engage in a public discussion within a community of peers
- present and discuss questions related to the interdisciplinary field of curating.

Competences:
- enter into process-oriented collaboration with other professionals in relation to curating as an act of “making public”
- assume professional responsibility in the context of a public event
- identify and reflect on their own production of curatorial knowledge
- modify a presentation according to the context in which it takes place.

Module 4

15 ECTS
Master Thesis 15 ECTS Semester 4 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students to self-reflectively conduct a significant academic inquiry into an aspect of their own curatorial practice and experience. During this process, students acquire in-depth knowledge of their chosen subject matter, they further develop their ability to employ theories and methods in academic investigations, and they consolidate their curatorial interests.

The course allows students to focus their attention on an academic inquiry based on a self-chosen aspect of their own curatorial practice, for example a curatorial project. The course supports the students’ efforts to construct a research question, outline their inquiry and structure their research process, and it furthermore facilitates individual and collective feedback processes and supervision.

In the Master Thesis, students are expected to draw on knowledge, skills and competences obtained during the previous modules as well as acquiring in-depth knowledge relevant to their particular investigation.

Depending on the students’ research interests, the inquiry can focus on practical aspects or pursue theoretical perspectives. Either way, questions related to the students’ own curatorial practices or projects should be the principal concern of the inquiry.

The course enables students to identify and employ relevant tools and to operate in a structured, research-based and analytical manner within a deadline. Drawing on the three previous courses, students further develop their ability to bridge practice-based questions and theoretical, methodological and conceptual considerations and to articulate these perspectives to their peers. Moreover students enhance their ability to operate self-reflectively and understand their own position in relation to various institutions, contexts and discourses.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- acquire and analyse in-depth knowledge about a self-chosen research topic
- reflect on and employ in-depth knowledge about methodologies, concepts and theories relevant to the chosen research question.

Skills:
- independently construct a research question related to their own curatorial practice or projects
- combine theoretical, methodological and analytical perspectives in an academic inquiry
- situate, analyse and discuss an aspect of their own curatorial practice or projects in relation to an interdisciplinary field of curating
- define, plan and realise a significant academic process.

Competences:
- analyse and reflect on their own curatorial practice
- identify relevant learning needs and assume responsibility for their own academic development and specialisation
- operate self-reflectively and knowledge-based within the interdisciplinary field of curating.

3.3 Registration and withdrawal

Registering for compulsory courses, exams and elective courses
Students who have been admitted to an entire degree programme will be registered automatically for teaching and exams in compulsory subjects in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students who have been admitted to a single module will be registered automatically for teaching and exams in the module concerned. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and exams. Students are responsible for applying for and (if necessary) listing their priorities for elective courses. This must be done by the registration deadline. After places for elective courses have been allocated, and after the deadline for withdrawal, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mit.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam. Students may not withdraw from exams after the deadline for withdrawal, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as an attempt to pass it.

Examinations
Assessment criteria and concepts used
Development of an 'academic basis':
On completion of each module, students should have gained an academic foundation of knowledge, skills and abilities, and competences within the module concerned. This means that in an examination situation students should be able to provide answers that are critically, systematically, theoretically and empirically well-founded. These criteria may be weighted differently in different modules, depending, for instance, on whether the module has a primarily theoretical or empirical focus.  

  • Critical answers mean that students problematise academic content, (re)defining key issues or hypotheses.
  • Systematic answers mean that students argue for the strengths and weaknesses of the methods used, use clearly defined concepts consistently, make logical arguments and structure the subject content.
  • Theoretically well-founded answers mean that students apply relevant theory in their analyses based on primary literature (possibly in translation).
  • Empirically well-founded answers mean that students include relevant empirical material based on primary studies (either existing or conducted by the students).

Concepts used in module descriptions:

  • Demonstrate comprehensive knowledge and insight: show knowledge of the entire subject area covered by the module (breadth), and be able to identify key issues (depth) in the field.
  • Analyse: be able to define key concepts in a subject area.
  • Assess: be able to substantiate the quality of different statements.
  • Discuss: be able to synthesise elements in a subject area, assess them in relation to one other, and put the results of the assessment into perspective.
  • Define (e.g. practice-related issues, study design or solution proposals): be able to describe and explain how issues within a subject area can be addressed.
  • Communicate: be able to communicate academic expertise about a subject area to a specific target group.
  • Carry out: to be able to perform a task in practice and explain the reasons for performing the task (e.g. a study, an evaluation, supervision, or a teaching plan).

3.4 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of Master's thesis and other major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to the student's command of the content knowledge.

In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.5 Regulations for assignments

To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.7 Exemption

An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the Board of Studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the Board of Studies forwards the application to the appropriate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented.More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal.

3.8 Appeals and complaints

Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order. More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.

3.9 Examinations

Closer regulations regarding how and to what extent the student must have participated in classes in which the type of examination involves such participation are stated in the description of the individual study element. In the assessment of all written examinations, emphasis will be placed on the student's ability to present an academic problem area and manage an academic task, including compliance with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral examinations, emphasis will be placed on the student's ability to present academic material, organise an oral presentation and enter into academically constructive dialogue. The results of all examinations are stated on the diploma, including their ECTS value. An average is calculated with one decimal place of all graded assessments. Each assessment is included in the calculation of the ECTS value. Acts and ministerial orders, as well as the university's rules and regulations relating to education, are available in the electronic rules and regulations of Aarhus University at http://www.au.dk/en/rules/index.html.
Calculation of average grade point
It is possible to get the weighted average grade point calculated and formally verified. The weighted average grade point is an expression of the average grade point calculated in relation to the ECTS weighting of the grade points. The average grade point is calculated to one decimal point based on all graded exams. The average grade is not calculated in relation to the grades of fellow students, and that the calculation of the average mark is not offered until the programme's last exam has been passed.
