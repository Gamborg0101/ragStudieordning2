1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The objective of the Master's Degree Programme in Children´s Literature, Media and Cultural Entrepreneuship is to meet an increased internationally and nationally focus on children's and young people's acquisition of literacy, and thereby contribute to creating competent, knowledgeable and critical citizens in a world where literacy and media skills set the agenda for access to information, entertainment and culture.
The Master's Degree Programme in Children’s Literature, Media and Cultural Entrepreneuship offers the students an integrated, international and coherent programme of study of the history, literary and media theory for children’s literature and young adult literature as well as critical methodologies through which to analyse, evaluate and critique the corpus of the children’s and young adults' literature and media texts.

Students will participate in an international degree that focuses on the interaction between children, young adults and adults in the production and consumption of multimodal texts. The degree promotes this interaction by combining culture, creativity and entrepreneurship, and considers the challenges of integrating multiliteracy into diverse educational contexts and policies.

The students will be able to help solve problems, disseminate knowledge about and take initiative in the production, reception, dissemination and use of children's and youths' texts, cultural products and media in national and international contexts.
Students will obtain skills that enable them to navigate the field of children’s literature, media and culture based on knowledge from literature and media studies as well as childhood history and pedagogical/didactic research in an international perspective.


1.2 Learning outcomes

Students acquire the following knowledge, skills and competences during the Master’s Degree Programme in Children's Literature, Media and Cultural Entrepreneurship:

Knowlegde:

  • Overall understanding of knowledge about the literature, media and culture of children and young people
  • In-depth knowledge of the characteristics of the digital and analogue media of children and young people, as well as analyses of such characteristics
  • Knowledge of the conditions governing the production and reception of the literature and media of children and young people in national and global markets
  • Knowledge of the conditions and characteristics of the lives of children and young people in terms of media, currently and historically
  • Knowledge of theories and methods for examining the literature, media and culture of children and young people.

Skills:

  • The ability to analyse and form, content and media for children and young people with a focus on local, national and global conditions
  • The ability to analyse literature and media of children and young people in cross-medial contexts
  • The ability to use methods and theories at an academic level
  • The ability to analyse, manage and present complex material in the field
  • The ability to work methodically, independently and critically, and to approach an issue from various points of view
  • The ability to communicate knowledge about the media of children and young people in national and international contexts and across academic areas.

Competences: 

  • The ability to design, launch and carry out research-related, investigative projects and development work
  • The ability to create, to manage and and to promote via marketing principals cultural and creative enterprises
  • The ability to cooperate on children’s literature, media and culture in international teams in concentrated project processes
  • The ability to use a special area of expertise in the humanities to interact with other areas in actual national and international work situation
  • The ability to independently take responsibility for their own learning and academic development.

Exam details

Description of qualifications:

Purpose: 
The course aims to develop students’ capacity to critically engage with theoretical and empirical literature in specialist areas related to children’s literature and literacies.
The course covers a range of theoretical approaches to children’s literature and applies these approaches to a selection of texts. It also includes an introduction to approaches and methodologies for empirical research that can be applied to different literacies.
The course provides students with an overview of current thinking and recent developments in their chosen topic within the field, allowing them to build other aspects of the programme on this critical engagement.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate an understanding of the social relevance and potential impact of the identified issues within the area of specialisation
- demonstrate how this informs both their immediate context and the wider community of practice and provides potential for further research.

Skills:
- apply specialist knowledge to analyse areas of specialisation and identify and justify issues for further investigation
- apply skills of critical enquiry in a literature review of the general area of specialization.

Competences:
- carry out an effective secondary literature search
- critically review research on topic
- provide supportive critical feedback to peers.

The course provides the historical core of the degree programme, introducing students to classic children’s texts and the development (historical and current) of literature for children. It is therefore relevant to any student as an introduction to the main field of study.

Cross Boundaries: Children's Literature and Other Media 5 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details

Description of qualifications:

Purpose: 
The course aims to enable students to (A) understand the relations between children’s literature and other media such as children’s theatre, film, comics, and adult literature; (B) examine the ways in which children’s literature is adapted and transformed to other media; (C) distinguish between different media and their specific characteristics; (D) understand the various stages and aspects of adaptation processes, (E) identify the relation between the verbal and the visual in picturebooks; (F) examine the negotiation of child and adult audiences in crossover texts; (G) use these insights to analyse and interpret an exemplary case; and (H) communicate their findings in classroom discussions as well as in academic writing assignments.

Since its inception in the second half of the 18th century, children’s literature has always been multi-medial. Text and images have played an important role. Besides books, there were children’s magazines and theatre plays for children. In the 19th century, children’s books were adapted for theatre, and during the 20th century, they were adapted for film, television and games as well. Today, children divide their time over a great number of different media, among which are books.

This course focuses on the relation between children’s literature and other media. Attention will be paid to the process of adaptation and the various transformations that take place when a written text is adapted to another medium. We will also study the interaction between the verbal and the visual in children’s books, notably, the interplay between text and images in picturebooks and comics.

Furthermore, border traffic and adaptation are key concepts in discussions on the relationship between children’s and adult literature. During the past few decades, the relation between both forms of literature has been negotiated continuously. Therefore, we closely examine the resemblances and differences between the two forms of literature.

The course ties in with the other courses taught in the programme in the first semester. It adopts a practical approach to the trends and evolutions covered by “Texts for Children and Young People” and “CL: Critical Enquiry” and provides theoretical insights and case studies that complement the content of “Literature and the Publishing Industry for the Early Years”. In addition, it lays the groundwork for the second-semester courses “CL Texts & Media” and “New Media for C&YAs”.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- explain why children’s literature has been essentially multi-medial since its inception in the second half of the 18th century
- describe the specific characteristics between children’s literature and other media such as children’s theatre, between children’s literature and other media such as children’s theatre, film, comics, and adult literature
- explain the various stages and aspects of adaptation processes
- explain how in picture books text and image interact to construct a narrative and describe various viewpoints on the relations between the verbal and the visual in picture books.

Skills:
- distinguish between different media and their specific characteristics
- examine the ways in which A) children’s literature is adapted and transformed to other media, and B) adult literature is adapted and transformed to children’s literature, viz. comparing adaptations to the adapted text and identifying the adaptation strategies used.

Competences:
- relate the knowledge provided in the course – on the text-image relationship in picturebooks and comics, the relationship between children’s and adult literature, and the border traffic between media – to insights gained in the related courses in the programme
- independently collect sources and conduct research on a topic related to the course contents, and communicate their findings in classroom discussions as well as in academic writing assignments.

Forms of instruction:

  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

This course is offered online and requires students to process the materials independently.

Exam language:

  • English

Exam options:

Form of co-examination: Internal co-examination

Assessment form: 7-point grading scale

Comments: Ordinary and re-examination:
The exam consists of a portfolio comprising three academic writing assignments, each on a topic of the student’s choice. The assignments are intended to allow the students to hone their skills in composing several components of the academic paper (e.g. introduction, research question, methodology, body paragraph). The total length of the portfolio is 2250 words.

Children's Literature and Childhood. The Interaction Between Concepts of Childhood, and Children's Literature and Media 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students (a) to account for, in general terms, the historical and current perceptions of childhood and their social contexts, (b) to analyse examples of texts and media of children and young people based on this knowledge and (c) to participate in and initiate discussions and investigations of the relationship between childhood perceptions and the texts and media of children and young people in transnational and cross-disciplinary contexts, (d) to discuss the relationship between concepts of childhood and children’s use of texts and media in specific contexts, and reflect on suggestions for change.

The course focuses on the concepts of childhood and youth from a historical and current perspective, and on how these are linked to the production and reception of texts and media for children and young people. The course provides a general introduction to childhood history with a focus on modern times and to theoretical approaches that can form the basis for analysing the perceptions of childhood and youth in connection with texts and media for children and young people. The students learn to delimit an issue within the course and to discuss childhood/youth perceptions as reflected in and around specific texts, media and contexts across national borders.

The course elaborates on elements of the history and theory of childhood from the semester1 courses by developing the students’ knowledge, skills and competences in this specific subject area. It also creates the preconditions for the students to include issues regarding the perceptions of childhood and youth in a well-founded manner in the semester 3 courses and in the Master’s thesis.

Academic objectives: 
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- account for, in general terms, historical and current perceptions of childhood in relation to children’s literature and media
- account for intended and/or actual use of children’s texts and media based on knowledge of changing concepts of childhood.

Skills:
- apply knowledge in order to critically analyze the interaction between concepts of childhood and examples of children’s literature and media, or children’s use of texts and media in specific contexts
- present, discuss and communicate aspects of the relation between children’s texts and media and concepts of childhood.

Competences:
- independently participate in and initiate discussions and examinations of the relationship between concepts of childhood and children’s texts and media
- relate their knowledge andskills concerning childhood and children’s texts and media to specific contexts, and suggest actions for possible change.

Forms of instruction:

  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

The overall course plan is organized by the teacher, but during the course it is conducted in collaboration between teacher and students. Students are expected to take part in presentations in groups. This is also done in order to secure a varied and transnational curriculum.

Children's Literature, Texts and Media: Transmedial Connections 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to enable students to (a) understand and account for methods and theories for analysing texts, media and contexts for children and young people as well as the interaction between and the use of texts and media and; (b) apply this knowledge in analyses of texts, media, and their use in specific contexts.

The course covers selected theories and methods for the analysis of children and young people’s literature, texts, media, and various interplays between texts and media, as well as children and young people’s use of and interaction with texts and media in specific contexts. The course is based on overall themes within the field, including the interaction between digital and analogue media, transmedia connections, genres, and discussions of target groups. The course also includes an introduction to children and young people as producers, co-producers and recipients.

In relation to the Master’s programme in Children’s Literature, Media and Cultural Entrepreneurship in general, the course focuses on methods of analysis and their foundation in theory and empirical studies. Through the course, the students acquire skills in analysing and systematising material in concrete studies and are introduced to minor user studies. Skills:
- apply knowledge in order to critically analyse examples of texts, media, transmedial connections between children’s literature, texts and media, and children’s and young people’s media use

Competences:

Life Writing and Fan Fiction 5 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose: 
The purpose of the course is that students (A) become familiar with the different genres of (verbal and visual) life writing, the main motives for life writing, and important theoretical concepts in the field of life writing studies, (B) get insight in how to apply these theoretical concepts in an analysis of life writing texts, (C) can analyse and interpret such texts independently, (D) are able to communicate the results of their analysis and interpretation, and (E) are able to discuss and evaluate the ethics of life writing.

Life writing is usually associated with genres such as diary, autobiography, and biography, but it also appears in other forms, such as short narratives about one’s life and (partly) fictionalised accounts of one’s experiences. This course provides an introduction to these different forms of life writing in the field of children’s and young adult literature, including online forms such as blogs and fan fiction. These life writing genres are related to central concepts in the field of life writing studies such as authenticity, identity formation, self-fashioning, and the ethics and politics in and of life writing. The concepts are applied to life writing texts for children and young adults.

The course builds on the primary aim of the CLMCE degree programme in that it provides a theoretical grounding in life writing and fan fiction (as life writing) as well as opportunities for students to acquire and develop skills in analysing life writing texts, and evaluate how they can function for young readers.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe the characteristics of different forms of (verbal and visual) offline and online life writing
- demonstrate knowledge of the most important theoretical concepts in the study of life writing
- reflect on how motives for telling about one’s own life or someone else’s life may determine the form and content of life writing.

Skills:
- apply theoretical concepts in an analysis of life writing texts
- discuss and evaluate the ethics of life writing
- communicate their knowledge of life writing to professional audiences.

Competences:
- Independently make an analysis of life writing texts;
- Identify the importance of life writing in their own lives and those of young readers.

Multidisciplinary Approaches to Principles, Practice, and Current Trends in Academic Research in Children's Literature 5 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose: 
This course invites students to map — and begin participating in — the processes through which scholarship is produced and disseminated, and the forums where scholarly work ultimately appears in print, including the professional organizations and conferences that support research.

In this course, students will enter an apprenticeship as researchers. By pursuing a project that entails reviewing relevant scholarship, developing a research question, writing a literature review, proposing and delivering a conference-style paper, and writing an outline for an article-length manuscript, participants will practice the discipline's most frequently used scholarly genres with the goal of producing some original scholarship.

By the end of this course, students will be prepared to present scholarly work at academic conferences and prepare them in to undertake some of the fundamental practices of the programme’s capstone.

Academic objectives: 
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Master's Thesis

30 ECTS
Master's Thesis 30 ECTS Semester 4 7-point grading scale
Exam details

Description of qualifications:
Purpose: 
The objective of the course is to produce a substantial piece of academic work which meets the academic standards of the programme. By working on their Master’s thesis, students develop in-depth knowledge of a topic they have chosen themselves which must be within the subject area, as well as knowledge of the relevant academic literature. Students thereby develop the ability to apply the theories and methods of the programme to address a well-defined thesis statement.

Students also develop the ability to independently structure their own work process, including the ability to plan, organise and work on a substantial academic assignment, identify the need for the independent acquisition of new knowledge, and present their work in a clear and comprehensible manner.

Academic objectives: 
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- present an overview of existing research on the thesis topic
- explain the theories, concepts and methods relevant to the thesis.

Skills:
- prepare a well-defined thesis statement with a view to using it for academic analysis
- use academic theories and methods critically and consciously, including choosing and applying relevant primary and secondary literature
- write grammatically and linguistically correctly, use the citing and referencing practice relevant for the field, and communicate and argue in an objective, coherent and comprehensible manner.

Competences:
- plan and carry out the thesis process independently within the given framework
- present academic knowledge to a target group in an appropriate manner
- relate the chosen topic, theories and methods to existing practice within the field and suggest possible new initiatives or changes.

Forms of instruction:

  • Supervision

Exam language:

  • English

Exam options:

   Take-home assignment on topic of student's choice

Form of co-examination: External co-examination

Assessment form: 7-point grading scale

Comments:

Ordinary exam and re-examination: 
The written assignment must have a scope of 20,000-25,000 words (not counting the front cover, table of contents, summary, bibliography and any appendices). The topic of the thesis and the thesis statement must be relevant to the degree programme and must be agreed on and approved by the thesis supervisor.

The thesis must contain a summary of 1-2 standard pages in English or in the mother tongue of the student if it is not English. The summary is included in the overall assessment. 

The Master’s Thesis is only taken for assessment if the supervision plan has been approved. The supervision plan must be approved prior to each examination attempt.

If the master’s thesis does not contain a summary the assignment is not taken for assessment.

3.2 Regulations for commencement of studies exam

3.3 Credit and flexibility

Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document the results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements.

Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad.

When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.


3.4 Registration and withdrawal

Registration for courses and exams must take place at the student-self service site. For rules regarding registration and withdrawal, please consult the university’s rules concerning the location of, registration for, participation in and withdrawal from courses and exams (Collection of internal rules for students at Aarhus University (au.dk)) as well as your degree programme’s study portal. 


3.5 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of Master's thesis and other major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to the student's command of the content knowledge. 

In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.6 Regulations for assignments

Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.7 Using computers for examinations

Most written exams at AU are taken in a digital exams system. More information about digital exams may be found on the study portal. See also Aarhus University’s rules and regulations on on-site examinations Collection of internal rules for students at Aarhus University (au.dk)

3.8 Project-oriented course

Options for project-oriented courses are stated in the description of the individual study element.

3.9 Exemption

An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the board of studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the board of studies forwards the application to the appropriate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and must be submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal.

3.10 Appeals and complaints

Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order.

More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.


3.11 Examinations

Calculation of average grade point
It is possible to get the weighted average grade point calculated and formally verified. The weighted average grade point is an expression of the average grade point calculated in relation to the ECTS weighting of the grade points. The average grade point is calculated to one decimal point based on all graded exams. The average grade is not calculated in relation to the grades of fellow students, and that the calculation of the average mark is not offered until the programme's last exam has been passed.
