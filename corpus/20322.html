1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The aim of the Master’s supplementary subject in English is to provide students with practical proficiency in English and knowledge of English language, literature and culture and the society and history of English-speaking countries, enabling them to act as communicators of English culture in various contexts. The student will also learn to independently obtain and process data about English-speaking countries and cultures, and to work as an upper-secondary school teacher in Denmark. The Master’s supplementary subject in English aims at vocational competences, including the ability to conduct systematic analyses of academic problems and stringent problem-solving skills.

1.2 Learning outcomes

During this degree programme students should acquire the following knowledge, skills and competences:

 Knowledge:

  • Varied knowledge of the linguistic, literary, cultural and historical aspects of English
  • An academically founded awareness of theories and methods in English
  • Research-based insight into a defined academic subject area
  • Insight into relevant academic literature

Skills:

  • Ability to express themselves correctly, appropriately and in suitably varied fashion in spoken and written English
  • Ability to apply relevant academic concepts, theories and terminologies to aspects of English language, literature, history, society and culture
  • Ability to find, evaluate and analyse relevant sources on a given subject area
  • Ability to isolate and define an object for analysis and study it critically and methodically
  • Ability to formulate a clear and relevant problem based on a complex subject area

Competences:

  • Competences to present complex issues, points and perspectives to various target groups
  • Competences to consider and adopt a critical approach to the methods and theories used in a given context
  • Competences to identify their own learning requirements in various contexts and situations
  • Competences to reason consistently when making academic choices (inclusion, omission and planning) in a given situation.

1.3 Admission requirements and prerequisites

You can only apply for admission to a Master's degree programme with a Master’s supplementary subject, if your main subject is taught at upper secondary schools, and if you as part of your qualifying Bachelor's degree programme has had the Bachelor's supplementary subject, as the master's degree is an extension of.

Have you read your Bachelor's supplementary subject at another University than Aarhus University, it is up to an individual assessment to determine if you can be admitted to the master's degree at Aarhus University. The academic assessment is made in connection with your application for admission.

Students who have a programme combination which has been extended by 30 ECTS credits will be enrolled on the line called “Master’s supplementary subject in English (extension)”, with the Master’s supplementary subject carrying 75 ECTS credits.

Other students will be enrolled on the line called “Master’s supplementary subject in English”.


1.4 This programme grants access to:

This degree programme does not grant access to other degree programmes.

2. Degree programme structure

2.1 Degree programme rules
In addition to the general rules (section 3) at the end of the academic regulations, the following rules apply to the programme:

2.1.1 Prerequisites for exam participation
Prerequisites are demands with which students must comply before taking an exam. If an exam includes prerequisites, this will be stated in the exam description for the specific course under the heading "Prerequisites for the examination participation". These must be met prior to taking the exam and must only be met once. Students who fail to meet this requirement will not be able to take the exam, and a examination attempt must be used.
The rules and deadlines for proving compliance with the prerequisites are stated on the study portal (studerende.au.dk/arts).

2.1.2 Lecture participation
Lecture participation is an exam form and involves: Regular, active and satisfactory participation in the teaching. 'Regular' means participating in at least 75% of the teaching activities offered. At the start of the semester, the teacher will announce in writing (on Brightspace) the teaching activities in which students are expected to participate, and the requirements regarding what will be construed as active and satisfactory participation

2.1.3 Language of teaching and exams
The language of the teaching and exams is English.

2.1.4 Syllabus
The syllabus can be reused for up to three semesters: the semester in which the course is taught, and the two following semesters.
If an exam needs to be conducted in accordance with an old syllabus, the students are responsible for pointing this out.
The syllabus rules can be found on the study portal.

2.1.5 Forms of instruction:
The programme will involve a variety of forms of teaching and student work. 
Teaching forms may include but are not limited to: lecturing, seminar instruction, leading in-class discussions, leading the close reading of set texts, asking and answering questions, assigning and clarifying tasks, providing supervision and feedback, and providing course materials and course-related information on Brigthspace.

Student work will include a variety of work forms, which may include but are not limited to: preparing individually and in groups for seminars and lectures, independent study activities, completing assignments, preparing and delivering individual or group student presentations in class, asking and answering questions, participating in classroom discussions, leading the close reading of set texts, practical training (guided / structured individual and group practice in class), leading seminars and discussions individually or in groups, conducting literature searches and carrying out research, incorporating and using supervision and feedback, including giving and receiving peer feedback, interacting with individuals, organizations, and companies, completing midterm and end-of-term course evaluations, and preparing for the exam.

Other teaching and student work forms may include teaching by student instructors, workshops, online interaction and tasks, field trips and excursions, and guest lecturers.
Forms of both teaching and student work will be appropriate for the course purpose and aligned with the learning outcomes and exam. The forms of teaching and student work for a given course will be announced on Brigthspace and in course guides at the beginning of the semester. Forms of teaching and student work may be subject to change.

Current topics in English linguistics 10 ECTS 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
This course deals with selected topics within English linguistics which are central to contemporary research into the English language. The course provides students with in-depth knowledge of modern theoretical, empirical and methodological issues in English linguistics. The approaches are interdisciplinary (i.e. they include philosophical, neurological, psychological or social perspectives) and/or comparative (i.e. they compare variations of modern English with each other, with other languages and with older forms of English).

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge of selected topics within English linguistics, their relevant research literature, tools of analysis, methods, theories and issues
- demonstrate knowledge and understanding of selected modern theoretical and methodical approaches to the study of English linguistics.

Skills:
- carry out independent topic-related literature searches and selections, and assess and apply different types of primary and secondary sources within English linguistics
- produce independent written academic material on a current linguistic topic and structure a given subject area
- communicate in a coherent, well-argued and comprehensible manner at a high level of academic precision in terms of grammar, spelling and language; using a suitably varied vocabulary, including relevant concepts and terminology; and using academic conventions in terms of layout, notes, references and citations as specified in the Department of English Style Guide.

Competences:
- critically analyse different issues within modern English linguistics
- work independently and professionally with cross-disciplinary and research-related material using a professional and focused approach.

  • Classroom instruction

Current topics in history, society and culture 10 ECTS 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
This course aims to develop the ability of the students to analyse and present knowledge on selected aspects of history, society and culture in the United Kingdom and Northern Ireland, Ireland, the United States and other English-speaking countries. Emphasis is placed on providing students with in-depth knowledge of recent theories and methods which raise relevant historical, social and cultural debates. The course is based on a combination of primary and secondary sources – theoretical as well as practical – and students work on the presentation of relevant theories and methods as well as current and historical topics in selected English historical, social and cultural popular science journals. Through a number of group and individual activities, students train their understanding of the communication of popular science (types, aims, terms), the structure of texts, the selection of publications and the relationship between academic analyses and public debate. The aim is to develop the students’ academic communicative skills in English in a societal context.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge and understanding of selected current debates and issues within the study of history, society, culture and media in English-speaking countries as well as selected modern theoretical and methodical approaches to the study of history, society, culture and media.

Skills:
- apply relevant theories, methods and terminologies in order to present and analyse, assess or contribute to selected current issues within the study of history, society, culture and media in English-speaking countries
- identify, assess, combine and apply different types of primary and/or secondary sources with particular regard for their target group, purpose and use
- demonstrate the ability to present research-based knowledge and professional and academic issues to both peers and non-specialists in the communication assignment in grammatically, orthographically and idiomatically correct and stylistically appropriate English
- communicate in a coherent, well-argued and comprehensible manner at a high level of academic precision in terms of grammar, spelling and language; using a suitably varied vocabulary, including relevant concepts and terminology; and using academic conventions in terms of layout, notes, references and citations as specified in the Department of English Style Guide.

Competences:
- acquire new knowledge and skills within the study of history, society, culture and media in English-speaking countries based on knowledge and skills acquired in the course.

Purpose:
This course deals with the effect of creative texts in English, for instance how they work when placed in the public domain, how they are used in social and scientific debates, and how they function in interaction with modern work environments. By working with one or more primary texts, the students study not just the production of literary and related texts, but also the institutional, commercial or technological factors that make these texts part of the modern world’s collective experiences as well as commercial and public discourses.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge of selected texts and the critical discussion of them
- demonstrate knowledge and understanding of cross-disciplinary approaches to the study of literature
- demonstrate knowledge of how selected literary texts work in a broader cultural context.

Skills:
- analyse how literary texts are transformed in the creative industry with a view to creating new meanings relevant to modern social and cultural environments
- understand how cultural and literary theory is made available to a broad market through the creative industry, including theatre, publishing, marketing, etc.
- demonstrate the ability to present research-based knowledge and professional and academic issues to both peers and non-specialists in the communication assignment in grammatically, orthographically and idiomatically correct and stylistically appropriate English
- communicate in a coherent, well-argued and comprehensible manner at a high level of academic precision in terms of grammar, spelling and language; using a suitably varied vocabulary, including relevant concepts and terminology; and using academic conventions in terms of layout, notes, references and citations as specified in the Department of English Style Guide.

Competences:
- acquire new knowledge and skills within the study of English literature on the basis of knowledge and skills acquired during the course.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Emphasis is placed on study methods such as the reading of primary and secondary texts, written assignments and/or oral contributions from the students, data collection and processing, classroom discussions and group work, including presentations and peer feedback. At the start of the semester, the teacher will inform the students of the number of assignments, their form, length and deadlines both on Brightspace and orally. The assignments can provide the basis for various forms of feedback and further development related to the teaching, but the individual assignments are not assessed on a continuous basis. All or some of these assignments can provide the basis for the student’s exam.

Language, evidence, and theory 10 ECTS 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The course examines different assumptions and misconceptions about what language is or does, including issues regarding the relationship between e.g. language and communication, language and thought, language and gender, and language and society. The course focuses on how evidence can be used to test claims and theoretically founded predictions. The course includes evidence of variations in the English language (for example syntactic, semantic, morphological or phonologic/phonetic) in various domains such as historical, dialectal or sociolectal variation, including multilingualism, comparative linguistics and language contact involving English. The course further develops the students’ insight into cognitive and methodical bias, and includes various sources in order to shed light on how the choice of source may limit the scope of a given theory about how language is understood and interpreted, and how it changes over time.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge of critical thinking and academic character as central concepts within the study of English linguistics
- demonstrate knowledge of various forms of linguistic evidence and methods of data collection
- demonstrate knowledge of how various aspects of linguistic theories are based on empirical evidence.

Skills:
- apply critical thinking and critically address all aspects of research in English linguistics: from designing a study to analysing and interpreting data/evidence
- identify and assess linguistic theories, predictions and/or claims
- communicate in a coherent, well-argued and comprehensible manner at a high level of academic precision in terms of grammar, spelling and language; using a suitably varied vocabulary, including relevant concepts and terminology; and using academic conventions in terms of layout, notes, references and citations as specified in the Department of English Style Guide.

Competences:
- demonstrate competence in the critical analysis of the quality of research in English linguistics, and how it can be used to adopt a critical approach to various widespread assumptions concerning language
- use different forms of empirical studies to argue for or against different linguistic hypotheses and claims about language.

3.2.1 Credit and flexibility
Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document his or her results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements. Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.

3.2.2 Registration and withdrawal
Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.

Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.

Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.
Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.

If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled (before the deadline for registering for exams, as stated on the study portal) to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

3.2.3 Spelling and fluency
In the assessment of major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to his/her command of the content knowledge.
In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.2.4 Definition of standard page
Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.2.6 Exemption
An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the Board of Studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the Board of Studies forwards the application to the appro-priate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal portal.

3.2.7 Appeals and complaints
Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing and must account for the student’s reasons for lodging the complaint. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order. More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.

3.2.8 Exchange
A degree programme may include study abroad. More information is available on the study portal.
