1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

Contemporary journalism faces multiple challenges and new opportunities. Societal, technological and market-related changes, propelled by the thrust of Globalisation, call for a new focus on key issues vital to the democratic function of the discipline. The overall aim of the Master’s Degree Programme in Journalism, Media and Globalisation is to prepare students to seize these opportunities and to confront these challenges, thus providing them with the best foundation for their future careers.

The study programme is unique by offering a two-year Master’s degree in Journalism, Media and Globalisation through studies in (at least) two European countries, and by integrating both academic and journalistic theories and practices. The study regulations below describe the courses during the first, foundational year, in Aarhus. These courses are tailored to combine academic and practical skills from political science, media and journalism studies and journalistic practice. The courses focus on theory, data, methods, modes of presentation and ethics in the study of contemporary journalism. In their second year, students choose to specialise in either “Politics and Communication” (Amsterdam), “Totalitarianism and Transition” (Prague), "Crisis and Conflict" (London) or "Cultures and Contexts" (Munich).

Each year, selected students will have the opportunity of following an individual 10 ECTS project placement (internship) module or of substituting their second semester courses by studies at Erasmus Mundus Journalism, Media and Globalisation Consortium credit awarding (exchange partner) universities.


1.2 Learning outcomes

Students acquire the following knowledge, skills and competences during the Master’s Degree Programme in Journalism, Media and Globalisation:

Knowledge:

  • General knowledge of and the ability to identify challenges and opportunities facing journalism in a changing world
  • Specific knowledge of selected academic and journalistic methods of analyses and tools of communication
  • Understanding of the role of national and international media systems in light of these challenges and opportunities
  • The ability to reflect on contexts of globalisation in regard to the challenges and opportunities facing journalism today.

Skills:

  • The ability to formulate research questions addressing the role of media and journalism in the light of existing theories on the interaction between media, culture and society
  • The ability to craft research designs, and to identify, collect and analyse relevant empirical data based on appropriate research questions in order to study the role of media and journalism
  • The ability to conduct both qualitative and quantitative studies
  • The ability to critically assess the quality of existing research on the challenges and opportunities facing journalism, in terms of theoretical, empirical and methods-based contributions
  • The ability to communicate research-based knowledge in both academic and journalistic genres.

Competences:

  • The ability to independently further-develop theoretical arguments based on critical assessments of existing research on the challenges and opportunities facing journalism
  • The ability to put current events into a wider perspective in a range of journalistic products
  • The ability to independently apply relevant qualitative and quantitative methodological skills in the study of challenges and opportunities facing journalism today
  • The ability to combine academic knowledge with practical skills by independently choosing between selected academic and journalistic genres to communicate research findings based on these analyses
  • The ability to combine academic knowledge with practical skills by independently applying relevant academic knowledge and skills in journalistic production
  • The ability to independently take responsibility for their own academic development by critically assessing research-based knowledge and pointing out theoretical, methods-based, empirical and ethically sound ways to address the identified weaknesses in future studies.

1.3 Admission requirements and prerequisites

Applicants must have completed a bachelor’s degree (or the equivalent) in a relevant subject in order to be admitted to the master’s degree programme in Journalism, Media and Globalisation.

Subjects within bachelor's programmes in Arts/Humanities and Social Sciences are by default considered to be relevant. Applicants who have completed other programmes, but whose qualifications are deemed by the Consortium to be relevant and adequate, may also be offered admission.

The consortium will evaluate the application as a whole. But to ensure that the best candidates are admitted, the Consortium has decided to give priority to academic merit; candidates will only be admitted if they have an above-average bachelor’s degree (a minimum of an above-average class mark/GPA is expected).

Language requirements:
The Master’s degree programme in Journalism, Media and Globalisation is taught in English. Students must therefore be able to read academic textbooks and articles, take part in classroom discussions and lectures, and undertake written work and exams in English. Therefore, non-native English speakers* applying to the programme must submit one of the following forms of evidence of English language proficiency:
- IELTS Academic test with a band score of 7,0 and a minimum score of 6.0 in each category
- TOEFL internet-based test with a score of at least 100
- Cambridge ESOL exams: Certificate of Proficiency (CPE) or Certificate in Advanced English with the grade A or at least 190 points on the Cambridge English-scale.

*We consider applicants to be native English speakers if they come from any of these countries: Antigua and Barbuda; Australia; The Bahamas; Barbados; Belize; Canada; Dominica; Grenada; Guyana; Ireland; Jamaica; New Zealand; St Kitts and Nevis; St Lucia; St Vincent and the Grenadines; Trinidad and Tobago; United Kingdom; United States of America

Applicants must have at least three months of journalistic experience:
Due to the nature of the programme, journalistic skills are required prior to course start, hence applicants must have at least 3 months’ journalistic experience. Applicants must document how their experience equals (at least) 3 months of full-time journalism experience.

Introduction to Journalism Studies: Theories and Approaches 10 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The overall aim of this course is to introduce students to different theoretical and epistemological approaches to understand modern-day news media institutions and journalism practices across the world. Through multidisciplinary perspectives, the course looks at the interrelationship between the news media, journalism, and broader societal processes such as politics, culture, the economy, and technology. In so doing, it introduces students to some of the foundational and emerging theoretical perspectives to understand and interpret contemporary sets of practices, production and reception contexts within which journalism occurs today. 

Overall, the training throughout this course orients students towards a cross-cultural, critical examination of the legacies and current debates in journalism studies. Throughout the course, students will be trained to apply the different theoretical perspectives in analyses of real-life cases across the world.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- present and assess the relevance of selected theoretical perspectives to explain the relationship between the news media, journalism and broader societal processes
- explain how these different theoretical perspectives contribute to the public construction and understanding of journalism in different cultural contexts
- theoretically reflect on and account for factors that influence journalism and the cross-cultural similarities and differences in journalistic norms and practices.

Skills:
- select and apply relevant theories in the analyses of real-life cases regarding the interrelationship between the news media, journalism, and broader societal processes.
- situate new developments in media and journalism within relevant historical contexts of transformations in the news media and journalism.

Competences:
- independently assess the strengths and weaknesses of theoretical perspectives in academic studies of the news media and journalism
- produce independent, critical, and theoretically informed analyses of selected cases of events and developments in the news media and journalism.

Methods of Researching Journalism 10 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:
Purpose:
This course builds on and further-develops students’ insights on researching journalism introduced in the course ‘Introduction to Journalism Studies: Theories and Approaches’. The course covers the basics of social science research by training students in the use of different methods for analysing original or secondary data in the study of relevant themes of interest in journalism studies. More specifically, focus will be on the distinctions between methodologies and methods; the basics of research designs; the basics of qualitative and quantitative research tools for empirical data collection, processing, and analysis and the concrete use of relevant qualitative methods in empirical research in journalism studies.

In addition to critically discussing methodologies and methods in class, students will be expected to formulate, develop, and carry out small-scale research projects addressing developments in the media landscape, in journalism and within processes of globalization. Given that it builds on and further-develops the theoretical perspectives introduced in the first course, this course aims to equip students with the ability to usefully and critically combine theories, methodologies and methods in the empirical study of selected phenomena relevant to the degree programme.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- understand and explain the basic foundations of proper academic research in the social sciences and humanities
- critically understand and reflect on the strengths and weaknesses of selected methodological approaches in the study of specific issues of interest in journalism
- critically reflect on how types of and formulation of research questions relate to the choice of research designs and methods in empirical research.

Skills:
- formulate relevant research questions based on critical evaluation of existing theories and empirical studies relating to the students’ topic of interest
- design, develop and carry out small-scale research project(s) within the broad parameters of the thematic orientation of the course and communicate the results in appropriate academic formats.

Competences
- independently assess the academic strengths and weaknesses of the student’s own work, that of peers and published research
- competently select and apply one or more relevant qualitative methods in the study of journalism
- competently use selected software/programmes to collect, process and analyze relevant data.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:
Classes for this course will be a mixture of lectures, seminar classes, and workshops facilitated by course instructors and Teaching Assistants (TAs), and involve group assignments, student presentations and discussions.

Journalistic Storytelling: Creating Stories with Engagement 10 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:
Purpose:
Whereas the courses in 'Introduction to Journalism Studies’ and 'Methods of Researching Journalism' seek to strengthen students’ abilities to study journalism, 'Journalistic Storytelling: Creating Stories with Engagement' focuses instead on enhancing their practical knowledge, skills and competences. 

This course introduces students to the principles and practices of engaged journalism. Stories told with engagement encompasses both stories where the audience is engaged, and stories developed by virtue of the journalist’s own engagement. Building on the students’ practical experience, 'Journalistic Storytelling: Creating Stories with Engagement' seeks to broaden students’ understanding of journalistic professionalism and the roles of journalism in contemporary societies. Thus, the course will focus on enabling students to produce forms of engaged journalism, to work independently with in-depth journalism in longer and research-intensive formats, and to enhance students’ critical reflections on the prospects and challenges of engaged journalism.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- interpret, reflect on and discuss the role of engagement journalism in relation to ethics, theories and practical methods
- describe, utilize and combine storytelling theories and models.

Skills:
- assess when and how it will be useful to use the ideals of engagement in professional practice
- assess the sources and data to build engaging narrative in the journalistic storytelling
- conduct engaged journalism in practice.

Competences
- independently work with engagement journalism in applying the theories, methods and practices relevant for specific media contexts
- collaborate with others in combining engagement journalism with different journalistic disciplines, roles and formats.

Applied Quantitative Research and Journalistic Practice 20 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:
Purpose:
'Applied Quantitative Research and Journalistic Practice’ builds on and further develops insights on data, ethics, theory, methods, and modes of presentation taught in the preceding courses - ‘Introduction to Journalism Studies: Theories and Approaches’, ‘Methods of Researching Journalism’, and ‘Journalistic Storytelling: Creating Stories with Engagement’. The course equips students with valued statistical skills relevant for academic research and journalism practice and allows students to take advantage of a changing media landscape where data - especially large sets of quantitative data, is a critical and growing resource and source of information.

The course will thus enable students to (A) develop and implement quantitative research designs to test theoretical questions of interest, and (B) report these results truthfully and ethically in relevant journalistic formats. By applying these insights to the study and reporting of socially or politically salient topics used as running examples throughout the course, students will learn how to conduct theoretically driven quantitative research, including developing a theoretical framework, identifying, collecting, and operationalizing data, analysing and presenting data using statistical software(s), and responsibly interpreting and visualizing results in relevant academic and journalistic formats.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge
- explain the role, impact, and ethics of quantitative data in academic research and journalistic practice
- competently reflect on and discuss how a socially or politically relevant case can be analysed and reported on using quantitative data.

Skills:
- combine knowledge of quantitative methods and journalistic skills to identify, analyse and tell stories linked to a relevant, current event
- design and carry out small scale research projects using quantitative methods and statistical software(s) to study socially or politically relevant phenomena
- communicate findings based on quantitative analyses of current events using relevant journalistic techniques.

Competences:
- utilize insights gleaned from quantitative data to enrich debates of public interest
- participate in and advance interdisciplinary discussions and practices by bridging quantitative research and journalistic methods
- independently reflect on the challenges and opportunities in reporting quantitative data truthfully and ethically in academic and journalistic formats and contexts.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:
The course consists of a combination of lectures, smaller seminars and workshops, individual tasks and group work, as well as discussions and debates, feedback sessions and in-person coaching. During the course, students will produce academic and journalistic piece(s) which incorporate lessons learnt on the use of quantitative data. The introduced quantitative methods will be applied using one or more socially and politically salient topics as running examples throughout the course. These topics will be presented to the students when the course commences.

Students produce a number of assignments during the course. At the start of the semester, the teacher will inform the students of the number of assignments, their form, length and deadlines both on Brightspace and orally. The assignments can provide the basis for various forms of feedback and further development related to the teaching, but the individual assignments are not assessed on a continuous basis. All or some of these assignments can provide the basis for the student’s exam.

Individual Project Placement 10 ECTS 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:
Purpose:
A project placement is an independent and delimited period of time during which the majority of the student’s academic activities take place in a non-academic setting. This can be in an organisation, a public or private company, or in the student’s own startup. The purpose of the Individual Project Placement is to provide students with the opportunity of applying and using their academic knowledge, skills and competences in a relevant job environment. Thus, in accordance with the overall purpose of the degree programme, the project placement focuses on the student’s ability to bridge between academic and practical insights.

As a 10 ECTS course, an individual project placement covers 273 hours of work, the equivalent of 5-8 weeks of work placement in a non-academic setting with 8-10 hours per week devoted to supervision and the production of the reflection report that constitutes the synopsis for the oral exam.

In order to sign up for an individual project placement, the student must find a project host that can be pre-approved by the programme coordinator and sign a contract with the project host and the programme coordinator. Both project placements in Denmark and abroad can be approved, but must fulfill the study requirements and national legislation in terms of working conditions, economic compensation and insurances. In case the student does not pass the exam, the project placement cannot be approved as credit-awarding.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- identify areas within the host organisation or company where their academic and journalistic knowledge, skills and competences can be successfully applied
- describe how particular academic insights and journalistic skills acquired in preceding courses can best be utilised in regard to these areas.

Skills:
- design a framework for applying the above-mentioned insights and skills in regard to these area
- enter into dialogue with the project host or external stakeholders concerning the actual implementation of these insights and skills.

Competences:
- take into account strengths and weaknesses of the designed framework specifically in light of the identified needs of the internship place
- take into account how insights from previous courses in the programme has been applied in the process of developing this framework
- independently reflect on the relevance of the content of the project placement in regard to the programme as a whole.

Forms of instruction:

  • Supervision

Comments on form of instruction:

As a 10 ECTS course, an individual project placement covers 273 hours of work, the equivalent of 5-8 weeks of work placement in an organisation or company with 8-10 hours per week devoted to supervision and the production of the journalistic product and the project report that constitutes the exam.

During the project placement, the student has regular meetings with the assigned supervisor. These meetings focus on the development of the journalistic product and the reflection report.

Description of qualifications:
Purpose:
A project placement is an independent and delimited period of time during which the majority of the student’s academic activities take place in a non-academic setting. This can be in an organisation, a public or private company, or in the student’s own startup. The purpose of the Individual Project Placement is to provide students with the opportunity of applying and using their academic knowledge, skills and competences in a relevant job environment. Thus, in accordance with the overall purpose of the degree programme, the project placement focuses on the student’s ability to bridge between academic and practical insights.

As a 10 ECTS course, an individual project placement covers 273 hours of work, the equivalent of 5-8 weeks of work placement in a non-academic setting with 8-10 hours per week devoted to supervision and the production of the reflection report that constitutes the synopsis for the oral exam.

In order to sign up for an individual project placement, the student must find a project host that can be pre-approved by the programme coordinator and sign a contract with the project host and the programme coordinator. Both project placements in Denmark and abroad can be approved, but must fulfill the study requirements and national legislation in terms of working conditions, economic compensation and insurances. In case the student does not pass the exam, the project placement cannot be approved as credit-awarding.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- identify areas within the host organisation or company where their academic and journalistic knowledge, skills and competences can be successfully applied
- describe how particular academic insights and journalistic skills acquired in preceding courses can best be utilised in regard to these areas.

Skills:
- design a framework for applying the above-mentioned insights and skills in regard to these area
- enter into dialogue with the project host or external stakeholders concerning the actual implementation of these insights and skills.

Competences:
- take into account strengths and weaknesses of the designed framework specifically in light of the identified needs of the internship place
- take into account how insights from previous courses in the programme has been applied in the process of developing this framework
- independently reflect on the relevance of the content of the project placement in regard to the programme as a whole.

3.1 Authority

3.2 Credit and flexibility

Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document the results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements.

Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad.

When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.


3.3 Registration and withdrawal

Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.

Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.

Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students cannot withdraw from the Master’s thesis.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.

Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.

If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Deadlines are stated on the study portal. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

Students can choose between the specified forms of exam for their Master’s thesis.


3.4 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of Master's thesis and other major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to the student's command of the content knowledge. 

In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.5 Regulations for assignments

Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.7 Project-oriented courses

Options for project-oriented courses are stated in the description of the individual study element. It is possible to take project placement with more than one project host if it is deemed to live up to all requirements for project placement.


3.8 Exemption

An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the Board of Studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the Board of Studies forwards the application to the appropriate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented.More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal.

3.9 Appeals and complaints

Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order. More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.

3.10 Examinations

Calculation of average grade point
It is possible to get the weighted average grade point calculated and formally verified. The weighted average grade point is an expression of the average grade point calculated in relation to the ECTS weighting of the grade points. The average grade point is calculated to one decimal point based on all graded exams. The average grade is not calculated in relation to the grades of fellow students, and that the calculation of the average mark is not offered until the programme's last exam has been passed.
