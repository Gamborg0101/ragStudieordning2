1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The Master’s degree programme in English encompasses the study of the language, literature, history, society and culture of the English-speaking world.

The subject area History, society and culture focuses on current academic topics, activities and conditions related to the English-speaking world in order to train the student’s skills in analysing and presenting historical and current developments and debates on the basis of relevant concepts, theories and methods. This academic area contains the following subjects: Current topics in history, society and culture, and the Cultural and creative industries.

The subject area Literature focuses on current academic topics, activities and conditions related to the English-speaking world in order to develop the student’s skills in analysing and presenting literary texts on the basis of relevant concepts, theories and methods. The student will be trained in analytical theories and methods which will be used in reading and critically interpreting many different types of text in a wide range of culture-historical, aesthetic, political, economic and philosophical contexts. This academic area contains the following subjects: Current topics in literature in English and Creative literatures at work.

The subject area Linguistics focuses on current academic topics, activities and conditions related to the English-speaking world in order to develop the student’s skills in analysing and presenting literary texts on the basis of relevant concepts, theories and methods. This academic area contains the following subjects: Current topics in English linguistics and Language data and linguistic theory.

The module Current topics comprises the courses Current topics in history, society and culture, which develops the student’s ability to analyse and present knowledge of selected aspects of history, society and culture in Great Britain and Northern Ireland, Ireland, the United States and other English-speaking countries; Current topics in literature in English, which introduces students to some of the central, ongoing debates within literary studies in English; and Current topics in English linguistics, which covers selected topics within English linguistics which are central to current research.
The module Applied humanities comprises the course Cultural industries, which examines selected cultural activities in the English-speaking world within the private and public sectors as well as nonprofit organisations: from production and financing through sales promotion and expenditure; Creative literatures at work, which deals with the effect of creative texts in English, such as how these work in the public sphere, how they are used in social and academic debates, and how they work in collaboration with modern work environments; and finally, Language data and linguistic theory, which examines different assumptions and misconceptions of what language is and what it does, including issues regarding the connection between for example language and communication, language and thought, language and gender, and language and society.
The module Methods and theory consists of the course Evaluating evidence, which builds on the students’ understanding of various approaches to evidence from linguistics, literary studies and the social, cultural and historical sciences. The elective courses in the module Advanced research topic further develops students’ knowledge of and proficiency and skills within specific aspects of the study of English, and targets their studies towards a specific profile in relation to the central disciplines of English: linguistics, literature, society, culture and history. The course Thesis research colloquium further develops students’ knowledge of and ability to work with a long-term project and immerse themselves in the methods and theories of the study of English.

Track B of the Master’s degree programme in English is intended and designed to develop the student’s knowledge, skills and competences with a view to the student achieving academic competences in English as a subject in preparation for teaching at upper-secondary schools, teacher-training colleges, universities, language schools or Danish folk high schools and continuation schools. The Master’s degree programme meets the academic minimum requirements for teaching English at upper-secondary schools.
Track A of the Master’s degree programme in English is aimed at developing the student’s knowledge, skills and competences to enable the student to work in the cultural and creative industries, and the communication and IT industries, for example as a communications adviser, web editor or copywriter; as a consultant, an administrative officer or project manager; and to work with translation in (for example) international companies and organisations. The Master’s degree programme in English develops the student’s knowledge, skills and competences with a view to enabling the student to perform many professional functions which require understanding of the English-speaking world, a high level of fluency in the English language, as well as analysis and interpretation of both fictional and non-fictional texts in various media. In addition to subject-specific knowledge, the broad range of theories and methods used in the degree programme – from those classically found in the humanities to those of the natural sciences – require adaptability and flexibility, and this prepares students to work within and communicate between different work cultures and environments. The student acquires skills in producing linguistically sophisticated and rhetorically convincing material, especially in English-speaking contexts, and in analysing and criticising other people’s material in an effective and constructive manner.


1.2 Learning outcomes

The Master's degree programme in English provides students with the following knowledge, skills and competences:

Knowledge:

  • Detailed knowledge of the linguistic, literary, cultural and historical aspects of English
  • An academically founded awareness of theories and methods of the subject of English in a historical, current and application-oriented perspective
  • In-depth, research-based insight into a defined topic area in this field
  • In-depth insight into relevant academic literature.

Skills:

  • Ability to express themselves correctly, appropriately and in suitably varied fashion in spoken and written English
  • Ability to apply relevant academic concepts, theories and terminology at a high academic level to aspects of English language, literature, history, society and culture
  • Ability to find, assess and analyse relevant source material relating to a given subject area, and organise this material in an appropriate way
  • Ability to isolate and define an object for analysis and study it independently, critically and methodically
  • Ability to formulate a clear and relevant problem based on a complex subject area
  • Ability to present complex issues, points and perspectives to various target groups
  • Ability to assess, justify and apply relevant humanistic theories and methods when solving problems
  • Ability to consider and critically assess the methods and theories used in a given situation; including their reassessment, adaptation and supplementation in relation to the situation in question.

Competences:

  • Competences to independently initiate, participate in and conduct subject-specific as well as cross-disciplinary collaboration with a view to fulfilling project objectives in a professional manner
  • Competences to identify and structure their own learning requirements in various contexts and situations
  • Competences to reason consistently and soundly when making academic choices (inclusion, omission and planning) in a given situation.

2. Degree programme structure

2.1 Degree programme rules
In addition to the general rules (section 3) at the end of the academic regulations, the following rules apply to the programme:

2.1.1 Prerequisites for exam participation
Prerequisites are demands with which students must comply before taking an exam. If an exam includes prerequisites, this will be stated in the exam description for the specific course under the heading "Prerequisites for the examination participation". These must be met prior to taking the exam and must only be met once. Students who fail to meet this requirement will not be able to take the exam, and a examination attempt must be used.
The rules and deadlines for proving compliance with the prerequisites are stated on the study portal (studerende.au.dk/arts).

2.1.2 Lecture participation
Lecture participation is an exam form and involves: Regular, active and satisfactory participation in the teaching. 'Regular' means participating in at least 75% of the teaching activities offered. At the start of the semester, the teacher will announce in writing (on Brightspace) the teaching activities in which students are expected to participate, and the requirements regarding what will be construed as active and satisfactory participation

2.1.3 Language of teaching and exams
The language of the teaching and exams is English.

2.1.4 Syllabus
The syllabus can be reused for up to three semesters: the semester in which the course is taught, and the two following semesters.
If an exam needs to be conducted in accordance with an old syllabus, the students are responsible for pointing this out.
The syllabus rules can be found on the study portal.

2.1.5 Forms of instruction:
The programme will involve a variety of forms of teaching and student work. 
Teaching forms may include but are not limited to: lecturing, seminar instruction, leading in-class discussions, leading the close reading of set texts, asking and answering questions, assigning and clarifying tasks, providing supervision and feedback, and providing course materials and course-related information on Brigthspace.

Student work will include a variety of work forms, which may include but are not limited to: preparing individually and in groups for seminars and lectures, independent study activities, completing assignments, preparing and delivering individual or group student presentations in class, asking and answering questions, participating in classroom discussions, leading the close reading of set texts, practical training (guided / structured individual and group practice in class), leading seminars and discussions individually or in groups, conducting literature searches and carrying out research, incorporating and using supervision and feedback, including giving and receiving peer feedback, interacting with individuals, organizations, and companies, completing midterm and end-of-term course evaluations, and preparing for the exam.

Other teaching and student work forms may include teaching by student instructors, workshops, online interaction and tasks, field trips and excursions, and guest lecturers.
Forms of both teaching and student work will be appropriate for the course purpose and aligned with the learning outcomes and exam. The forms of teaching and student work for a given course will be announced on Brigthspace and in course guides at the beginning of the semester. Forms of teaching and student work may be subject to change.

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The aim of this cross-disciplinary course is to develop students’ understanding of various approaches to linguistic, literary, social, cultural and historical evidence. The course aims to further develop students’ skills and ability to find, analyse and assess academic English primary sources in order to increase their understanding of the ways in which sources can reflect or interact with their context. The course provides students with an increased knowledge of the concept of science and academia, the difference between qualitative and quantitative analysis of evidence, and various methods and good practice in academic and scientific research. The course provides students with insight into different kinds of cognitive and methodological bias as well as the necessary skills and competences to think critically and work professionally with evidence in a variety of contexts.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge of critical thinking and science, allegations, hypotheses and data/evidence, as well as intuition and theory as central concepts within the study of English, including linguistics, literature, history, society, culture and media
- demonstrate knowledge of concepts and tools used to analyse, assess and interpret evidence within the study of English, including linguistics, literature, history, society, culture and media.

Skills:
- identify, assess and interpret different types of evidence to support claims, arguments and decision-making
- identify, assess and interpret different types of cognitive and methodological bias and apply critical thinking in the assessment of various types of evidence to support claims, arguments and decision-making
- communicate in a coherent, well-argued and comprehensible manner at a high level of academic precision in terms of grammar, spelling and language; using a suitably varied vocabulary, including relevant concepts and terminology; and using academic conventions in terms of layout, notes, references and citations as specified in the Department of English Style Guide.

Competences:
- assess the relevance and applicability of various types of evidence within various areas of knowledge
- identify and understand different methods of data collection, analysis and interpretation
- use skills and knowledge acquired in the course in other academic areas.

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
This course aims to develop the ability of the students to analyse and present knowledge on selected aspects of history, society and culture in the United Kingdom and Northern Ireland, Ireland, the United States and other English-speaking countries. Emphasis is placed on providing students with in-depth knowledge of recent theories and methods which raise relevant historical, social and cultural debates. The course is based on a combination of primary and secondary sources – theoretical as well as practical – and students work on the presentation of relevant theories and methods as well as current and historical topics in selected English historical, social and cultural popular science journals. Through a number of group and individual activities, students train their understanding of the communication of popular science (types, aims, terms), the structure of texts, the selection of publications and the relationship between academic analyses and public debate. The aim is to develop the students’ academic communicative skills in English in a societal context.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- demonstrate knowledge and understanding of selected current debates and issues within the study of history, society, culture and media in English-speaking countries as well as selected modern theoretical and methodical approaches to the study of history, society, culture and media.

Skills:
- apply relevant theories, methods and terminologies in order to present and analyse, assess or contribute to selected current issues within the study of history, society, culture and media in English-speaking countries
- identify, assess, combine and apply different types of primary and/or secondary sources with particular regard for their target group, purpose and use
- demonstrate the ability to present research-based knowledge and professional and academic issues to both peers and non-specialists in the communication assignment in grammatically, orthographically and idiomatically correct and stylistically appropriate English
- communicate in a coherent, well-argued and comprehensible manner at a high level of academic precision in terms of grammar, spelling and language; using a suitably varied vocabulary, including relevant concepts and terminology; and using academic conventions in terms of layout, notes, references and citations as specified in the Department of English Style Guide.

Competences:

Purpose:
This course deals with the effect of creative texts in English, for instance how they work when placed in the public domain, how they are used in social and scientific debates, and how they function in interaction with modern work environments. By working with one or more primary texts, the students study not just the production of literary and related texts, but also the institutional, commercial or technological factors that make these texts part of the modern world’s collective experiences as well as commercial and public discourses.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The objective of the course is to produce a substantial piece of academic work which meets the academic standards of the programme. By working on their Master’s thesis, students develop in-depth knowledge of a topic they have chosen themselves which must be within the subject area, as well as knowledge of the relevant academic literature. Students thereby develop the ability to apply the theories and methods of the programme to address a delimited thesis statement.

Students also develop the ability to independently structure their own work process, including the ability to plan, organise and work on a substantial academic assignment, identify the need for the independent acquisition of new knowledge, and present their work in a clear and comprehensible manner.

If the Master’s thesis takes the form of a product thesis, the student will develop the ability to create a connection between the analytical/theoretical/empirical point of departure and the product.

If the Master’s thesis takes the form of a portfolio thesis, the student will develop the ability to produce academic texts which deal with one overall academic issue, as well as a synthesising presentation which presents relevant theoretical and methodical issues and perspectives in relation to the overall assignment.

If the Master’s thesis takes the form of a group thesis, students will also develop their collaborative skills.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to

Knowledge:
- demonstrate in-depth knowledge of the thesis topic
- demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the theories and methods relevant to the thesis
- demonstrate knowledge of how the chosen topic, theories and methods relate to current research and/or practice within the field.

Skills:
- prepare a delimited thesis statement with a view to using it for academic analysis
- use academic theories and methods critically and consciously, including choosing and applying relevant primary and secondary literature
- communicate in a coherent, well-argued and comprehensible manner at a high level of academic precision in terms of grammar, spelling and language; using a suitably varied vocabulary, including relevant concepts and terminology; and using academic conventions in terms of layout, notes, references and citations as specified in the Department of English Style Guide
- for product theses, students must also demonstrate a clear connection between the analytical/theoretical/empirical point of departure and the product
- for portfolio theses, students must also produce academic texts which deal with one overall academic issue, as well as a synthesising presentation which presents relevant theoretical and methodical issues and perspectives in relation to the overall assignment
- for group theses, students must also initiate and carry out mono-disciplinary and cross-disciplinary collaboration in an independent manner as well as taking professional responsibility.

Competences:

3.3 Credit and flexibility

Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document the results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements.

Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad.

When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.


3.4 Registration and withdrawal

Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.

Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.

Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students cannot withdraw from the Master’s thesis.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.
Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.

If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Deadlines are stated on the study portal. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

Students can choose between the specified forms of exam for their Master’s thesis.


3.5 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of Master's thesis and other major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to the student's command of the content knowledge. 

In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.6 Regulations for assignments

Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.9 Exemption

An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the board of studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the board of studies forwards the application to the appropriate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and must be submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal.

3.10 Appeals and complaints

Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order.

More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.

Calculation of average grade point
It is possible to get the weighted average grade point calculated and formally verified. The weighted average grade point is an expression of the average grade point calculated in relation to the ECTS weighting of the grade points. The average grade point is calculated to one decimal point based on all graded exams. The average grade is not calculated in relation to the grades of fellow students, and that the calculation of the average mark is not offered until the programme's last exam has been passed.
