1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

The Bachelor's Supplementary Subject in Cultural and Creative Industries (CCI) gives students both the knowledge of and skills for working in an analytical, critically reflective, interdisciplinary and problem-based way within the Cultural and Creative Industries – from socio-cultural and financial, local and international, and contemporary and historical perspectives. Through case- and project-based working methods in collaboration with external partners, knowledge and theoretical-reflective skills are translated into the student’s ability to identify and act in relation to opportunities and challenges in CCI. 

Cultural and Creative Industries include music, gaming, design, fashion, sports, tourism, festivals, theatre, publishing, art, media, film and television. The basic issues of the field include the characteristics of CCI as a sector and its links with other sectors, areas of society, institutions and practice – including the importance of social, cultural, political, financial, legislative and media conditions. The programme also thematises the interplay between cultural production and users, creativity and working conditions. The relevance of the programme arises from the strong links of CCI to the commercial production of culture. 

The teaching alternates between the acquisition of theoretical concepts and methodological tools (e.g. documentation, concept development and project management), case-based analyses as well as group-based development projects involving companies, organisations or institutions with an interest in CCI. The programme also introduces CCI theoretically, analytically and critically in a global perspective, emphasising the field's implications for, for example, culture, the market and the media. The programme includes a field trip to a (European) capital in order to visit, for example, industry festivals, companies and/or related degree programmes or research institutions.


1.2 Learning outcomes

Students acquire the following knowledge, skills and competences during the Bachelor’s Supplementary Subject in Cultural and Creative Industries:

Knowledge:

  • Knowledge of specific industries related to the cultural and creative industries as well as their socio-cultural and financial, local and international, contemporary and historical significance
  • Knowledge of relevant theories and methods related to working with CCI
  • The ability to understand and critically reflect on theories, methods and practice in relation to CCI.

Skills:

  • The ability to identify issues relevant to studies of CCI or specific CCI actors
  • The ability to translate knowledge of CCI as well as relevant theoretical and methodological approaches into problem-driven, case-based analyses
  • The ability to identify relevant opportunities or challenges related to specific CCI actors based on a critical analysis of issues related to CCI
  • The ability to establish contact with local or regional CCI actors as well as design and implement projects related to these actors
  • The ability to convey insights on CCI concisely in written and oral form.

Competences:

  • The competence to adopt a problem-solving approach and to address complex and development-oriented situations in the study of CCI, including reflecting on and justifying selected approaches and strategies
  • The competence to enter into interdisciplinary collaboration on CCI-related issues with an awareness of one’s own professional field
  • The competence to actively contribute to the study of CCI, including the competence to structure one’s own learning.

Critical Cultural and Creative Industry Studies 15 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The course Critical Cultural and Creative Industry Studies will introduce students to CCI as a specific field of productive, economic and cultural practice as well as an area of research and academic study. This will be done in a historical and contemporary perspective, through introductions to relevant theories within sociology, economics, media and cultural studies in tandem with theories from the arts, and with an emphasis on the particular modes of operation of various CCIs regarding the use of media affordances, aesthetic appeals, user/audience relationship configurations, etc. To this end, students will be presented with and involved in case-based discussions from cultural fields such as music, visual art, literature, theatre, film, gaming, and fashion. The course is fundamentally concerned with the characteristics and demarcations of CCI as a field of practice in relation to other cultural and economic sectors, and how this may be subjected to empirically informed critical-theoretical enquiry.  

Through this course students will gain a critical toolbox for understanding what CCIs are, how they have come about, how they function in various ways, and how they are related to other sectors and practices within contemporary society. Furthermore, students will gain an understanding of how to study and research CCI. These learning outcomes will benefit the study of Global Perspectives on Cultural and Creative Industries and Case Studies: Problem-based Research and Development in Cultural and Creative Industries in the following semester.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- identify the different characters, interrelations, and particular modes of operation of various branches of cultural and creative industries
- account for the historical, socio-economic and political background for current regimes of CCI, including their development and societal role
- demonstrate an understanding of the research field related to CCI (key theories and approaches), and reflect on this field on an academic basis and identify academic issues as a result. 

Skills:
- identify, describe and analyse various types of CCI products and agents
- identify, describe and analyse forms of production, communication and usage associated with CCI
- relate specific CCI products and processes to wider societal circumstances.

Competences:
- participate and collaborate in inter-disciplinary collaborations with students from other academic traditions around analytical work on concrete CCI constellations, subfields and products
- independently organise their own academic competence development.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

Teaching will alternate between lectures and workshops. Other activities such as group-based projects and excursions may be included.
Students produce a number of assignments during the course. The assignments can provide the basis for various forms of feedback and further development related to the teaching, but the individual assignments are not assessed on a continuous basis. All or some of these assignments can provide the basis for the student’s exam.

Case studies in Cultural and Creative Industries: Problem-based Research and Development 20 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
The purpose of the course is to provide students with methodological tools and thematic perspectives, which enable them to conduct case-based analyses of topics relating to CCI. Moreover, the course aims to enable students to develop project-based investigations of and suggest solutions to issues pertaining to industry partners.

Through case studies, students will gain knowledge about select businesses, organisations and institutions within the field of CCI as well as legal, technological, political and economic conditions pertaining to cultural production within select industries. Working on case studies will entail identification and motivation of relevant cases connected to CCI-related issues, the development of problem-based analyses based on empirically founded and methodologically reflected accounts, and a balance of critical-analytical and potentially, solution-oriented approaches. Students will be expected to engage industry partners with the aim of developing project-based investigations.

The course will integrate insights gained from Critical Cultural and Creative Industry Studies and Global Perspectives on Cultural and Creative Industry Studies in 'problem-driven' and project-based case studies.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe select businesses, institutions and organisations within the field of CCI
- account for key topics relating to doing CCI case studies (e.g. regarding legal, technological, political and financial conditions, issues of business logics, management, branding, product development, creativity, audiences, entrepreneurship, etc.)
- explain and select methodological tools of relevance to doing CCI case studies
- account for choices in relation to project design and development.

Skills:
- identify cases of relevance to issues pertaining to CCI and conduct empirically founded case-based analyses
- participate in planning and managing projects, which engage with industry partners.

Competences:
- reflect on the projects' relation to industry partners as well as overall perspectives of CCI
- reflect on student’s own academic background in relation to the interdisciplinary field of CCI.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

Teaching will alternate between lectures, workshops, group-based projects, collective supervision and excursions.

Global Perspectives on Cultural and Creative Industries 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
This course introduces theoretical, analytical, and critical-reflexive approaches to cultural and creative industries (CCI) in an international perspective, emphasising the field’s global implications on cultural, commercial, and media-specific transformations. Students will study different manifestations of CCIs from across the world, learn how they are structured and function within particular (trans)national contexts, and trace the production and circulation of cultural artefacts at varying geographic scales. Moreover, students will examine the characteristics and components of different ‘models’ of CCI practices and interrogate topical issues in CCI research, such as structural challenges in the international division of cultural labour, and national and transnational CCI strategies.

In Global Perspectives, students will draw on the theoretical and critical knowledge gained from Critical Cultural and Creative Industries Studies along with methodological skills developed in Case Studies: Problem-based Research and Development in Cultural and Creative Industries and apply these to the analysis of various international case studies, thereby activating a critical comparison of CCI practices and processes at a global level. The course will enrich students’ knowledge of CCIs as an international phenomenon and highlight the ways in which students may position themselves as researchers and (potential) practitioners in different CCI contexts. This course includes an excursion to a (European) metropolis with visits to relevant CCI organisations as well as related academic and research institutions to gain insights on how CCI practice and research are conducted in a different cultural and socio-political setting.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- identify the key structural components and characteristics of specific CCI cases in different market contexts and explain the processes and interconnections between them
- compare and contrast the different approaches to CCI development at national, regional, and global scales.

Skills:
- analyse the production of specific cultural artefacts within a designated CCI context and evaluate the reasons for their circulation and mobility at local, regional, and global levels 
- apply relevant theoretical and methodological approaches in the critical analysis of CCI cases in a global context
- communicate arguments and ideas effectively and persuasively.

Competences:
- select and apply interdisciplinary perspectives and concepts from across the humanities and social sciences to their analyses and justify the intellectual stance for the selected approach.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

Teaching will be based on classroom seminars and fieldtrip(s) that includes individual and group work. Students will be expected to engage with class materials and activities via Brightspace.
Students will be expected to engage with both self-directed and teacher-led learning activities while on the overseas excursion.

3.2.1 Credit and flexibility
Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document his or her results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements. Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.

3.2.2 Registration and withdrawal
Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.
Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.
Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.
Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.
If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Deadlines are stated on the study portal. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

3.2.3 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to his/her command of the content knowledge.
In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.2.4 Definition of standard page
Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.2.5 Using computers for examinations
Most written exams at AU are taken in a digital exams system. More information about digital exams may be found on the study portal. See also Aarhus University’s rules and regulations on the use of computers at exams http://www.au.dk/fileadmin/www.au.dk/Regelsamlingen/2016/REGLER_OM_BRUG_AF_COMPUTER_VED_PROEVER_2016.pdf

3.2.6 Exemption
An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the Board of Studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the Board of Studies forwards the application to the appro-priate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal portal.

3.2.7 Appeals and complaints
Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing and must account for the student’s reasons for lodging the complaint. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order. More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.

3.2.8 Exchange
A degree programme may include study abroad. More information is available on the study portal.
