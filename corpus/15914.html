1. About the degree programme

1.1 The academic direction and primary subject areas of the programme

Data radically impact our lives, our perception of the world and the world itself. Data and digital infrastructures are not only representing reality, but are literally world-changing. Data and the digital are thus not just powerful tools for analysis, but transforms human and earthly life as such. It matters what and how things are datafied and how they are processed and related in and through digital infrastructures. Data are not just technical instruments, but are socially and culturally constructed. To understand and act in a world in which data plays an increasingly important role requires people skilled in understanding data as sociomaterial, complex phenomena. The skills required are interpretation, critical reflection and cultural analysis and they are well established in the humanities and social sciences. In order to act responsibly and ethically in a world of data requires interpretation, imagination, creativity and speculation.

The programme explores what data is, how it is produced, and how the humanities can study the social and political implications of data and datafication. It matters, also on a cultural level, how data is collected, stored, categorized, displayed and archived. The premise of the programme is that theory and practice go hand in hand and that learning and knowledge production is an embodied practice. The programme seeks to enable the students to become engaged in the making of sustainable and ethical futures.

The Bachelor's supplementary subject in Critical Data Studies provides the students with digital tools and techniques for data production and analysis and with theory and methods for understanding and reflecting on the consequences of data. The programme engages micro- and macro level perspectives by including the student’s own experiences with data and the larger societal consequences of data. Project and collaborative work, critical reflections and discussion, designing and imagining are central elements in the programme.

The overall aims of the supplementary subject are to 1) enable students to critically reflect on data and datafication, 2) provide practical experience and skills in data production and analysis by introducing various digital methods, such as network analysis, data visualization, data analytics etc, and 3) enable students to explore and critically reflect on the impact of data and datafication on everyday life and society through hands-on practices, curation, speculation, posthumanist and sociomaterial theory, design and ethnography.


1.2 Learning outcomes

Students acquire the following knowledge, skills and competences during the Bachelor’s supplementary subject in Critical Data Studies:

Knowledge:

  • Theoretical and conceptual knowledge about data and datafication
  • Knowledge about curation, speculation, posthumanist and sociomaterial theory, critical thinking and ethical concerns
  • Knowledge about tools and methods for data collection, production and analysis
  • Knowledge about the role of data and datafication in society and individual everyday life
  • Ablility to understand and reflect on the potentials and challenges of data and datafication.

Skills:

  • Ability to conduct and apply digital methods and tools for production and analysis of data on a basic level
  • Ability to speculate and design with data
  • Ability to assess, justify and apply theories and concepts for critical analysis and reflection on data and datafication
  • Ability to critically reflect on methods and the selection of methods
  • Ability to communicate academic issues and concerns in relation to data and datafication to specialists and non-specialists or partners and users.

Competences:

  • Competence to independently plan and execute specific types of data collection and production
  • Competence to independently plan and execute academic and critical analysis of data and datafication
  • Competence to enter into academic and cross-disciplinary collaboration in a professional and independent manner in relation to data and datafication
  • Competence to identify own learning needs and structure own learning processes in relation to data and datafication and critical reflection on data and datafication.

Curating Data 15 ECTS Semester 1 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:
Purpose:
The course introduces the practice of curating, normally associated with caring for collections of objects and art in museums and galleries, as a way to investigate, analyse, and reflect on data and digital culture.
Curating data means generating, selecting, organising, managing and displaying data and emphasise that this is a sociomaterial practice comprised of human, technical and material actors.
The course introduces the students to various theories, tools and methods for gathering, sorting, categorising and analysing data. The course provides the students with the sense that these practices entail construction, care, ethics and decision-making as central aspects to working with data. For example, when querying data collections, analysing social media and understanding and visualising relations in network data. The course draws on fields such as Digital Methods, Data Analysis and Aesthetic Programming and forms of curating.
The students will curate their own data collections and investigate how data is collected, stored, categorized, displayed and archived and what it means to curate data. Students will be required to exhibit and present their data collections as part of the course work.
The course provides the foundation for the program and provides the students with essential tools and methods as well as the overall idea of the program. The work and products made in the course will also be revisited and further developed later on in the program.

Academic objectives
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe and discuss curation of data
- explain theories, tools and methods of data collection, production and analysis
- reflect on posthuman theories and sociomaterial practices. 

Skills:
- collect, describe, visualize and analyse different types of data
- critically reflect on methods and the selection of methods
- organise and present data collections
- communicate about the processes and practices of collecting and producing data.

Competences:
- independently plan and execute specific types of data collection and production.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:
The course uses a combination of large classes (e.g. lectures) and student work, and may include teaching in smaller groups and/or exercises.
Students produce a number of assignments during the course. The assignments can provide the basis for various forms of feedback (e.g. peer feedback) and further development related to the teaching, but the individual assignments are not assessed on a continuous basis. All or some of these assignments can provide the basis for the student’s exam.

Datafication of Society 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose:
This course addresses the concept of data, focusing on the increasing use of data and datafication in contemporary society where human experience, natural processes and phenomena etc. are turned into discrete data points, numeric values, or specific categories of meaning. The course will explore the history and theories of data and datafication from a sociological perspective and with an ethical concern focusing on topics such as algorithms, big data, machine learning and artificial intelligence. The main question of the course is: “What are the social, societal and political implications of data and datafication?”
The course provides the students with methods and concepts that enable them to investigate, analyse and critically reflect on data as historically and politically situated with consequences for society and how we act in the world.
The course provides the students with an overview of the historical background and the significance of data and datafication in contemporary society. The course provides the students with an overall orientation competence in relation to sociological and historical analyses of data and datafication.

Academic objectives:
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- account for theories and concepts of data and datafication
- account for history and genealogy of data and datafication
- reflect on data as historically and politically situated phenomena.

Skills:
- investigate and analyse data and datafication from a historical and sociological perspective
- communicate about the role of data historically and in relation to contemporary society
- critically reflect on the role of data historically and in relation to contemporary society.

Competences:
- take into account the historical background and the significance of data in contemporary society in relation to specific cases and problems.

Data Subjects 10 ECTS Semester 2 7-point grading scale
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:

Purpose
The course focuses on the individual as a user and producer of data, but also as shaped by data. The course focuses on the various practices and technologies by which we as individual persons produce data and how data shapes our understanding of ourselves and our subjectivity and affects how we live.
The purpose of the course is to enable the students to investigate, analyse and reflect on the relation between data and the formation of subjectivity and identity. By focusing on the use practices of devices and technologies such as social media, mobile devices, apps, geolocation, sensors, state governed and private information systems etc., the students will learn to investigate and analyse these practices and their consequences for the subjectivity of the user. The students will explore phenomena and technologies such as filter bubbles, echo chambers, recommender systems, quantified self, tracking, data doubles, surveillance and dataveillance.
The course provides the students with the competences for independent and cross-disciplinary research on the relation of data and the subject, which is relevant in order to assess and develop technologies as well as for the development of knowledge about the relation of data and the subject. The course builds on the three other courses in the programme and is a concluding part of the programme.

Academic objectives
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe and discuss theories, concepts and methods for investigating the role of data in relation to the formation of the subject
- critically reflect on the relation between data and subjectivity.

Skills:
- analyse and discuss the relation between data and subjectivity in general and in specific cases
- communicate about the relation between data and subjectivity in general and in specific cases.

Competences:
- independently plan and execute studies of data and subjectivity in specific cases.

Forms of instruction:

  • Lecture
  • Classroom instruction

Comments on form of instruction:

The course uses a combination of large classes (e.g. lectures) and student work, and may include teaching in smaller groups and/or exercises. The course may also include assignments, peer feedback etc.

Language of instruction:

Alternative Data Futures 10 ECTS Semester 2 Passed /failed
Exam details See courses

Description of qualifications:
Purpose:
The course focuses on imagining alternative data futures. The aim of the course is to enable students to imagine and think about alternatives to existing data practices and technologies that takes into account contemporary discussions about ethics and the impact of data.
This course introduces students to methods of future thinking and processes of imagining futures. The course will enable students to (1) explore how speculative methods can be tools for both understanding the present and analysing different futures; (2) use ethical concerns as starting points for design and storytelling.
During the course students will revisit the projects developed in the Curating Data course for developing their speculative designs. The two courses, Datafication of Society and Data Subjects, will inform the design work. The course is relevant for students wishing to work with developing new technologies and new practices for a broad set of reasons such as for artistic and cultural reasons, for societal and democratic reasons and for commercial and business. 

Academic objectives 
In the evaluation of the student’s performance, emphasis is placed on the extent to which the student is able to:

Knowledge:
- describe how ethical frameworks can be applied to the critical exploration of data
- describe and discuss how speculative methods can be applied to the critical exploration of data.

Skills:
- apply speculative methods in order to do design and storytelling with data
- present and communicate alternative data futures
- critically reflect on data by applying speculative thinking.

Competences:
- independently plan and execute speculative design and critical fabulations for imagining and designing alternative data futures

3.2 Curriculum for The Supplementary Subject

3.2.1 Credit and flexibility
Students may apply for advance approval of degree programme elements which are subsequently completed at another university or institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. An approved application for advance approval of transfer credit obligates the student to document his or her results – both passed and failed degree programme elements, as soon as these marks are available. The board of studies may – if necessary – procure the necessary documentation directly. The student will then receive transfer credit for the passed degree programme elements. Applications for advance approval of transfer credit and submission of documentation for completed degree programme elements with a view to registration of transfer credit must take place in accordance with the rules published on the study portal. In addition, the board of studies may approve applications for transfer credit for degree programme elements which have been completed at an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad. When the board of studies has approved the credit transfer of a passed course/course element from an institution of higher education in Denmark or abroad, credit must be transferred as ‘Passed’. If the course element in question is assessed in accordance with the 7-point grading scale at both institutions, the assessment is transferred with the grade given, cf. the Degree Programme Order and the Examination Order.

3.2.2 Registration and withdrawal
Registering for compulsory courses, elective courses and exams
Registering for compulsory courses and elective courses:
Students will be registered automatically for teaching in compulsory courses in the order of semesters prescribed in the academic regulations. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for courses and elective courses. Students must register for and (when necessary) prioritise elective courses by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal. After this deadline, elective courses become a compulsory part of the student’s degree programme and cannot be changed.
Students who stick to the prescribed curriculum will be given priority when places are assigned for compulsory courses. After the deadline for registration, places can only be assigned to students if there is room in the classroom, and only until the start of the semester.

Selection criteria for elective courses
There may be a limit to the number of students admitted to elective courses, and the creation of such courses is contingent on the number of students who register for them. The criteria for allocating places on elective courses which only admit a limited number of students are stated on the study portal. (studerende.au.dk/arts).

Registering for and withdrawing from ordinary exams and re-examinations:
Students will be registered automatically for ordinary exams in compulsory courses. Students are responsible for ensuring that they are always registered correctly for exams. Students are responsible for registering for re-examinations by the set deadline, which can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan. Students can only register for re-examinations if they have already tried to pass the ordinary exam before the re-examination.
Students can withdraw from exams using the self-service system at mitstudie.au.dk, following the procedure described on the study portal. Students cannot withdraw from exams after the deadline, and the failure to take part in an exam for which they are registered will count as a failed attempt to pass it. The deadlines for registration and withdrawal can be found on the study portal and in the exam plan.

Forms of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations
The form of exam for ordinary exams and re-examinations is stated in the academic regulations. The ordinary form of exam is offered immediately after courses have been taught.
Students taking exams in the re-examination period will be examined using the form of re-examination that has been specified.
If the ordinary exam and the re-examination both use the same form of exam, students are entitled to apply for a re-examination in the ordinary exam period immediately after the course teaching has been completed. Deadlines are stated on the study portal. Students should not expect any requests submitted after the deadline to be granted.

3.2.3 Spelling and fluency

In the assessment of major written assignments, emphasis must be placed on the student’s spelling and fluency in addition to his/her command of the content knowledge.
In the assessment of all written exams, emphasis will be placed on the extent to which the student is able to present an academic topic and structure an academic assignment including complying with formal academic requirements (references, quotations, etc.). In the assessment of all oral exams, emphasis is placed on the student's ability to present academic information, structure a presentation and take part in constructive academic dialogue.

3.2.4 Definition of standard page
Conditions regarding the extent of written submissions are stated in the description of each degree programme component. A normal page for written submissions is 2400 characters (with spaces). To calculate normal pages, both text and notes are included, but not the front page, table of contents and bibliography. Written submissions that do not comply with these conditions cannot be accepted for assessment.

3.2.5 Using computers for examinations
Most written exams at AU are taken in a digital exams system. More information about digital exams may be found on the study portal. See also Aarhus University’s rules and regulations on the use of computers at exams http://www.au.dk/fileadmin/www.au.dk/Regelsamlingen/2016/REGLER_OM_BRUG_AF_COMPUTER_VED_PROEVER_2016.pdf

3.2.6 Exemption
An exemption is a deviation from that or those regulations that normally apply for the area in question. Exemption can be granted on the basis of an application sent to the authority that has the power to grant such exemption. An application for exemption must be submitted to the Board of Studies. If another authority has the power to grant exemption, the Board of Studies forwards the application to the appro-priate authority (e.g. the dean, rector or ministry). An application for exemption must be made in writing, stating reasons, and submitted as soon as possible. For the application to be processed immediately, it must include a precise account of the regulation from which exemption is sought, and what such exemption is intended to achieve (e.g. permission to use special aids, extension of examination time, postponement of time limits). Documentation for the unusual conditions that justify exemption must be enclosed with the application. Importance will not normally be attached to such conditions if they are not documented. More information about exemptions may be found on the study portal portal.

3.2.7 Appeals and complaints
Complaints about exams should be submitted to the university. For the application to be processed, it must be made in writing and must account for the student’s reasons for lodging the complaint. The complaint must state both the reason for the complaint and what the complainant expects to achieve. Complaints about exams must be submitted within fourteen days after the deadline for the publication of exam results, cf. the Examination Order. More information about exam complaints is available on the study portal.

3.2.8 Exchange
A degree programme may include study abroad. More information is available on the study portal.
